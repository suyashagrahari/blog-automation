You asked for very high volume even at very high KD. Tier 2 *is* the very-high-volume
list, and it is all cut — **not because it is too hard, but because it is the wrong
product.** A page ranking #1 for `gift for girlfriend` sends visitors who want a watch
delivered by Tuesday. That traffic does not convert on a personalised-link product, and
`birthday gift for girlfriend under 500` is worse still: Google serves that query
**entirely in US dollars** (Neiman Marcus, David Yurman, Architectural Digest), so the
Indian ₹500 intent it looks like it has is not the intent being served.

The cut list is printed in full so the trade is visible rather than hidden.

## Gate 5 did the work this run, not Gate 4

Most runs are decided by difficulty. This one is decided by **conversion fit**, which is
why the inventory carries a `conversion_fit` (1–5) column:

- **fit 5** — the searcher wants exactly what SubhSandesh makes (`virtual gift link free`)
- **fit 3–4** — adjacent; a page can serve them with a genuine angle
- **fit 1–2** — they want a physical object or a gift card. Cut regardless of volume.

`priority_score = winnability × conversion_fit × volume_band`, so a 10,000+/mo keyword
at fit 2 scores below a 650/mo keyword at fit 5. That is the intended behaviour.

## The honest read

**1. Your highest-volume ask is the one I cut, and I want to be direct about why.**
`gift for girlfriend` and its siblings are 10,000+/mo and they are genuinely winnable-ish
on difficulty — 4 weak results in 10. They are cut on **conversion fit**. If your goal is
raw sessions, they work. If your goal is people who create a page, they do not, and
building 25 physical-gift listicles would be the most expensive way to learn that. If you
want the traffic anyway, say so and I will re-bucket them — that is a strategy call, not
a data one, and you may have reasons I cannot see from the SERPs.

**2. The good news is bigger than the bad news.** Your category SERPs are held by
**seven small startups**, not by retail giants: gifft.me, qlovy.com, surprises.gift,
ourgiftverse.com, digiflower.net, giftsqr.com, messagear.com. SubhSandesh has ~900 live
pages and real topical depth. These SERPs are winnable in a way that almost no
10,000+/mo commercial SERP ever is.

**3. `digiflower.net` deserves a direct look.** It ranks for `virtual gifts for
girlfriend` and `virtual gifts for long distance boyfriend`, and it competes head-on with
your existing `digital-bouquet` / `digital-flower-bouquet` pages. It is the single most
useful competitor to study on this axis.

**4. Tier 3 is the under-priced tier.** `surprise gift for girlfriend website` (9
prefix-hits), `surprise gift for girlfriend link` (9), `online gift for girlfriend link`
(11) — real people typing your product's mechanic into Google, with almost nobody
targeting the phrasing. Individually small; collectively the cheapest qualified traffic
in this sheet.

**5. Realistic ceiling.** The 73 survivors sum to roughly **4,500–5,500 clicks/mo** at
position 3 across the board, which will not happen. A realistic 6-month read on the
8-page publish order is **800–1,400 clicks/mo** — but at a far higher conversion rate
than the Tier 2 traffic would have been, because every one of them is fit 4–5.

**6. Was the seed right?** Yes — more so than "miss you". This axis maps directly onto
what SubhSandesh sells, and the competitive set is beatable. The scope error would be
chasing the retail vocabulary instead of the product vocabulary.
