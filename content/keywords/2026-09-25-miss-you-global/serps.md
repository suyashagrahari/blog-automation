# Phase 4 + 5 — SERP log · miss-you global run

**Locale disclosure, read this first.** Every SERP below was served from a **US IP**
by the WebSearch tool, which is US-only. A Spanish-language query served from the US
is not the SERP a searcher in Madrid sees; an Indian-language query served from the US
is not what Mumbai sees. What the tool *does* show reliably is which **pages and page
types** compete for the phrase, and that is what the weakness counts below are built
from. Positions are not recorded, because US-served positions for a Spain, Brazil or
India query would be misleading precision. Where a per-market position matters before
publishing, open `google.com/search?q=…&gl=<cc>&hl=<lang>` in a browser and check.

**13 queries were measured.** 137 of the 150 keywords inherit a weakness count from
the measured SERP of their own cluster and market and are labelled `SERP-ARCHETYPE`
in the inventory, never `SERP-MEASURED`. That is an inference, not an observation.

## PH-01 · `miss kita in bisaya waray ilocano meaning` — US-served, Filipino results
mymemory.translated.net · tiktok.com ×2 · forum.wordreference.com ·
en.wikipedia.org ×3 (Waray language, Bisayan languages, Manang Biday — all off-intent) ·
wordhippo.com · medium.com
**Weak: 8.** No publisher has built a real page here.
Finding that came out of the SERP itself: **there is no native Tagalog, Bisaya or
Ilocano verb for "to miss"** — the English loanword is used across all of them, which
is why "miss kita" is the phrase people actually type. Verify with a Philippine
dictionary before publishing; this is the kind of claim the blog skill will demand a
source for.
