## Phase 2 — gap analysis

Because no informational article ranks, the usual table-stakes/gap comparison against
five text competitors cannot be run. What can be read off the SERP is the shape of the
demand:

- **Table stakes (inferred from the surfaces, not from any page's body):** named
  decoration ideas, a doorway/entrance idea, a balloon idea, a photo-wall idea, and
  visible imagery. A text post that names no concrete objects will look thin next to a
  530-pin board.
- **The gap:** every surface in this SERP assumes the reader is browsing. Pinterest
  assumes time to scroll and pick; the three services assume the reader will pay someone
  to do it, today. **Nobody sorts decoration by the hours the reader has left** — and
  `7eventzz`'s own promise ("from ₹1399 | Same-Day Setup") is direct evidence that the
  same-day segment is large enough to build a business on. Our 182-page lead-time
  distribution measures that segment from the inside: half are on the day, 62.6% within
  a day, and 12.6% are already late.
- **Stale data:** not assessable — image boards and service pages cite nothing.
- **Unanswered questions:** what is genuinely finishable in one hour; what to do when the
  birthday has already passed; who should physically do the decorating; why buying the
  setup is so common when the materials are cheap.
- **Fan-out sub-queries** (each became an H2, an H3 or an FAQ): *simple birthday
  decoration at home with things I already have* · *last minute birthday decoration
  ideas* · *how long does birthday decoration take* · *birthday decoration ideas at home
  without balloons* · *is it cheaper to do it yourself* · *decoration ideas for a
  surprise at midnight* · *what to do if you missed the birthday* · *how to decorate for
  a birthday in a small room* · *should I book a decorator*.

**Angle:** wins by being the only page that sorts birthday decoration at home by the
hours the reader has left, using a first-party lead-time distribution (median 0 days,
50.0% on the birthday itself, 12.6% after it) that measures the exact same-day audience
the three ranking decoration services monetise.

**Cannibalisation.** No live decoration post. The nearest sibling in this batch,
`birthday-surprise-ideas-for-wife-at-home`, also organises by time remaining, so this
post deliberately keeps to the physical-decoration axis — doorway, wall, light, who
holds the tape — and uses the *lead-time* measurement rather than that post's 0.9-hour
build time as its spine. The two share no source URL and no source domain.

### 1. Clarke, A. J. (2007) — required lane-P scholarly source

- `https://www.scielo.br/j/ha/a/BzbtSDWJq5swQGvKffsfyQc/?lang=en`
- *Consuming children and making mothers: birthday parties, gifts and the pursuit of
  sameness*, Horizontes Antropológicos 13(28), Dec 2007. Open access, CC BY-NC.
- **Full text read** (English version on the SciELO landing page).
- Verified from the paper: ethnography of **76 households** on one London street; the
  field site was established in 1994/5 and this article draws on 2004/5 research;
  techniques were object biographies, taped interviews and participant observation.
- Verified claim used: the growth of children's party provision runs alongside a popular
  discourse lamenting the loss of "authentic" kinship-based parties and **home-made
  provisioning**, but the mothers studied in fact **avidly embrace market goods and
  services**, as a way of producing sameness and avoiding the risk of visibly
  exceptional mothering.
- Subject test: children's birthday parties in the home, home-made versus bought — the
  centre of lane P.
- **Swap test: failed, and recorded as an audit failure.** After drafting, the in-batch
  measurement showed the sibling post `belated-birthday-wishes` already cites **this exact
  URL**, for the same "market goods maintain a culture of sameness" finding. The URL is
  therefore in 2 posts (at the cap, not over it) and `scielo.br` in 2 posts. The use here
  is different in substance — the buy-versus-make decision, with Clarke and the
  76-household ethnography named in the body, where the sibling names neither — but the
  test asks whether a source *could* sit unchanged in another keyword's post, and this one
  provably has. It was kept rather than dropped because it is the only readable
  open-access study of home-made versus bought birthday provisioning that exists (see
  "What was searched and not found"), and removing it would leave the post with no lane-P
  scholarly source at all.
- Licence note: CC BY-NC forbids commercial reuse, so nothing is reproduced — the finding
  is stated in our own words and linked.

### 2. Marsh, L. E., Kanngiesser, P. & Hood, B. (2018)

- `https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/a6712575-1710-448c-93df-046bfd540da8`
- *When and how does labour lead to love? The ontogeny and mechanisms of the IKEA effect*,
  Cognition 170, 245–253. University of Bristol repository record; the deposited
  full-text PDF is the final published version under CC BY.
- **Abstract only.** The repository's PDF returned HTTP 403 from this environment and the
  publisher copy is behind Cloudflare, so only what the record's abstract states is
  cited. Recorded in the audit.
- Verified claims used: people treat their own constructions as more valuable than
  identical items built by someone else; the bias **emerges at age 5**; in 5-to-6-year-olds
  it is **not moderated by the amount of effort** invested; ownership alone does not
  explain it.
- Subject test: who makes the thing, and what making it does to how it is valued — the
  home-made half of lane P. Swap test: the post uses the developmental finding (age 5,
  effort not moderating) to argue about **who should physically do the decorating at
  home**, which no other keyword in the batch asks.

### 3. Fathima Zehba, M. P., Rajendran, L. P. & Firoz C., M. (2025)

- `https://www.cogitatiopress.com/urbanplanning/article/view/9272`
- *"Where Is the Time?": Time Poverty and Women's Urban Mobility Narratives in Kochi,
  India*, Urban Planning 10, published **23 April 2025**. Open access, CC BY.
- **Abstract read on the landing page; the published PDF was downloaded (1.07 MB) but its
  body text is not machine-readable in this environment** (embedded font encoding), so
  only the abstract and the figure labels are cited. Recorded in the audit.
- Verified claims used: in-depth interviews conducted **December 2022 – January 2023**
  with low-income women workers in Kochi; entrenched gender roles concentrate unpaid
  domestic and caregiving work on women, producing severe time poverty. From the paper's
  own framework figure: a day divides into necessary time (sleeping, eating), **committed
  time (unpaid work, e.g. house chores, childcare)**, contracted time (paid work) and
  travel time, with free time the residual.
- Subject test: household labour and the time it leaves — the constraint the whole post
  is organised around, in an Indian city. Swap test: no other keyword in the batch is
  about household time.

## Phase 6 — audit: 47 passed, 3 failed

The three failures are the internal-links/conversion item below, the **swap test** (the
duplicated Clarke URL, explained in Phase 3), and **"Slug short, hyphenated, lowercase, no
stop words"** — the assigned slug keeps the stop word "at", the same structural failure
`birthday-surprise-ideas-for-wife-at-home` recorded in wave 2. All 50 item strings were
generated by parsing `references/publish-checklist.md` directly, including the `**bold**`
markers and the wrapped source-cap item's trailing exception clause, so none is a
paraphrase or a truncated prefix; the build script aborts if a `failed[].item` is not
byte-identical to a parsed item.

**The intent mismatch is recorded as an audit failure**, against this item, copied
verbatim from `references/publish-checklist.md`:

> 2–4 internal links with descriptive anchor text, placed after value is established

That is the checklist's only conversion item — the internal template links are the entire
conversion path — and the mismatch is a failure of fit, not of placement. The links are
real, descriptive and correctly positioned; what fails is that the searcher's satisfied
intent in this SERP is an image board or a same-day setup crew, and a shareable digital
page is neither. Nothing in the drafting closes it, which is why it stays open rather
than being argued away. The mapping is stated here so a reviewer can check it, following
the precedent in `BRIEF-WAVE2.md` correction 3.

The post is honest about the second half of this too: the SERP is weak **and** the format
may be wrong. Both are in `honestAssessment`.
