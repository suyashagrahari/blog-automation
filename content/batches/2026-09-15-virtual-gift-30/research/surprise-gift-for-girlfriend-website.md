## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** — present on all five, so the post must cover them:

1. A definition of what a "surprise website" actually is.
2. What you put on the page (photos, message, theme, an interaction).
3. That it is shared as a link, usually over WhatsApp or Instagram.
4. That no coding is needed and it takes minutes, not days.
5. An FAQ answering "is it free" and "do I need an account".

**The gap** — not one of the five covers **what happens to the link after you send
it**. Every page ends at "share the link". None answers:

- Who else can open it, and what an unlisted URL actually guarantees (nothing).
- Whether the page can show up in Google.
- Whether you can password-protect it, and whether anyone actually does.
- How long building one really takes, as opposed to the marketing "2 minutes".
- When a link is the wrong gift.

That absence is the post's entire reason to exist: **the link, not the page, is the
product.** It is also where the first-party data is strongest — the 40.6%
password-protection rate and the 6.2-hour median edit gap directly contradict the
"private by default, ready in 2 minutes" framing every competitor uses.

**Stale or absent data.** There is no stale data to supersede because there is
almost no data at all. The only numbers on any of the five pages are marketing
claims ("+50,000 couples", "2 minutes", "3 steps"), none sourced. Any measured
number is a differentiator here.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped by the competitor set:** "is it private?"
(asserted, never explained), "does it expire?", "can I schedule it?".
