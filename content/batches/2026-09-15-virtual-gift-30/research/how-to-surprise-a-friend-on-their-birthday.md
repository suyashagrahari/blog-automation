## Phase 2 — Gap analysis and fan-out

**The gap:** proportionality. Every ranking page answers "what can I do"; none answers
"how much should I do, given how close we actually are". The ordinary friend is
unserved, and so is the honest conclusion that for many of these friendships the right
answer is offline and small.

## Phase 3 — Sources

Route used: Crossref bibliographic search → Unpaywall for OA locations → landing page.
OpenAlex worked but rate-limited under load (33–40s backoff) and was used sparingly.
**PDFs do not parse in this environment, so every source below is an abstract-and-
metadata read.**

Search terms tried: `gift giving relational distance tie strength`, `friendship tie
strength closeness acquaintance`, `birthday ritual significance celebration`,
`reciprocity norms acquaintance social exchange`, `gift giver recipient effort
appreciation mismatch`, `weak ties wellbeing casual friends`, `acts of kindness weak
ties recipients undervalue`, `friendship reciprocity perceived mutual asymmetry
network`, `indebtedness gratitude gift recipient burden`, `birthday celebration ritual
adults social relationships meaning`.

**A real negative finding worth recording:** `birthday ritual significance celebration`
returns almost nothing usable — the Crossref hits are festschrift notices and
century-old obituary-adjacent items (Lister 1907, Einstein 1929). The adult birthday as
a social ritual is essentially unstudied as such; the studied phenomenon underneath it is
gift-giving asymmetry and tie strength, which is where the sources below come from.
`Naito & Sakata 2010` (gratitude/indebtedness on receiving a friend's favour,
*Psychologia*) looked ideal but J-STAGE returned HTTP 500 and Crossref carries no
abstract, so it was **dropped rather than cited unverified**.

## Templates

`/searched` is described by four siblings already, so it is framed here on a dimension
none of them uses: **it is the only format on the site where the recipient does the work
of the joke.** Nothing is declared at them; they type their own name and pull each line
out themselves, which is precisely what makes it safe at a distance where a declaration
would not be. The accompanying instruction is new too — fill the autocomplete with what
*everyone* already knows, because private material is what makes the format curdle for a
non-close friend.

`/flags` is used here for its **direction**: the mock dating profile is of *your* worst
habits, so the sender is the subject of the joke. That removes the "am I close enough to
tease them" problem, which is the wave-5 friend register problem in miniature. Both
siblings that link `/flags` use it romantically (crush, boyfriend).

`/birthday-gf` is the sincere contrast case, named as partner-designed (510 pages, 9.8%)
and recommended only if stripping the romantic lines leaves something that still stands.

No swap was needed; all three assigned templates fit.
