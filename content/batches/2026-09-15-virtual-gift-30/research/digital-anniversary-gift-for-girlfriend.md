### The five used

1. **Evans, N. D., Juhl, J., Hepper, E. G., Wildschut, T., Sedikides, C., &
   Fetterman, A. K. (2022). Romantic nostalgia as a resource for healthy
   relationships.** *Journal of Social and Personal Relationships*, 39(7),
   2181–2206. Open-access accepted manuscript at
   <https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/454349/>.
   Verified from the repository record: *"We examined romantic nostalgia in one
   correlational study, two experiments, and one daily diary study (N = 638).
   Romantic nostalgia was positively associated with greater relationship
   commitment, satisfaction, and closeness."* Published June 2022.
   **Subject test: pass** — it is about nostalgia *for a partner*, which is the
   emotional mechanism an anniversary page runs on. **Swap test: pass** — it would
   be wrong in the apology, best-friend, birthday or long-distance siblings.
   **Peer-reviewed and open-access: yes.**

2. **Petrelli, D., & Whittaker, S. (2010). Family memories in the home:
   contrasting physical and digital mementos.** *Personal and Ubiquitous
   Computing*, 14(2), p. 153. Author accepted manuscript at
   <https://shura.shu.ac.uk/2907/>.
   Verified from the abstract: *"digital mementos were initially perceived as less
   valuable… somewhat limited in function and expression, largely involving
   representational photos and videos, and infrequently accessed."*
   **Subject test: pass.** Used as the post's *downside* section — it is the
   strongest available argument against the thing the post recommends, which is
   why it is in the body rather than buried. **Swap test: pass.**

3. **Campbell, K., & Ponzetti, J. J. (2007). The moderating effects of rituals on
   commitment in premarital involvements.** *Sexual and Relationship Therapy*,
   22(4). Repository record at
   <https://scholarworks.lib.csusb.edu/psychology-publications/1/>.
   Verified from the abstract: N = 100 university students *"in a couple
   relationship but not married"*; *"rituals were significant predictors of
   commitment; however, no unique variance was accounted for once investment model
   variables were taken into consideration."* The caveat is reported in the body,
   not dropped.
   **Subject test: pass** — premarital couples specifically, which is exactly the
   "girlfriend, not wife" reader. **Swap test: pass.**
   *Limit:* the deposited full text was not opened; only the repository record.

4. **Majzoobi, M. R., & Forstmeier, S. (2022). The relationship between the
   reminiscence of relationship-defining memories and marital outcomes: a
   systematic review and meta-analysis.** *Journal of Family Theory & Review*,
   14(1), 7–27. **Abstract only** — the Wiley full text returned 403. The publisher
   abstract was read via the Ukrainian Open Citation Index record at
   <https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/en/works/9GJOLRPl/>: 16 studies reviewed, 14
   meta-analysed, *"a relatively moderate and high correlation of RDMs with marital
   outcomes (r = .334) and marital satisfaction (r = .445)"*.
   The abstract-only status is disclosed **in the body prose**, not only here.

5. **O'Donoghue, J. (19 August 2026). What are you going to do with all the photos
   on your phone?** RTÉ Brainstorm, University College Cork.
   <https://www.rte.ie/brainstorm/2026/0819/1588219-digital-photography-smartphone-photos-curation-selection-collections-memories/>
   Verified in the fetched page: *"A roll of film might contain 24 or 36
   exposures"*; the shift from *"take, select and preserve"* to *"take, preserve
   everything and perhaps select"*.
   Not peer-reviewed — an academic-authored analysis piece for a public
   broadcaster, used for the photo-curation framing only.
