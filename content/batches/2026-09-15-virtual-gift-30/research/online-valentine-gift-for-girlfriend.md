## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** — what "online Valentine gift for girlfriend" pages must cover:
a definition of the format, a set of concrete options, a sense of cost/time, and
a "will she like it" reassurance.

**The gap.** Nobody in this SERP treats the question as an *effort-visibility*
problem, and nobody has any measurement of effort at all. The entire category
argues from taste ("romantic", "cute") rather than from behaviour.

**Stale/absent data.** Competing pages carry no dated statistics of any kind.

**Unanswered questions.** How long does building one of these actually take?
Does anyone finish them? Is a link private? Is Valentine's Day itself a risky
occasion to make a big gesture on?

**Angle (recorded as `batchMeta.angle`).** Wins by being the only post that treats
a Valentine's gift for a girlfriend as a visible-effort problem, pairing
peer-reviewed evidence that Valentine's buying is last-day and flowers-and-
chocolate by default with SubhSandesh's own 6.2-hour median edit window and 40.6%
password rate.

**Differentiation from the boyfriend sibling** (`virtual-valentine-gift-for-boyfriend`):
this post is built on the gendered finding in Close & Zinkhan (2006) — men in that
study default to "status quo" roses/chocolate/perfume and "listen to the shelves",
while women describe wanting gifts that are meaningful, thoughtful or unique. The
girlfriend-direction post is therefore specifically about *beating the default
bouquet*, a problem the boyfriend-direction post does not have. It is also not a
Valentine's-week guide: rose day, propose day, teddy day and hug day are separate
sibling posts and are not mentioned.
