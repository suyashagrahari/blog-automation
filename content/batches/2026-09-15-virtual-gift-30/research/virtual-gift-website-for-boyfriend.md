## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all five cover): what a virtual gift page is; that it is shareable
by link; that there are templates; that a free tier exists; photos + message + music.

**The gap.** Every ranking page assumes you have already chosen *their* service and
then sells you a template. Nobody answers the question the query actually contains —
**which website, and how do you tell a good one from a bad one before you upload
twenty photos.** No page mentions signup walls for the recipient, editability after
sending, password removal, pricing disclosure timing, or whether the link survives
the year.

**Stale / absent data.** There is no stale data to supersede, because there is no
data at all. giftsqr's "ready in 3 minutes" is the only figure on any of the five
and it is unsourced.

**Unanswered questions** the pages raise and drop: what happens to uploaded photos;
whether the recipient needs an account; what the free tier withholds; whether the
page expires.

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that treats "which virtual gift website" as the
real question — a five-check test covering signup, editability, password, price and
link survival — grounded in SubhSandesh's 40.6% password rate, 94.1% publish rate,
6.2-hour median edit window and a 1.29-pages-per-creator figure showing most people
choose a service exactly once.

**Differentiation from siblings.** `surprise-gift-for-girlfriend-website` is about the
link as the product (how to send and keep private) written man→girlfriend;
`online-gift-for-boyfriend` is a ranked idea list answering "will he find it cringe";
`virtual-gift-for-long-distance-boyfriend` is distance-led; `virtual-gift-for-boyfriend-birthday`
is occasion-led. This post is **service-selection-led**, written woman→boyfriend, and
shares no H2 with any of them.
