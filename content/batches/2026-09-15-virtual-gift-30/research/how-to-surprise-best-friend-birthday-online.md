## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (must be present or the post looks incomplete): what counts as an
online surprise; a concrete list of things to do; the midnight-vs-morning question;
something about making it personal; a note on what it costs in effort.

**The gap.** Nobody treats this as a **coordination problem**. Every page assumes one
person acting alone. The actual query — *surprise best friend on her birthday online* —
is almost always a group operation: a group chat that has to act together, three or four
friends who each owe one thing, a reveal at a fixed hour, and people on different apps
in different cities. No page in the ten names an owner, a runway, a chase step, or what
to do when a contribution never arrives.

**Stale data.** None of the analysed pages cites any dated statistic at all, so there is
nothing to supersede — there is simply no evidence on these pages.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** how far ahead to start; how many people to
involve; what to do when one of them leaks it; what happens if the call drops.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:**
1. what an online birthday surprise has to solve → Block 2 definition
2. what to do on which day → the five-day timeline (H3s)
3. why friends do not send the thing they promised → group-size / social loafing
4. which format survives contributions arriving late → templates
5. which reveal format costs least coordination → comparison table
6. what breaks on the video call → social presence / turn-taking
7. when not to do this at all → objection block
