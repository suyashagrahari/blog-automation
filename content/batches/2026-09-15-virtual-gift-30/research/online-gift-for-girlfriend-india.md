## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present across the competitive set, so the post must cover them):
what the thing is; a list of options; a price signal; how fast it arrives; how you send it.

**The gap — four things none of them do:**

1. **Nobody prices the alternative.** Every page says "instant" or "same day" and none states what the
   physical option actually costs. India Post publishes an exact retail tariff (₹82 metro-to-metro,
   ₹90 to another state, up to 500 g) and exact service standards (1–3 days, 4–5 days). Putting a real
   rupee number against a real day-count is the single most citable thing on the page.
2. **Nobody discusses privacy**, which in India is the deciding feature. Phones get handed around;
   many relationships are not disclosed to parents; a wrapped box at the door invites a question.
   SubhSandesh's 40.6% password-protection rate is the only hard number that exists on this behaviour.
3. **Nobody ranks options by what people actually build.** Every list is an editor's opinion. The post
   ranks six options by real creation volume across 4,357 pages.
4. **Nobody admits when the digital option loses.** Not one competitor page names a case where you
   should buy the physical thing instead.

**Stale data:** not applicable — the competitive set cites no dated data at all, which is itself the
finding.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped by the competitive set:** "how do I make sure only she sees
it", "does she need an app", "is this too cheap/lazy", "what do I write".
