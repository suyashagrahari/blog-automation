## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all five cover these, so the post must too): a numbered list of
ideas; shared playlists; digital subscriptions and game passes; personalised
digital notes; countdowns to the next meeting; virtual date nights.

**The gap — four things none of the five answer:**

1. *Will he actually open it?* Nobody publishes an open rate, a device split, or
   a share rate. Every list assumes delivery.
2. *Is a shared link private?* A virtual gift for a boyfriend is often the most
   personal text either of you has written. Not one page mentions passwords,
   forwarding, or who else can open the URL.
3. *How long does making one actually take?* "Quick and easy" appears on four of
   five pages. None gives a measured duration.
4. *When is this the wrong gift?* Zero of five name a downside. That is the
   clearest E-E-A-T gap on the SERP.

**Stale data:** none of the five cites data at all, so there is nothing to
supersede — the win is publishing the first numbers in the category rather than
replacing older ones.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** "send it at the right time" appears
on three pages with no guidance on time zones; "make it personal" appears on all
five with no definition of personal.

**Fan-out sub-queries** (each becomes an H2 or an FAQ):
what counts as a virtual gift · which virtual gifts for a long distance boyfriend
are free · what does it cost · will he open a link · how do I keep it private ·
when should I send it across time zones · is a virtual gift too impersonal ·
what if he has bad internet · does it work for a birthday or only an ordinary day.

**Angle (`batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only post that says whether a
long-distance boyfriend actually opens and reopens a virtual gift — using 4,357
real personalised pages, a 94.1% share rate, a 40.6% password-protection rate and
a measured 6.2-hour median build time, against a SERP where no page carries a
single number.
