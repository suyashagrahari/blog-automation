**What the sources say, verbatim-adjacent.**

1. RAC (n = 257): the Eros style predominates and drives frequent gifting through
   a social motive; among secondary styles the altruistic **Agape** most often
   induces gift-giving and generates a **sacrificial perception** in the offerer,
   and sacrifice is perceived in gift-giving under other love styles too.
2. Estudios Gerenciales (n = 1,085, PLS-SEM): gift-giving is influenced by five
   personal values — **self-determination, hedonism, tradition, achievement and
   power**. The post's editorial reading (achievement and power are about the
   giver's standing) is stated as a reading, not as the paper's finding.
3. Nordisk Østforum: 19 Latvian interviewees, partners >45 km apart. Intimacy is
   maintained through **imagined intimacy** — inward, non-interactive practices:
   writing letters, keeping a relationship diary, copying the partner's SMS into a
   notebook; a t-shirt carrying the partner's smell. Article three of the
   dissertation identifies **eight "time work" strategies**. All zero cost.
4. IJoC: Filipino transnational couples in an "**intimacy limbo**" perform
   "**performative mediated intimacies**"; the private/public marital line blurs
   on Facebook.
5. Psychological Science and Education: n = 587 students in LDRs; Pearson r =
   0.987, p < 0.01, authors read commitment as contributing 97.4% of psychological
   well-being. **The post flags this correlation as implausibly high and declines
   to lean on the figure**, keeping only the sample size and the direction.
