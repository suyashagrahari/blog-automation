## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all five carry these, so the post must too): a definition of what a
virtual/online gift actually is; a numbered list of formats; how sharing the link works;
the "no app, no download" reassurance; a long-distance framing.

**The gap.** Four things none of the five do:

1. **Direction.** Every one is gender-neutral or implicitly written for a man choosing for
   a woman. Nobody writes it as *a woman sending to her boyfriend*, which is the actual
   searcher. This post is written entirely in that direction.
2. **The cringe question.** "Will he think this is stupid?" is the real blocker and not one
   of the five addresses it. It gets its own H2 here, answered with three studies.
3. **Real usage data.** Not one page says how often these things get opened, how long they
   take to build, or how many people lock them. All four first-party numbers here are
   unavailable to any competitor.
4. **India.** Prices in USD, statistics about US couples, no Indian context anywhere. The
   Chennai masculine-norms study and rupee/WhatsApp framing close that.

**Stale or weak data.** messagear's stat block is the only quantified competitor content
and its sources are aggregator sites ("Gitnux", "LDR Magazine", "LuvLink LDR Research
2024") with no primary study behind them. Superseding that with four peer-reviewed,
open-access papers is the clearest citation win available on this keyword.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped by the five:** whether he will actually reopen
it; what to do if he does not react; whether the link is private; whether the format
works when you will see him soon.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s.**

study of 151 urban Indian men plus SubhSandesh's own 4,357-page dataset showing three of
the five most-built page types are named for a girlfriend.
