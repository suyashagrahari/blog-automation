## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all five cover): a definition of "virtual gift"; a long
numbered list; coverage of streaming subscriptions, online classes and gift
cards; occasion labels; "delivered instantly, no shipping".

**The gap.** Not one of the five states a single measured number about what
people actually choose. Every list is ordered by affiliate value, not by
behaviour. None has a comparison table, none has FAQ markup, none quotes a
rupee figure, and none distinguishes the two unrelated things called a "virtual
gift" — a live-streaming tipping token versus a page you make for one named
person. That last confusion is why the SERP is so noisy, and no page resolves it.

**Stale data.** There is no data to be stale. This is unusual and it is the
opening: a category with zero measured content.

**Unanswered questions the pages raise and drop:** how long does making one take;
does anyone finish; do you lock it; what happens if they open it on a laptop; is
it odd with no occasion.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:**

| Sub-query | H2 |
|---|---|
| what is a virtual gift / what isn't | `## What a virtual gift is, and what this list leaves out` |
| virtual gift ideas for [partner / birthday / long distance / apology] | `## Nine virtual gift ideas, by relationship and occasion` |
| which virtual gift is best / most popular | `## Which virtual gift ideas people actually finish, in numbers` |
| how to send a virtual gift | `## How to send a virtual gift so it actually gets opened` |
| are virtual gifts a bad idea / impersonal | `## When a virtual gift is the wrong choice` |

**Angle:** wins by being the only post that ranks virtual gift ideas by how often
real people actually build and finish each one — 1,125 love pages, 1,070
bouquets, 1,043 apologies out of 4,357 — instead of listing US streaming
subscriptions nobody measured.
