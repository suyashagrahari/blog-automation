## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all five cover these; the post must too):

- a definition of what an online / digital gift actually is
- a list of concrete options
- how it gets delivered (a link or a QR code)
- personalisation advice (photos, a song, dates)
- free versus paid

**The gap — the question all five dodge.** Not one of them asks the thing the buyer is
actually nervous about: *will she think this is a real gift, or will she think I took a
shortcut?* Every ranking page assumes the answer is yes and sells from there. There is
published research that answers it directly and none of them cite it.

Three further gaps:

- **No page quantifies effort.** They all imply "3 minutes", which is precisely the
  property that makes a digital gift stop reading as a gift.
- **No page discusses who else can open the link.** A personalised page is a URL, and a
  URL is guessable, forwardable and screenshottable. Nobody raises it.
- **No page says "buy the physical thing instead."** BRIEF §2 explicitly permits and
  encourages this, and it is the clearest E-E-A-T signal available in a SERP of five
  landing pages.

**Stale data:** there is none to supersede, because none of the five cite any data at
all. The superseding move here is not "newer number" but "any number, from a named
source, with a date."

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped by the ranking pages:** what happens to the
page after it's opened; whether the recipient reciprocates; whether an online gift is
appropriate when you live in the same city.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:**

1. what is an online gift for a girlfriend → *What counts as an online gift for a girlfriend?*
2. is a digital gift lazy / does it count → *Does an online gift read as lazy? What the research says*
3. best online gift for girlfriend (secondary keyword) → *Six online gifts for a girlfriend that hold up*
4. online gift vs physical gift → comparison table H2
5. how long does it take to make one → *How long a good one actually takes*
6. is a gift link private / can anyone open it → *Who else can open the link*
7. when not to send a digital gift → *When to buy the physical thing instead*

**Angle (recorded as `batchMeta.angle`):** this post wins by being the only one in the
SERP that answers whether an online gift actually *reads* as a gift to the person
receiving it — pairing the CSCW 2017 finding that digital gifts "often involve less
labor" and "are sometimes not perceived as gifts by the recipient" with SubhSandesh's
own 6.2-hour median build time and 40.6% password-lock rate across 4,357 pages.

## Phase 3 — Sources

Five sources. Zero generic context statistics (no PIB / TRAI / Census / MEA). Zero
Wikipedia in the body. Every abstract below was fetched and read; where only the
abstract was read, that is stated.

| # | Source | Date | Verified finding used | Subject test | Peer reviewed / OA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kwon, Koleva, Garrett, Benford — "'It's Not Yet A Gift': Understanding Digital Gifting", **CSCW '17** (ACM). Open copy: Loughborough repository | 2017-02-14 | "digital gifts often involve less labor, are sometimes not perceived as gifts by the recipient and are rarely reflected on and reciprocated" | **Pass** — the paper is literally about digital gifting | Peer reviewed (CSCW), open via institutional repository |
| 2 | Chan & Mogilner — "Experiential Gifts Foster Stronger Social Relationships Than Material Gifts", *Journal of Consumer Research*. Open PDF hosted by UCLA Anderson | 2016-11-07 | experiential gifts produce greater improvements in relationship strength than material gifts, and the improvement comes from the intensity of emotion evoked **when the gift is consumed**, not when it is received | **Pass** — gift-giving and relationship strength | Peer reviewed; **abstract only was read** (the hosted PDF did not yield extractable text) |
| 3 | "Valuing digital possessions: the role of affordances", *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication* 27(6) | 2022-09-15 | value in digital possessions is released "in the doing" — people must do things with digital objects to obtain value from them; 47 postphenomenological interviews in the UK | **Pass** — how people value non-physical things | Peer reviewed, open access |
| 4 | Jiang & Hancock — "Absence Makes the Communication Grow Fonder", *Journal of Communication* 63(3). Open PDF hosted by Stanford Social Media Lab | 2013-05-11 | long-distance couples make more adaptive self-disclosures and form more idealised perceptions; effects vary with the **cue multiplicity, synchronicity and mobility** of the medium | **Pass** — mediated intimacy at distance | Peer reviewed; **abstract only was read** |
| 5 | Madianou & Miller — "Polymedia: Towards a new theory of digital media in interpersonal communication", *International Journal of Cultural Studies*. Open record: University of Leicester figshare | 2012-08-22 | drawn from a comparative ethnography of Filipino and Caribbean transnational families: under polymedia the choice of medium itself "acquires communicative intent", shifting emphasis to the social, emotional and moral consequences of choosing between media | **Pass** — mediated relationships across distance | Peer reviewed; abstract verified via OpenAlex and the figshare record. Record is marked All Rights Reserved, so it is cited and linked, never reproduced |

**Searches run:** `digital gift giving research open access study symbolic value virtual
gifts`, `digital possessions immateriality gift giving study "digital gift" consumer
research open access`, `experiential gifts foster stronger social relationships than
material gifts`, `Jiang Hancock absence makes the communication grow fonder`,
`"It's Not Yet A Gift" Understanding Digital Gifting CSCW pdf`.

**Rejected:** arXiv 2501.09235 (virtual gifting on Twitch) — genuinely about virtual
gifting, but about pay-it-forward dynamics in a live-streaming chat room, which has no
bearing on one person sending one page to one partner. Including it would have been
citation padding.

**Rejected on the batch cap, after drafting:** arXiv 2509.16932 ("Understanding Digital
Gifting Through Messengers Across Cultures", CSCW 2025) was the original source 5 and is
an excellent subject-test fit. `blogs/` was empty when this post started, but by the time
it was validated **all six concurrently-written posts in this batch had independently
cited the same paper** — 6 posts against a 2-post URL cap, and arxiv.org in 9 source
slots against a 3-post domain cap. It was replaced here with Madianou & Miller's
polymedia paper, which makes a stronger version of the same argument (medium choice
carries communicative intent) from an ethnography of transnational families rather than
from university students in East Asia. **The cap is still breached across the other five
posts and they will each need the same fix** — this post alone cannot resolve it.

**Batch cap check after the swap:** domains claimed by this post — `repository.lboro.ac.uk`,
`anderson.ucla.edu`, `academic.oup.com`, `socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu`,
`figshare.le.ac.uk` — none of which appears in any sibling post. Every URL here is used
once across the batch.

**Swap-test honesty:** source 2 (Chan & Mogilner) is the weakest on the swap test. It is
about gifts in general rather than online gifts specifically, so it could sit in another
post on this axis. It is kept because it is the only peer-reviewed result that gives the
*mechanism* — emotion at the moment of consumption — that the whole "make it something
she does, not something she looks at" argument rests on. This is recorded as a failure in
the audit rather than hidden.
