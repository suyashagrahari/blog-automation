found "in.pinterest.com twice plus video results"; the full localised harvest finds **zero**
text results of any kind, competitor or otherwise.

What follows from that, stated without spin:

- A markdown blog post is the wrong asset to win **position 1** for this query. The query's
  dominant intent is "show me pictures/video I can copy", and Google is serving exactly that.
- The realistic targets for a post are (a) the PAA slots, which are text-answerable —
  especially *"Do we make rangoli on Ganesh Chaturthi?"* — (b) longer-tail variants that carry a
  constraint the image boards cannot answer (`how long does a rangoli take`, `rangoli that
  survives being walked on`, `rangoli powder quantity for 3 feet`), and (c) answer-engine
  citation, where a text page with numbers beats a Pinterest board by default because a board
  has no extractable prose at all.
- This is recorded as an audit failure with an honest `why`, not papered over.

Because there are **no informational top-5 pages to profile**, the Phase 1 per-page profiling
step (word count, heading structure, where the answer lands, data cited) cannot be run against
the actual ranking set. For gap analysis I read the nearest-ranking informational pages that

## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (what any page on this query is expected to carry, from the near-competitors
and the PAA): named design types for the festival (Ganesha face/silhouette, modak, om/swastik,
lotus, peacock, dotted/`sikku` grid, floral/`pookalam`-style); a beginner-to-advanced spread;
the materials list; where the rangoli goes (threshold, in front of the mandap); the festival
date.

**The gap — and it is a wide one.** Every competing asset shows a *finished* rangoli.
Not one states:

- **time-to-draw** — how many minutes a given design actually takes on a real doorstep;
- **floor area** and therefore how much powder or flour a design needs;
- **durability** — what survives being walked on for ten days of visitors, versus what is gone
  by the first aarti;
- **when it is drawn** — that a festival rangoli is made on the morning of, in the same
  compressed window in which people do everything else festival-related.

That last point is where SubhSandesh's own measurement earns its place. The batch's Raksha
Bandhan lead-time probe (n=89, both rakhi collections) found **49.4% of pages built on the
festival day itself**, **median lead time 0 days**, and of the 44 same-day pages, **24 before
noon and only 5 after 18:00**. A morning-of, one-sitting behaviour. Rangoli is the one
Ganesh-Chaturthi "decoration" that *must* be made morning-of — it cannot be bought in advance
and it cannot survive being made early. The behavioural finding and the craft constraint are
the same shape, and no competing page connects them because none of them has behavioural data.

**Stale data in the competing set:** not applicable — the competing set cites no data at all,
of any vintage. Titles carry "2025" and "2026" year-stamps that are the only dated element on
the page.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** the PAA asks "Do we make rangoli on Ganesh
Chaturthi?" and the ranking assets, being images, answer it only by implication.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2/FAQ candidates:**

1. Do you make rangoli for Ganesh Chaturthi, and on which day?
2. How long does a Ganesh Chaturthi rangoli take to draw?
3. Which designs are easy enough for a beginner in under 20 minutes?
4. What material should I use — rice flour, stone powder, flower petals, chalk?
5. How much powder do I need for a 2-foot / 3-foot design?
6. Will it survive people walking over it for ten days?
7. Should the rangoli go at the door or in front of the mandap?
8. What is the geometry behind a dotted rangoli / kolam?
9. Is rangoli a Ganesh Chaturthi thing or a daily thing?
10. Do I redraw it every day of the ten days?
11. What do I do with the rangoli at visarjan?
12. Can flower rangoli be reused or composted?

(n=89) were built on the festival day itself with a median lead time of 0 days.
