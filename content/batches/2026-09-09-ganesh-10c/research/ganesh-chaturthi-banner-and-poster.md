## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (what all ten results implicitly supply, so the post must acknowledge):
a size, a file format, an aspect ratio, festive iconography (modak, durva, kalash, mushak),
and the words "Happy Ganesh Chaturthi" set as display type.

**The gap.** Not one result states a text height, a viewing distance or a contrast ratio.
PosterMyWall and Canva give you a canvas in pixels; Adobe Stock and Vecteezy give you a
vector. None of them tells you that the same file which reads perfectly at arm's length on a
phone is illegible from the far side of a lane — and none of them separates the **printed
banner** (viewed at 5–30 m, landscape, one line of text) from the **shared image** (viewed at
30–40 cm, portrait or square, cropped by the platform). Different aspect ratios, different
text sizes, different failure modes. That is the post.

**Stale data.** Not applicable — the results carry no cited data at all, stale or otherwise.

**Unanswered questions the results raise and drop:** what size to order a flex banner in;
why a downloaded 1080×1080 poster prints blurry at 4×3 ft; whether a WhatsApp status crop
will cut the sponsor line; whether gold-on-orange is readable in direct sun.

**Fan-out sub-queries** → become the H2s and FAQs:
1. How big should a Ganesh Chaturthi banner be?
2. How large does the text have to be to read from the street?
3. What is the right size for a poster shared on WhatsApp or Instagram?
4. Why is banner ≠ poster ≠ shared image?
5. What contrast do the festival colours actually give you?
6. What resolution do I need for print vs screen?
7. When is a banner the wrong choice altogether?

**Angle (recorded as `batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only post that turns
sign-legibility research into an actual text height for a Ganesh Chaturthi banner versus a
shared image — 2.4 mm of cap height per metre of viewing distance, 36 mm at 15 m against
0.8 mm at 35 cm — and states which of the two surfaces SubhSandesh can serve, using its own
49.6% phone share of 46,005 measured views.

### What was searched and deliberately not cited

An Indic-script legibility source was wanted and not found in usable form. Searched
`Devanagari typeface legibility research open access`, `Indic script legibility studies`,
`Devanagari character recognition distance`. The best candidate is Vishwanath et al.,
*"A method for studying the contextual similarity of characters in Cyrillic, Devanagari, and
Latin scripts"*, **Visual Cognition**, doi:10.1080/13506285.2026.2627306, confirmed CC BY 4.0
and open access via OpenAlex. It is **not cited**, because the publisher returned HTTP 403 and
the University of Reading repository copy (CentAUR 128544) is a CID-encoded PDF whose body text
could not be extracted — so no specific finding in it could be verified. Citing it from the
title alone would be citing from memory. Recorded here as a real gap: **this post has no
source on Devanagari or Marathi legibility, and its cap-height numbers are derived from
Latin-script and highway-sign research.** That is a genuine limitation of the advice for a
banner set in Devanagari, where the shirorekha and conjunct stack change the effective
x-height, and it would close if a readable Indic legibility study were found.

### Derived numbers — my arithmetic, not anybody's published figure

These are computed in the post and stated as conversions, not attributed to a source:

- 35 ft/in → **2.38 mm of cap height per metre**; 17 ft/in → **4.90 mm/m**; 30 ft/in → 2.78 mm/m.
- 0.2° x-height → **3.49 mm per metre** of viewing distance (52 mm at 15 m).
- One arcminute → 4.36 mm at 15 m → **5.8 ppi acuity limit**; 29 ppi at 3 m, 87 ppi at 1 m,
  250 ppi at 35 cm. An 8 × 4 ft banner is fully resolved by ~557 × 278 px; 1080 px across 8 ft
  is 11.3 ppi.
- Canvas fractions: 36 mm on a 1219 mm-tall banner = **2.93%**; 0.83 mm on a 141 mm phone
  display = **0.59%**. Ratio **4.96×** — the five-fold figure the post leads on.
- A 2:1 image fitted into a 9:16 frame occupies **28.1%** of the frame height, so
  banner-proportioned text lands at ~1.16 mm — above the 0.83 mm average-reader floor, below
  the 1.72 mm an 85th-percentile older reader needs. **Asymmetric failure:** phone→print fails
  by 5× for everyone, print→phone fails only for weak-acuity readers.
- WCAG contrast ratios computed with the standard relative-luminance formula:
  gold `#D4AF37` on saffron `#FF9933` = **1.01:1**; white on saffron 2.13:1; `#FFD700` on
  `#FF6F00` 1.99:1; white on marigold `#FFB300` 1.79:1; white on `#7B1E1E` 10.26:1;
  `#7B1E1E` on `#FFF3D6` 9.30:1; `#D4AF37` on `#7B1E1E` 4.88:1; white on `#C62828` 5.62:1;
  `#4A1B0F` on saffron 6.77:1.
