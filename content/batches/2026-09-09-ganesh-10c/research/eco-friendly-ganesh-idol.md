## 6. Recommendation — fold into `ganpati-visarjan-2026`

Extend the existing H2 **"What immersion actually does to a lake"** rather than creating
a new post. Three additions, in priority order:

1. **Per-material dissolution timescale.** A controlled lab experiment compared small,
   painted, identical idols in **shadu clay, PoP and paper mache**, in fresh and marine
   water, over **60 days**: clay dissolved completely (faster in marine than fresh
   water), PoP remained insoluble and accumulated as sediment. This is the timescale
   claim the live post lacks — it currently has only field before/after chemistry.
   **Blocker: sourcing.** The study surfaced only on `academia.edu` and
   `researchgate.net` — aggregator mirrors, not open-access publishers. **Do not cite
   either.** Resolve to the original journal first; if it cannot be resolved, drop the
   numeric claim and keep only what CPCB itself states about PoP insolubility.
2. **Separate paint from plaster.** The live post reports cadmium and lead but attributes
   the chemistry loosely. Synthetic pigment and PoP are two distinct inputs, and an
   unpainted clay idol and a painted clay idol are not the same object — worth one
   sentence, because it is the actual buying decision.
3. **The honest limits of clay.** A clay idol still adds silt and turbidity; shadu is
   mined; natural dyes are not universally available. The post already refuses to
   over-claim for the eco label; it should also refuse to over-claim for clay. This is
   the strongest remaining piece of the assigned angle and needs no new source.

Also add `eco friendly ganesh idol` / `eco friendly ganpati idol` to that post's declared
folds if — and only if — the format finding in §4 is accepted, i.e. that the head term is
a shopping query the site should not chase with a standalone article.

**Do not** create `blogs/eco-friendly-ganesh-idol.json`. The slug stays free on Strapi.
