## Phase 2 — gap and angle

**Table stakes:** a set of usable messages; the case of a friend proposing; the case of a
partner proposing; something about honesty and timing.

**The gap, stated narrowly:**

1. **The refusal has to travel.** In the Indian family-mediated case the "no" is said to a
   parent, an aunt or a broker — not to the person. No page I read gives that message.
2. **Reasons are treated as a kindness.** Three of four pages hand the reader an excuse.
   A supplied reason is a thing the other side can rebut, fix, or relay back as a
   counter-offer. This page argues the opposite and says why.
3. **The softened no is never named as the unkind option.** It is the default advice.
4. **Persistence is a safety matter, not a romance one.** Almost absent.
5. **Nobody says "send nothing built".** Every commercial page in this space has a product
   behind it. This one names the wrong object and declines to sell one.

**Angle:** wins by being the only page on this query written for the person saying no —
including the Indian case where the refusal must travel through parents or a broker —
using IHDS's 73% of women whose parents or relatives alone chose their husband and
SubhSandesh's own 5,148 pages across 21 page types, none of which fits a refusal.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s / FAQs:** what a rejection message is · how long should it be ·
do I owe a reason · how to say no to a rishta · how to tell my parents no · can I say it
over text · what if they keep contacting me · what if I already said yes.
