## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present across the SERP, must be covered or the page looks thin):

- 8 February as the date.
- That the card can carry a name and a photo.
- That it is shareable on WhatsApp / Instagram.
- That there is a free way to do it.

**The gap.** Every result answers *"where do I get a card?"*. **Not one answers
"what does a card have to do on that particular morning?"** The occasion is
treated as a design theme, not as a dated event with a weekday, a window, and an
expected reply. Specifically missing everywhere:

1. The **weekday**. Propose Day 2027 is a **Monday**; 2026 was a Sunday. Nothing
   on the SERP has a weekday on it at all.
2. The **reply path**. A propose day card is the one card in the year that expects
   an answer, and no template tool treats the answer as part of the artefact.
3. **Whose card it is.** Free template output carries the maker's branding, and
   nobody on the SERP mentions it (obviously — they are the maker).
4. **Why Rose Day and Propose Day are different kinds of day.** 7 February has a
   physical supply chain; 8 February has only words.

**Stale data.** The 2025/2026 year-stamps across four-plus incumbent titles and
URLs. Superseding them with a correct, weekday-accurate 2027 is the cheapest
freshness win available and the brief flags it as the batch-wide lever.

**Unanswered questions raised but not resolved:** whether a free card is watermarked;
whether the recipient needs an account; how long the thing takes to make.

**Fan-out sub-queries** (each became an H2 or an FAQ): when is Propose Day 2027 /
is it the first day of Valentine's week / what to write on a propose day card /
is a propose day card the same as a Valentine's card / is it okay to propose with
a card / will the free version be watermarked / morning or evening / how long does
it take / can I lock the card.

**Angle:** *Wins by being the only post that treats the propose day card as an
object owed on a specific dated morning — Monday 8 February 2027, the second day
of Valentine's week — rather than a JPG to download, grounding the format choice
in platform behaviour numbers and in peer-reviewed work on how imported romantic
holidays are re-shaped locally.*

### What could NOT be sourced — stated in the body rather than asserted

The brief flagged that Valentine week's day-by-day structure (Rose / Propose /
Chocolate / Teddy / Promise / Hug / Kiss) being a **South Asian elaboration absent
from the Western original** would be the single most interesting fact available.

**No peer-reviewed source for it was found.** Terms tried, across DOAJ, OpenAlex,
Crossref, Europe PMC and web search:

- `"Valentine week"` (DOAJ: 0 results)
- `"Valentine's Day" globalization localization` / `Valentine Day India culture`
  (DOAJ: 0 relevant)
- `Valentine's Day India cultural reception peer reviewed`
- `Valentine's Day India commercialization youth culture` (Crossref: nothing on point)
- `Valentine's Day globalization non-Western adoption`
- `invented tradition romantic holiday` / `invented tradition festival consumption` (DOAJ: 0)
- `"White Day"` / `"giri choco"` / `"obligation chocolate"` — the Japanese
  parallel, which would have carried the "imported holiday sprouts locally
  invented extra days" argument by analogy (DOAJ: 0 relevant)
- `Valentine's Day localization Asia consumer` / `Valentine Day China Japan localization`

The only sources stating it are Wikipedia and content farms. **The body therefore
says the structure is observed in India, states plainly that we could not find
peer-reviewed scholarship on its origin, and refuses to invent a history for it** —
then makes the weaker but defensible claim the Estonian and Serbian papers do
support: imported romantic holidays reliably change shape when they land. That
refusal is itself a differentiator on a SERP where nobody sources anything.
