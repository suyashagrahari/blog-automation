## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes (must be present or the page reads incomplete):** what Propose Day is
and its position in Valentine week; the date; some concrete set of ways to propose;
what to say; the ring/no-ring question; what happens if she says no.

**The gap (none of the five cover any of these):**
1. **The weekday.** Propose Day 2027 is a Monday. Nobody states it, and one page
   states it wrongly. All the advice assumes unlimited time and a free evening.
2. **Time of day.** The difference between 10:40am in a standup and 9:30pm at home
   is the entire outcome, and it is never discussed.
3. **Different cities.** Treated, at best, as "send a gift". Never as a planning
   constraint that changes what you should do.
4. **Ask type.** "Propose" is doing double duty — marriage proposal vs "will you be
   my girlfriend" — and every page silently assumes marriage.
5. **The case against the date.** No page is willing to say that a date on which
   everyone proposes carries less signal than a date you chose.

**Stale data:** there is no data to be stale. Not one of the five pages cites a
statistic, a study or a dated source. That is the opening.

**Unanswered questions raised but not resolved:** "make it personal" (how?),
"choose the right moment" (which moment?), "be sincere" (say what?).

**Fan-out sub-queries → became H2s or FAQs:** when is Propose Day 2027 / what day of
the week / what time to propose / how to propose long distance / what to say / is it
for marriage or for asking someone out / what if she says no / should I do it in
public / ring or no ring / Propose Day vs Valentine's Day / what if we just had a
fight / how much to spend.

**Angle (recorded as `batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only post that plans
around Propose Day 2027 being a working Monday — hour-by-hour windows, a
different-cities branch and the case against the date itself — grounded in the
platform-wide 40.0% password-protection rate across 4,524 pages and the 48.9%
phone-open share of 51,164 views.

### Used

1. **Bieleke, Keller & Gollwitzer, "If-then planning", *European Review of Social
   Psychology* 32(1), 88–122 — published online 10 December 2020, CC BY 4.0.**
   `https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10463283.2020.1808936`
   Verified: full text read. Reports a summary table of meta-analytic effect sizes
   for implementation intentions, including Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), *d* = 0.65,
   and a meta-analysis of meta-analyses at *d* = 0.54; also states the
   intention–behaviour gap plainly ("only about half of those who want to engage in
   physical activity perform the intended behaviour").
   *Note on retrieval:* tandfonline.com returns HTTP 403 to automated fetching. The
   text was read from the identical CC BY author copy deposited at KOPS, University
   of Konstanz (bitstream for `urn:nbn:de:bsz:352-2-1b6t3bswnb0k79`). The version of
   record is what is cited and linked.
   Subject test: pass (rehearsal/planning as a determinant of whether an intention
   becomes an action). Swap test: pass — no sibling post in this batch argues about
   rehearsing a specific sentence at a specific time.

2. **"Rejection in romantic relationships: Does rejection sensitivity modulate
   emotional responses to perceptions of negative interactions?", *BMC Psychology*
   12(1) — 25 June 2024, CC BY 4.0.**
   `https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-024-01864-w`
   Verified: **abstract only** — both link.springer.com and
   bmcpsychology.biomedcentral.com return a bot-challenge page to automated
   fetching; the abstract was read in full via the Europe PMC REST API and the
   metadata cross-checked against Crossref. **Full text not read; recorded as an
   audit item.** Abstract states: N = 298 couples in the transition to parenthood,
   multilevel modelling of repeated daily reports; rejection-sensitive individuals
   did **not** report higher negative emotions when they perceived negative
   interactions, and did not remain longer in a negative emotional state.
   Subject test: pass (fear of rejection inside a romantic relationship). Swap test:
   pass.

3. **Itzkin, Van Dijk & Azar, "At Least I Tried: The Relationship between Regulatory
   Focus and Regret Following Action vs. Inaction", *Frontiers in Psychology* —
   27 October 2016, CC BY.**
   `https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01684/full`
   Verified: abstract read in full via Europe PMC REST. 330 participants, online
   survey, six decision scenarios rating regret after action and after inaction.
   Findings used: promotion-focused individuals attributed less regret than
   prevention-focused individuals to action decisions; **a trigger for change in the
   situation decreases regret following action**.
   Subject test: pass (regret after acting vs not acting). Swap test: pass — it is
   load-bearing here specifically because Propose Day *is* a trigger for change.
   Domain count check: frontiersin.org appears in 1 sibling post; this makes 2, under
   the cap of 3.

4. **Mendes Rodrigues, Carneiro & Sousa, "Past and future regret and missed
   opportunities: an experimental approach on separate evaluation and different time
   frames", *Psicologia: Reflexão e Crítica* 30(1) — 21 September 2017, CC BY 4.0.**
   `https://prc.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s41155-017-0074-8`
   Verified: **abstract only** — springeropen.com serves a bot-challenge page;
   abstract read in full via Europe PMC REST, metadata cross-checked against
   Crossref. **Full text not read; recorded as an audit item.** 216 participants;
   when evaluated separately, future misses caused less regret than past misses, but
   future misses shifted feelings of regret more intensely, and **regret levels did
   not decrease when future misses were further away**.
   Subject test: pass (regret over a missed opportunity, which is the exact shape of
   "should I wait for a better day"). Swap test: pass.

5. **Wikipedia, "Propose Day"** — `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propose_Day`
   Entity/date reference, not research. Used once in the body to disambiguate the
   observance and to support the statement that Valentine's Week is India-specific.
   No publication date (continuously edited), so `datePublished` is omitted from the
   `citation` block per `references/structured-data.md`. Appears in 1 sibling post;
   this makes 2, at the URL cap.

### Searched and deliberately not used

- **MDPI (first-listed allocated repository) produced no topical fit.** Queried via
  DOAJ and web search for `anticipated regret` (35 DOAJ hits) and
  `rejection sensitivity` (84 hits) restricted to MDPI journals. Every MDPI result in
  the anticipated-regret literature is health-behaviour or consumer/supply-chain —
  HPV vaccination, COVID vaccination, pre-sale pricing, pro-environmental behaviour,
  total knee arthroplasty. Every MDPI rejection-sensitivity result is clinical
  (problematic internet use, body dysmorphia, emotional abuse, trauma-informed care).
  All fail the subject test for a post about proposing to your girlfriend. Recorded
  as an audit note rather than papered over with an off-topic citation.
- `anticipated regret` × `romantic`/`interpersonal` in DOAJ: **0 results**. The
  anticipated-regret literature is overwhelmingly health and consumer. That is a real
  finding: the emotion is well studied, the romantic application is not.
- `"romantic initiation"`, `"expressing romantic interest"`, `"confession of love"`
  in DOAJ titles: **0 results**.
- `"marriage proposal"` in DOAJ titles: 4 results, all about grant proposals.
- **No generic context statistic was used at all.** MoSPI's PLFS table on average
  weekly hours worked was sought to support the "Monday is a working day" claim and
  is JavaScript-gated to automated retrieval; rather than substitute an unrelated
  government figure, the claim is left as the plain calendar fact it is. Per the
  batch brief, no Valentine-week retail spending release was used.
