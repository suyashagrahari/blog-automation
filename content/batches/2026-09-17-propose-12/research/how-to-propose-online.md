## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** — all pages cover, so the post must too:
- Build a dedicated page/link rather than sending a plain message
- Pick a template/theme; add photos and your story
- The interactive YES button, and the NO button that dodges the cursor
- Pair the link with a video call
- Long-distance framing

**The gap — none of the five cover any of these:**
1. **What happens to the link after you send it.** Not one page mentions that a URL is
   open to anyone holding it, or that you can password-protect it. This is the post's
   lead and its strongest first-party hook.
2. **Which question you are actually asking.** All five treat "propose online" as one
   thing. It is at least five: marriage, "be my girlfriend", a date, a make-up-then-ask,
   and a long-distance delivery. This is the hub structure.
3. **When not to do it.** No page names a case where an online proposal is the wrong
   call. E-E-A-T gap and a conversion gap at once.
4. **How long it actually takes.** Two pages claim "5 minutes" / "60 seconds". First-party
   data says the median first-save → last-edit gap is 6.3 hours (sampled on /love-gf).
5. **Any research at all.** Zero sources across all five pages.

**Stale data:** result #5 is a 2009 URL whose article no longer exists; result #3's
related posts date to 2013. BRIEF.md notes four incumbents still carry a 2025 title.
The post states **Propose Day 2027 = Monday 8 February 2027** explicitly (verified:
1 Jan 2027 is a Friday, 1 Feb 2027 is a Monday, so 8 Feb 2027 is a Monday).

**Unanswered questions the pages raise and drop:** whether an online proposal "counts";
what to do if she says no on the page; whether free builders keep the page up.

**Angle:** wins by being the only post that routes the reader to the right kind of online
