## Phase 1 — SERP analysis

### The split the BRIEF predicted, confirmed

A bare search for **"online proposal link"** returns **7 of 8 B2B proposal software**: Flipsnack,
Visme, Canva, Better Proposals, Jotform, FlippingBook, Venngage. Only PixeryHub appears from the
romance half. This is not a search failure — it is the finding. The head phrase is owned by
business-proposal SaaS, and the romance intent only surfaces once a romance word is added
("will you marry me", "girlfriend", "propose").

Practical consequence: the post has to earn the romance interpretation inside its own title,
H1 and first sentence rather than assuming it. Every one of them does.

Romance-qualified SERP (`"proposal link" will you marry me send link girlfriend online`) returns
the real competitive set. All are two-person product startups, not publishers. Per
`references/competitors.md` none is cited, linked or paraphrased anywhere in the post.

## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

### Table stakes (all five cover — the post must not look incomplete)

- What the link is and that it opens as its own page
- The three-step flow: write it → get a link → share it on WhatsApp / Instagram / iMessage
- The runaway "NO" button as the signature mechanic
- Free, no sign-up, no app, works on any device
- What to write on the page (story, photo, the plain question at the end)
- That it suits long distance, and works as a companion to an in-person proposal

### The gap — what none of the five address

This is the whole post. Not one of the five pages says anything about:

| Unanswered question | Nearest thing any page says |
|---|---|
| Does the link expire? | One page asserts "Never! Your link stays active forever" — an unsupported promise about its own survival |
| Can I delete it afterwards? | Nothing, on any page |
| Who else can open it if it is forwarded? | Nothing, on any page |
| Can the page be password-protected? | **Nothing, on any page.** Not one of the five mentions a password |
| Will the WhatsApp preview spoil the surprise? | One page asserts the preview "doesn't spoil the surprise" with no explanation of how previews are built |
| Can I tell whether they opened it? | Two offer a notification email; none distinguishes a view from a read |
| What if they screenshot it instead? | Framed only as a positive ("screen-recordable so she keeps the moment") |
| What if they open it at work, or with someone beside them? | Nothing, on any page |
