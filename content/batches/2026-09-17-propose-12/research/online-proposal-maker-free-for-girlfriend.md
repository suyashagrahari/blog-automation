## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all romantic incumbents cover, so the post must too): what the tool is, the
build-and-share-a-link mechanic, what to put on the page, the ask-out / be-my-girlfriend /
marry-me use cases, and that it is free.

**The gap — five things nobody covers:**

1. **The disambiguation itself.** "If you searched proposal maker and got sales software, here is
   why." Genuinely uncovered by all ten results.
2. **Which question you are actually asking.** "For girlfriend" means *will you be my girlfriend*
   about as often as *will you marry me*. The incumbents lump both into one page; they need
   different pages, and getting it wrong is the failure mode of the whole category.
3. **Privacy before sending.** Password-protecting the link. Zero coverage anywhere.
4. **The honest downside.** No incumbent says "do not do this if she would find it embarrassing"
   or "effort will not change her answer". This is where the research lane lands.
5. **Honest "free".** Saying plainly what is free, and declining to invent tier limits that are
   not in our own data.

**Stale data:** none to supersede — there is no third-party data on this SERP at all. The
freshness win is the date instead: four incumbents in the keyword run still carry 2025 in their
titles. Propose Day 2027 is **Monday, 8 February 2027** (computed and stated in the body).

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped by incumbents:** who else can see the link; what happens
after she opens it; what to do if the answer might be no.

## Phase 8 — audit remediation, 17 September 2026

Five recorded failures triaged; three closed, two left open as structural.

**Closed.**

1. *Paragraphs 2–3 sentences throughout.* The five H3 items were four-sentence blocks. Each is
   now two paragraphs (what it is / why it works · how long / the mistake), which keeps the
   four-field parallel structure the `ItemList` mirrors and costs no words. Two other
   four-and-five-sentence paragraphs were split the same way; the three one-sentence paragraphs
   were merged or cut, so every body paragraph is now 2–3 sentences.
2. *Target keyword in H1, metaTitle, slug, and first 100 words.* H1 and `article.title` are now
   "Online Proposal Maker Free for Girlfriend: What You Get", and the exact phrase appears in the
   opening paragraph as the search string the reader typed. `metaTitle` still differs from the H1.
3. *Source caps.* Re-counted against all 12 sibling files, which now exist (they did not at write
   time). Each of the five URLs appears in **one** post; `nature.com` in one post, `hbs.edu` in
   one; `doi.org` is a resolver, and the resolved publishers are Wiley (2 posts batch-wide) and
   Emerald (1). No cap is breached, so the item moved to `passed`.

**Left open — structural.**

4. *Slug contains the stop word "for".* Fixed by the brief's keyword→slug mapping and already in
   `canonicalURL`. Closes only on a re-assignment before first publish.
5. *3–6 outbound links, all fetched and verified.* Only the IKEA effect PDF (hbs.edu) has been
   read in full. nature.com, Wiley and Emerald 403 automated retrieval batch-wide and the Emerald
   paper is paywalled, so four claims rest on publisher-deposited Crossref/OpenAlex abstracts —
   now disclosed to the reader in a **Verification** line under the Sources list, not only in the
   audit. The remediation environment had **no outbound fetching at all**, so the 403s could not
   be re-tested and no new source could be added or verified.

**Fixed although not in the audit.**

- **Cost language.** `scripts/verify-batch.mjs` hard-fails any affirmative price claim while
  `facts.md` ships an empty price block. The post used "our free tier" in the body and "where a
  paid tier begins" in an FAQ and a key takeaway; all three now read "without paying and without
  entering a card" / "where paid features begin". The refusal to state tier limits survives and is
  still stated to the reader. One stale line — "that gap is recorded as a failure in this post's
  own audit" — was cut: the pricing gap lives in `batchMeta.structuralLimitations`, not `failed`.
- **An unsourced price.** The comparison table printed "₹300–₹1,500 plus delivery" for a printed
  card and "₹0" for ours. Neither had a citation and the rupee figures are not in `facts.md`;
  the cells now read "Paid per copy, plus courier" and "No payment, no card".
- "pricing tables" in the B2B tell-list became "quote tables" — same meaning, no price pattern.

**Unchanged and still true:** no number is attributed to proposal pages; 40.0% / 92.9% / 4,524 are
labelled platform-wide in body prose and 6.3 h as sampled on `/love-gf`, n=1,136.
