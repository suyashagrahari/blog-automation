## Phase 2 — gap analysis
- **Table stakes:** a long list of activities; mix of home / out / food / crafts; something for kids and adults; a printable/checklist feel.
- **The gap (none of the five does any of it):** (1) no page gives dates — not the equinox, the moon, or that the clocks change inside the Halloween season; (2) no page spreads the list across the season — every list is undated, so it collapses into the last week; (3) no time or cost per item; (4) no grouping by who (solo / couple / family / friends) except Hallmark's thin "with friends"; (5) none covers the "fall bucket list" sub-intent with leaf-colour timing; (6) none cites any research on why anticipation is worth planning for.
- **Stale data:** none cited at all.
- **Unanswered:** when to start; how many items is realistic; what changes between 2026 and 2027 (UK clocks go back ON Halloween night 2027; Halloween 2027 is two days after a new moon).
- **Fan-out sub-queries → H2s/FAQs:** what is a Halloween bucket list; when to start; Halloween season dates 2026/2027 (moon, clocks); ideas by week; ideas by who (solo/couple/family/friends); fall bucket list / when do leaves peak; how many items; cost; sharing with someone far away.

**Angle (batchMeta.angle):** Wins by being the only halloween bucket list that dates every one of 40 original items across the 1 September–5 November season with time and cost estimates and verified 2026/2027 anchors (equinox, full and new moons, UK and US clock changes), using the Raksha Bandhan lead-time data (49.4% built on the day, none more than 7 days ahead) to argue for spreading the list out.

## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched 2026-10-14)
1. **Pancini E, Cremaschi G, Degani M, Cisotto E, Villani D. "Savor-Aging: The Art of Savoring Positive Emotions in Older Adulthood — A Randomized Controlled Trial." Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being, 2026 (epub 4 Jul 2026), CC BY.** https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13332415 — full text read. 76 healthy older adults (M = 69.9), 3-week online intervention, six activities across past/present/future; one is "anticipation of positive events" (visualise positive events in the next hours, days or weeks). Savoring group showed more consistent improvements, reductions in negative affect and depressive symptoms maintained at 1-month follow-up; most time × group interactions were NOT significant (stated in body).
2. **Dawson C, Johnson SGB. "Asymmetric Anticipatory Emotions and Economic Preferences: Dread, Savoring, Risk, and Time." Cognitive Science 50(1):e70160, 20 Jan 2026, CC BY.** https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12818390 — full text read. UK panel data (BHPS ~10,000 households; Understanding Society 40,000+); displeasure from anticipating losses outweighs pleasure from anticipating equal gains; lab studies find little savoring but substantial dread. Used for the "a 40-item list can become dread" nuance.
