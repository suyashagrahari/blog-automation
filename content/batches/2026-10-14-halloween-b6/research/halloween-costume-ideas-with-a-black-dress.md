## Phase 2: gap analysis
- **Table stakes:** witch, cat, vampire, Audrey/Holly Golightly, Wednesday/Morticia, skeleton. All of these are owned by siblings or are too generic, so this post deliberately leaves them out and says so in a FAQ.
- **Gap:** no page organises the ideas by the dress's cut, gives budgets or build times, or says how to avoid damaging the dress. None explains the history or why black reads as frightening. None mentions being visible at night.
- **Stale data:** the real pages are from 2012 and 2018.
- **Fan-out:** what to be in a black dress, ideas by slip/maxi/sleeved cut, the cheapest option, non-witch options, office-safe options, why black is spooky, who made the LBD famous, night visibility, how to share the photo.
- **Angle:** see `batchMeta.angle`.

## Phase 3: sources (all fetched)
1. Vogue, 1 Oct 1926, Vol 68 Iss 7, p. 69 (archive.org OCR, full issue). The caption reads "The Chanel 'Ford'—the frock that all the world will wear—is model '817,' of black crêpe de Chine". **Correction to the prompt:** the print does not say "Chanel's Ford", and it does not say "little black dress". The issue is US public domain (1926).
2. V&A T.86-1974. A 1922 Chanel evening dress in black georgette. Its label says black dresses had maximum exposure in magazines, particularly Vogue, from 1925 to 1927. Read through the V&A API. The label carries no date.
3. Jonauskaite & Mohr 2025, Psychon Bull Rev (Springer, CC BY), PMC12325498, full text read through the Europe PMC API. It covers 132 articles, 42,266 participants and 64 countries. Black is linked to sadness in 75% of studies and to fear in 68%. Black also links to high-power emotions. This paper does not appear in the BRIEF §10 list.
4. Wood, Tyrrell, Lacherez & Black 2017, Ophthalmic Physiol Opt (Europe PMC MED/28211184, abstract only, Wiley). Pedestrians in black were recognised at 319.1 m with biomotion markers and 184.5 m with a vest alone. It replaced GOV.UK Highway Code Rule 3 because gov.uk reached 4 posts in this batch while siblings were writing. The paper is now in 2 posts (last-minute and this one), which is its cap.

Searches tried: findpapers "colour emotion associations black fear", "black clothing perception colour of clothing", "enclothed cognition clothing symbolic meaning", "pedestrian clothing conspicuity night recognition distance". Frontiers, SAGE and Elsevier hits were excluded.
