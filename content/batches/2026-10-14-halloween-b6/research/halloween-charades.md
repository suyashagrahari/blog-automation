## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched and read)
1. Britannica 1911 "Charade" (Wikisource) — "probably invented in France during the 18th century"; acted charade most popular form.
2. Britannica 1911 "Crambo" (Wikisource) — Dumb Crambo: meaning expressed "by dumb show", rhyme as clue.
3. Thackeray, Vanity Fair ch. LI (Gutenberg #599) — "acting charades had come among us from France"; "First two syllables"; spoken lines in scenes; Agamemnon.
4. Ćwiek, Fuchs, Pouw, Kadavá 2026, Behavior Research Methods (Europe PMC PMC13263253, CC BY) — 71 dyads, 84 concepts; ~45% gesture, ~46% combined, ~12% vocal; 32% overall.
5. Ortega & Özyürek 2020, Behavior Research Methods (PMC7005091, CC BY) — 20 adults, 109 concepts, 8% passes (444/5,440); acting/drawing/personification mapping.
6. Żywiczyński, Wacewicz & Lister 2021, Phil Trans R Soc B (PMC8059511, CC BY) — charades as pantomime emerging when language is blocked; actor stands.
Rejected: PLOS ONE gesture-memory paper (journal FULL); Frontiers pantomime papers (banned publisher); enactment-effect MDPI paper (megajournal); Wikipedia "Charades" (cap pressure; used only for orientation, not cited).
