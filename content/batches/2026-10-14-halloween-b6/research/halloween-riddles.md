## Phase 2: gap analysis
- **Table stakes:** many riddles with answers, split by kids and harder.
- **Gap:** none of the five pages (a) sorts riddles by age with a hint plan, (b) says anything about why solving feels good or how to hint, (c) mentions the history of riddles (no Exeter Book, no Old English), or (d) cites anything. Most "riddles" on the SERP are pun jokes that cannot be worked out.
- **Stale data:** none cited by competitors.
- **Fan-out:** what is a Halloween riddle; easy riddles for kids; riddles with answers; riddles for adults/teens; how to give a hint; why riddles are fun; oldest riddles; sending a riddle; party length.
- **Angle:** wins by being the only post that pairs 30 original, age-tiered riddles with an evidence-based hint method and a sourced Exeter Book history, using the 48.3% phone-open share and 11.1 views per page to justify putting each answer right after its riddle.

## Phase 3: sources (all fetched)
1. Mercier, Garsmeur & Mercier 2025, accepted manuscript for *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts* (APA), PsyArXiv arn2h_v2. Full PDF read: 43 cultures in eHRAF; 18/60 PSF societies; Study 1 n=197; +25% chance of choosing another problem; insight predicts willingness to share.
2. Bieth et al. 2024, *Communications Psychology* (Nature, CC BY), PMC11332086. Full text read: 15.6% vs 34% solve rate; 210 s vs 75 s.
3. Danek et al. 2013, *Psychological Research* (Springer). **Abstract only** (paywall): 64.4% vs 52.4% recall after 14 days, n=50.
4. Higl 2017, *American Journal of Play* 9(3), ERIC full text EJ1153368. Full PDF read: no solutions in the manuscript; Riddle 1's "hero" invitation; Niles traces nine answers for Riddle 74.
5. The Riddle Ages (AHRC; University of Birmingham), Exeter Book about page, plus the Riddle 47 post. Facts: ~95 riddles among ~130 poems; late 10th century; Leofric 1072; no solutions.

Rejected: Journal of Intelligence papers (MDPI; batch has one MDPI slot left), Frontiers (banned), J Exp Child Psychol 2020 riddle development (Elsevier, banned), Zipke riddle-reading papers (spent in b1), Klein/Mahony child-humour papers (spent by knock-knock). Searched "riddles language learning vocabulary children" and found no open, non-banned riddle-literacy paper. The literacy angle is therefore left out rather than padded.
