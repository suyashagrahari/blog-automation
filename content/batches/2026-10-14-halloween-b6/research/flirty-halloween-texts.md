## Phase 3: sources (all fetched)
1. **Pew Research Center, Couples, the Internet, and Social Media** (2014-02-11). Full page read.
   - 21% of partnered adults felt closer because of online or text exchanges.
   - 41% of 18-29s said the same.
   - 25% have texted their partner while both were at home.
2. **Gibson, Flirting and winking in Tinder chats**, Internet Pragmatics (2024-02-15, CC BY). Full text from UCL Discovery.
   - 19 volunteers in Spain and Denmark, data from 2021.
   - Winks were 6% of emoji in the Spanish data and a little over 12% in the Danish data.
   - The paper finds four sources of wink ambiguity.
3. **Dubé, Gesselman et al., Beyond words**, PLOS ONE (2024-12-06). Full text read.
   - 320 adults.
   - No significant gender difference in emoji sent to romantic partners.
   - Avoidant attachment went with fewer emoji to partners.
4. **Steinebach, Stein & Schnell**, BMC Psychology (2025-02-21). Abstract and methods read.
   - 51 couples.
   - Partners showed tracking accuracy and an assumed-similarity bias.
   - Couples who messaged more judged arousal more accurately.
5. **Gesselman, Ta & Garcia, Worth a thousand interpersonal words**, PLOS ONE (2019-08-15). Full text read.
   - 5,327 singles.
   - 37.6% never used emoji with potential dates; 28.2% used them regularly.
   - 53% gave "more personality" as their reason.
   - Emoji use was associated with more first dates. The study is correlational.
