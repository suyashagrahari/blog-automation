## Corrections writers made to the brief / prompts / the SERP
- **Bhoot Chaturdashi 2026 is 7 Nov** (Narak Chaturdashi 8 Nov, Kolkata); in 2027 it falls on the same day as Kali Puja (28 Oct). **b3 `halloween-in-india` says 8 Nov 2026 — needs a fix.**
- Ghostface: Fun World holds TM 4,256,208 but lost *Fun World v. Rozier* (2021, fair use); "Fun World owns the mask" is overstated.
- No dated record of Celts disguising from spirits exists; costume history runs mumming (London ban 1418) → guising (1488) → 1930s retail.
- Knock-knock: 1936 was the craze; the exchange is in Jonson (c.1597) and a 1929 game. "Shakespeare invented it" is a myth.
- "You've been booed": earliest record is a 2005 About.com page (not "1990s Midwest"); the rule was 2 days, not 24 hours.
- Samhuinn is on Calton Hill, not the Royal Mile; guising also ran at Christmas/Hogmanay; no dated record of treacle scones.
- Samhain: the Tlachtga druid fire first appears in Keating c.1634; Scottish Gaelic "November" is "an t-Samhain".
- Teal Pumpkin: FARE never dates 2012 or calls teal "official"; origin dates from a 2014 KWFA interview.
- Mexico City Día de Muertos parade began 2016 (post-*Spectre*); children's ofrenda days are 30–31 Oct in fieldwork.
- Philippines: 2027 holidays are out (Proc. 1427, 8 Sep 2026) and contradict themselves on 2 Nov; pangangaluluwa is an All Souls' custom.
- Germany: Burg Frankenstein's Halloween event left Darmstadt after 2023 (now Burg Königstein); Reformationstag is a holiday in 9 states (the #1 result says 5); Bavaria has no outright All Saints' dance ban.
- Vogue 1926 prints "The Chanel 'Ford'", never "little black dress".
- Emily Post 1922 lists only 8 anniversary milestones; year-by-year gift lists are later.
- 31 Oct is not the cross-quarter midpoint (6 Nov 2026 / 7 Nov 2027).
- Black-cat shelter holds: shelters *once* held them back (HSUS) — refines the b5 note.
- Halloween is not the "2nd biggest commercial holiday" (NRF's own figures put Mother's Day, Valentine's and Easter higher); spending per person +137% nominal but +41% real since 2005.
- "I have love in me…" is from the 1994 Branagh film, not Mary Shelley; the "Romeo and Juliet effect" failed replication (2014).
- *Night of the Living Dead* dialogue never says "zombie" (the screenplay does once). Pictionary's mark belongs to Pictionary Incorporated, not Mattel. The CROWN Act House vote was 18 Mar 2022.
- No study shows eating dinner before trick-or-treating cuts candy intake; no open source says activated charcoal interferes with medication (cocktails left charcoal and dry ice out).
