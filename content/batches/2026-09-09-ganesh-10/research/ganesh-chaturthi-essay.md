## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes (must be present or the page reads incomplete):**
essay structure; who Ganesha is; why the festival is celebrated; the rituals
(sthapana, aarti, modak, visarjan); model paragraphs at 100/200/500 words; a closing
"values" line; eco-friendly visarjan.

**The gap — four things none of the five do:**

1. **The history is wrong in all five, in the same way.** Every page implies Tilak
   *invented* Ganesh Chaturthi. The scholarship says the opposite: collective, street-
   processional Ganpati worship is documented from the Peshwa period, when Ganpati was the
   *kuladevatā* of the ruling Chitpavan Brahmins, and the nineteenth-century change was a
   *popular revival* given a deliberately **public** character (Larios 2018).
2. **Nobody explains "ten days" arithmetically.** Bhadrapada shukla *chaturthi* (4th tithi)
   to Anant Chaturdashi (14th tithi) — Drik Panchang calls Anant Chaturdashi the 11th day
   from Chaturthi, which is why the festivity is described as ten days, and why household
   visarjan alternatives are 1½/3/5/7 — odd numbers.
3. **Nobody dates anything.** Not one of the five gives the 2026 date, and none cite a
   source a teacher could check.
4. **No Hindi model**, despite the 200-word Hindi query.

**Stale data:** not applicable — there is no data on any of the five pages to supersede.

**Unanswered questions the pages raise and drop:** why *public*? why *ten* days? what did
the British have to do with it? was it always ten days at home too?

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2/H3/FAQ targets:** what should a Ganesh Chaturthi essay contain ·
why is Ganesh Chaturthi celebrated · who started Ganesh Chaturthi · what did Tilak actually
do in 1893 · was Ganesh Chaturthi celebrated before Tilak · why ten days · when is Ganesh
Chaturthi 2026 · how do I write 200 words in Hindi · what facts can I cite in the essay.

**Angle (`batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only Ganesh Chaturthi essay page that
separates ancient Ganesh worship from the 1893 *sarvajanik* reinvention, cites open-access
scholarship and a colonial governor's own letter for it, and still hands the student model
paragraphs at 100/200/500 words in English and Hindi.
