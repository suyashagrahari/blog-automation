## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (must be present or the page looks incomplete): the 2026 date, a set of
actual Hindi quotes in Devanagari, some Ganpati Bappa Morya reference, WhatsApp/status framing.

**The gap.** Nobody answers the question a person actually has at 8 a.m. on 14 September:
*which of these do I send to my mother-in-law and which to my flatmate, and do I send it in
Devanagari or in Roman letters?* Every ranking page is a list; none is a decision. Nobody gives
a transliteration, so a reader who speaks Hindi but reads only Roman script cannot use any of
these pages. Nobody gives a meaning, so a diaspora reader cannot check what they are sending.
Nobody mentions the pronoun system, which is the single thing that makes a Hindi greeting land
or misfire.

**Stale data.** zalkari.com is year-stamped 2025. infinitylearn's FAQ answers 2026 dates but
its quote body is undated.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped by the top pages.** Whether "Ganesh Chaturthi
mubarak" is acceptable; why some Devanagari arrives broken on a phone; whether the visarjan
chant is right on day one.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s.**

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that gives each Hindi Ganesh Chaturthi quote in
Devanagari, Roman transliteration and English gloss and then tells you which register
(आप / तुम / तू, shloka or Hinglish) fits which recipient — backed by transliteration and
politeness research plus SubhSandesh's platform-wide 49.6% mobile-open rate across 45,656
views.

**Deliberate divergence from the sibling post.** `ganesh-chaturthi-wishes-in-marathi` covers
Marathi sociolinguistics and already has an H2 on "Devanagari or Roman letters". This post is
structured around **register and audience** instead — the pronoun system, the Hinglish
code-mixing trend and the "not a forward" status section are Hindi-specific and appear in no
sibling. No source is shared with it.
