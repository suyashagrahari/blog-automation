## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all five cover, so the post must too): the festival name and its "remover of
obstacles" meaning; short lines usable on WhatsApp; wishes segmented by recipient (family,
friends, colleagues); the "Ganpati Bappa Morya" phrase; quotes usable as captions.

**The gap.** Not one of the five pages treats a wish as something a person *receives*. They are
supply-side lists: 101, 150+, 202+ lines to copy. None asks whether the recipient can tell the
line was forwarded, none explains why the same eleven lines arrive from nine people every 14
September, and none measures anything. The reception side of the greeting is completely
unoccupied territory on this SERP.

**Stale data.** There is no data on any of the five pages to supersede — which is itself the
finding. Only giva.co states the 2026 date.

**Unanswered questions the pages raise and drop:** is it rude to forward a wish? Do you send on
day one or across all eleven days? What do you write to a boss without sounding like a mailer?
What do you send to someone who could not come home for the festival?

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s and FAQs:** what is a Ganesh Chaturthi wish · when to send it in
2026 · Happy Ganesh Chaturthi vs Ganpati Bappa Morya · wish for parents / friends / boss / a
person who is away · Ganesh Chaturthi quotes for a caption · is a forwarded wish acceptable ·
which format gets a reply · what to send on visarjan day.

**Angle (one sentence, recorded as `batchMeta.angle`):**
*Wins by being the only post on this SERP that treats a Ganesh Chaturthi wish as something the
recipient receives rather than a list to copy — pricing each format by what it costs to send and
what the recipient can tell, against SubhSandesh's platform-wide 49.6% mobile-open rate and 11.8
views per page.*

**Deliberate scope limits (anti-cannibalisation):** Marathi wishes (post 5), Hindi quotes
(post 6), WhatsApp/Insta status (post 4), images (post 3), the date explainer (post 2), aarti
(post 8) and visarjan (post 10) are siblings. This post mentions language choice and the
visarjan date in one sentence each and does not build a section on any of them.
