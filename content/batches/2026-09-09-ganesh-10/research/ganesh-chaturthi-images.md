## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all five cover, so the post must too): the 2026 date; the sub-types people mean
by "images" (stock art, name-personalised card, own-mandap photo, banner/poster); WhatsApp and
Instagram as destinations; the "with name" personalisation idea.

**The gap.** Every competing page treats the image as the end state. None of them treats it as a
*delivery format with measurable properties*. Nobody says that most images on WhatsApp are
forwarded rather than made, that the platform resizes and re-encodes the file and strips its
metadata, or that a name typed into a JPEG cannot be corrected after sending. Nobody names the
cases where an image is genuinely the right call — they simply assume it always is.

**Stale data.** There is none to supersede: the competing pages cite no third-party data at all.
The freshest thing on any of them is their own template count.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** why photos arrive blurry; what size to export for a
status versus a banner; whether a free stock file may be used on a business poster; what to do
when you have already sent the image with the wrong name.

**Fan-out sub-queries → became H2s / FAQs:** what counts as a Ganesh Chaturthi image · which
format for which recipient · why images get forwarded rather than made · what WhatsApp does to a
photo on upload · image vs shareable page · when an image beats a page · status and banner sizes
· sending without quality loss.

**Angle (→ `batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only page that treats a Ganesh Chaturthi image
as a delivery format with measurable limits — 54.5% of WhatsApp image messages are forwarded not
composed, uploads are resized, recompressed and stripped of metadata — set against SubhSandesh's
platform-wide 49.6% phone-open rate and 96.1% publish rate.

Exact claims taken, and nothing beyond them:

- **Melo 2024:** dataset of 9.78M messages from 1,101 public Brazilian political groups; 1,361,021
  image messages; 26.13% of all messages forwarded; by media type the forwarded share is documents
  75.6%, videos 65%, **images 54.5%**, audio 18.2%; text and stickers negligible; individual images
  recur "hundreds or even over a thousand" times; the authors attribute the pattern to the effort
  of producing media versus forwarding it. Brazilian, not Indian — flagged as such in the body.
- **Chauchard & Garimella 2022:** ~36,000 images hand-coded from 533 closed partisan threads across
  Uttar Pradesh over nine months; the largest share is phatic or entertainment content rather than
  partisan, and much of it is religion-related.
- **Garimella & Eckles 2020:** 1.6M images from >5,000 Indian public political groups, >5M messages,
  >250,000 users, Oct 2018–Jun 2019; **old images taken out of context ≈34%** of the labelled
  misinformation image set. (The article gives image misinformation prevalence as 13% in the essay
  summary and "around 10%" in the abstract; that figure is therefore **not used**.)
- **Pasquini 2021:** sharing platforms including WhatsApp apply **resizing and JPEG recompression**
  on upload under platform-specific rules, and **usually strip optional metadata fields** such as
  acquisition time, GPS coordinates and acquisition device.

**Generic context statistics: zero.** No PIB, TRAI, Census or MEA figure appears.

**Swap test:** none of the four could sit unchanged in a sibling post. Post 1 is CMC/forwarding as
*greeting behaviour*, post 4 is ephemeral status posting, post 5 is Marathi sociolinguistics, post 2
is calendrics — all four sources here are specifically about *images as files that circulate*.

**Not found, and worth recording:** no peer-reviewed study of festival-greeting image sharing in
India was located. Searched `good morning images WhatsApp India`, `festival greetings social media
India sharing`, `religious images WhatsApp devotional sharing`, `darshan digital devotional images
visual culture`, `image macro forwarded greeting phatic communication` across OpenAlex, DOAJ-indexed
journals and general web search. The closest hits — Monteiro's *'Welcome to selfiestan'* (Media,
Culture & Society, 2020) and the *WhatsApp iconology* paper (JEMS, 2023) — are both behind
publisher bot-walls and were not readable, so neither is cited. The Chauchard & Garimella coding
of religion-related images is the nearest available substitute.
