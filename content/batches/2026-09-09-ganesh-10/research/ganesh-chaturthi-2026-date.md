## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes (all five cover these, so the post must too):**
date and day; madhyahna puja muhurat; the visarjan / Anant Chaturdashi date; the shorter
1.5 / 3 / 5 / 7-day observances; the moon-sighting prohibition; a one-line "why the date
changes" gesture.

**The gap — nobody shows the arithmetic.**
Every page states the date. Not one page states *the rule that produces it*. Archyam has
a heading literally called "Why the Date Changes Every Year" and answers it with "it
follows the lunar calendar". None of the five:

1. names the **madhyahna-vyapini** selection rule (Ganesh Chaturthi is fixed by the tithi
   being current at **midday**, not at sunrise — unusual, and the reason the 2026 festival
   lands on a day whose tithi has not even begun at dawn);
2. gives the **tithi begin/end times** and derives anything from them (Chaturthi 2026 runs
   07:06 on 14 Sep to 07:44 on 15 Sep — 24 h 38 min, longer than a solar day);
3. explains the **+18-day jump** from 27 August 2025 to 14 September 2026, or names
   Adhika Jyeshtha 2026 (ended 15 June 2026) as the cause;
4. explains why the muhurat **differs by city** at all — the 1955 Calendar Reform
   Committee found almanac-makers differing by up to five hours and the same festival
   being fixed on two successive days in one city;
5. carries a single number about how people actually coordinate the day remotely.

**Stale data:** none of the five cites dated research at all; there is nothing to
supersede, only an absence to fill.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** "why does it move"; "is it 10 days or 11";
"which city's muhurat applies to me"; "how many days to go".

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:**
- what fixes the date of Ganesh Chaturthi → H2 2

**Angle (→ `batchMeta.angle`):**
Wins by being the only post that shows the arithmetic behind 14 September 2026 — the
