## HowTo steps
1. **Open the Template** — Go to the Happy Diwali page on SubhSandesh and press Create.
2. **Choose Who It's For** — Pick your love, family or a friend; the default wishes, blessing and reply follow the choice.
3. **Write the Wishes** — Add both names, the tag on the diya, a wish for each diya and the letter sealed in the liffafa — or let the AI write it from a short story.
4. **Add Photos and Music** — Upload up to six photos for the sky lanterns, then paste a YouTube song or pick a Diwali favourite.
5. **Finish the Night** — Set the initials for the rangoli, the word for the sparkler, their favourite sweet and your blessing.
6. **Publish and Send** — Press Save to preview, Publish, then send the private link on Diwali evening.

## FAQPage (verbatim)

**Q: How do I send an interactive Diwali card?**
Open the Happy Diwali template, press Create and add their name and yours, a letter, up to six photos and a wish for each diya — or describe the two of you in a few lines and let the AI write it for you. Press Save to preview, then Publish and send the private link on WhatsApp, Instagram or by text. It opens in any phone or laptop browser with no app and no account.

**Q: What happens when they open it?**
They find a diya waiting at their door with a little tag from you and light it. Then they light a row of diyas, each one lifting one of your wishes; colour a rangoli until your initials glow in the middle; write their name in the air with a sparkler; light paper sky lanterns that hold your photos; light crackers in a playground of their own (anar, chakri, rockets, phuljhadi) until the sky fills with fireworks; open your letter from a red-and-gold shagun liffafa into warm diya light; and open a box of mithai onto a Shubh Deepavali card signed by you.

**Q: Can I send it to family or a friend instead of a partner?**
Yes. Choose who it is for — your love, family or a friend — and the default wishes, the blessing card and the reply change with it. Every line stays editable.

**Q: Can I add a Diwali song?**
Yes. Paste a YouTube link (with an optional start time) and only the sound plays while they explore, or pick a Diwali favourite such as Happy Diwali, Aayee Hai Diwali or Deepavali Manayi Suhani. You can also keep the soft built-in festive melody. Music starts only after they light the first diya.

**Q: Does it work on any phone?**
Yes. Every scene fits one screen on phones, tablets and laptops, and every interaction works with a simple tap. Motion is reduced automatically for anyone whose phone asks for less motion, and the crackers and fireworks are drawn by the page, so there is no noise or smoke.

**Q: When is Diwali 2026?**
Diwali (Lakshmi Puja) falls on Sunday, 8 November 2026. Good moments to send the link are that evening, as the first diyas are lit, or at midnight going into the day; for someone far away, open it together on a video call. The page works on any date.

**Q: Can they keep it?**
Yes. The Shubh Deepavali card at the end can be saved as a 1080×1920 image (Instagram Story size), and the link keeps working, so they can open it again any evening.

**Q: Can I lock the page with a password or edit it later?**
Yes to both. You can set an optional password when you publish, and you can edit and republish the page any time; the same link always shows the latest version.
