## Phase 2 — gap
- Table stakes: ideas grouped for tall men, women and kids; fit problems (short sleeves, hems, jumpsuits).
- Gap: nobody gives headroom per headpiece, door heights, or any research; no page uses characters whose height is stated in a primary text; nobody gives numbers for the fit gap.
- Angle: wins by measuring each costume's headroom against the ADA 78-inch door-closer allowance and the Duck! study, on public-domain characters whose height is in the text.
- Fan-out: what suits tall people / tall women / tall kids; how to lengthen a costume; will a headpiece clear doors; was Frankenstein's creature eight feet; how to photograph a tall costume.

## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched)
1. NCD-RisC 2016, eLife — europepmc PMC4961475 (abstract + metadata via Europe PMC REST): 18.6 m participants; Dutch men >182.5 cm; South Korean women +20.2 cm.
2. Schmidt & Talbot 2025, PLOS Mental Health — europepmc PMC12798471 (full text): taller perceived as more persuasive and leaders; femininity stereotyped as smaller stature; dissatisfaction includes "too tall".
3. Stefanucci & Geuss 2010, Atten Percept Psychophys — europepmc MED/20601715 (abstract only; PMC manuscript bot-walled): helmet/shoes → larger safety margin, ducked at shorter heights.
4. US Access Board, 2010 ADA Standards 307.4 — access-board.gov/ada (full text): 80 in vertical clearance; door closers/stops 78 in minimum.
5. Shelley 1818 first edition — gutenberg #41445: "about eight feet in height"; yellow skin, lustrous black flowing hair, straight black lips.
6. Irving 1820 — gutenberg #41: "tall, but exceedingly lank … hands that dangled a mile out of his sleeves".
Also verified, not linked: Andersen, The Snow Queen (gutenberg #27200) "a lady, tall and white" — dropped as a costume because b3/b5 own it.
