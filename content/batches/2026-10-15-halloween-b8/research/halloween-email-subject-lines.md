## Phase 2 — gap
- Table stakes: categorised lists, urgency lines, emoji, puns, length under ~50 chars, A/B mention, spam-trigger warning.
- Gap: no page ties lines to dated sends (teaser / launch / last chance / 1 Nov) for 2026 and 2027; no page pairs every line with a preheader or prints character counts; no page sources the legal rules (CAN-SPAM statute, UK PECR, Gmail sender requirements); no page cites research — only vendor emoji open-rate claims; no industry split for food/services/B2B with B2B restraint.
- Stale/weak data: vendor "spider emoji 23% open rate" claims, undated zippia stat.
- Fan-out: what makes a good line; lines by stage; when to send; lines by industry; how long / emoji; spam & legal; A/B testing; preheader; post-Halloween email.
- Angle: wins by being the only post that maps 44 original lines to four dated sends with exact character counts and preheaders, backed by two field studies and the actual CAN-SPAM, PECR and Gmail rules, framed by SubhSandesh's 48.3% phone-open rate.

## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched)
1. Sahni, Wheeler & Chintagunta — ideas.repec.org/p/ecl/stabus/3409.html (abstract only; Marketing Science 2018 version paywalled): name in subject line +20% opens, +31% leads, −17% unsubscribes.
2. Janke 2014, Evidence Based Library and Information Practice (full text read; CC BY-NC-SA, link only): week-2 response 10.35% vs 5.6% when the reminder subject named the prize; observational.
3. 15 U.S.C. § 7704 via law.cornell.edu — deceptive subject heading ban; 10 business days opt-out; postal address. (ftc.gov 404 to fetcher; ecfr bot wall.)
4. ICO, Electronic mail marketing (PECR reg 22) — consent, soft opt-in, companies; guidance under review after DUAA.
5. Gmail Email sender guidelines — no fake Re:/Fwd:, no deceptive emoji, spam rate <0.10% / never 0.30%, one-click unsubscribe for 5,000+/day since 1 Feb 2024.
6. Unicode emoji FAQ (cap-exempt) — colour vs black-and-white presentation varies by platform.

Rejected: Paulo et al. 2022 (Elsevier, banned); Behav Sci 2025 emoji review (MDPI full); Cohen-Vernik & Pettiette 2023 SSRN emoji paper (403, no abstract available); Computers in Human Behavior 2016 (Elsevier).
