## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched, full text read)
1. Dunbar, Frangou, Grainger & Pearce 2021, PLOS ONE, PMC8362988 (via europepmc.org, exempt). Laughter was about 4× more likely in a group of four than alone, during a ~20-min comedy clip. Laughter raised pain thresholds and was associated with bonding to strangers (at least in women), with no reliable effect on generosity. This is a different paper from the party-ideas sibling's Dunbar 2022 and 2017.
2. Sci Rep 2024-12-28, PMC11680777, Mafia game and LLM deceiver detection. Says the game was "developed by Dimitry Davidoff in 1986".
3. J Autism Dev Disord, online 2024-07-05, PMC12476318, board games and autistic people. Surveyed >1,600 gamers; found a "social lubrication effect of games" and "difficulties with deception".
4. dúchas.ie Schools' Collection (National Folklore Collection, UCD), Twomilehouse, Co. Kildare, "Hallow Eve - Games": apples in a tub lifted with the mouth, a barmbrack with a ring, and about 934 Halloween entries in the topic index.
5. dúchas.ie, Valleymount, Co. Wicklow, "Festival Customs": a blindfolded player catches an apple from spinning crossed poles, and the ring-finder "is said to be married soon".
