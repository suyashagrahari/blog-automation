## Phase 3: sources (all fetched as full-text XML via the Europe PMC REST API; europepmc.org is cap-exempt)

1. Trott, Walker, Taylor & Coulson 2025, Cognitive Science 49(5) e70066, PMC12093450. Theories stress incongruity detection and resolution. Humans and LLMs both misclassify non-jokes with surprising endings as jokes. Licence CC BY-NC-ND, so the post cites it only.
2. Fan et al. 2025 (online 2024-09-16), PsyCh Journal 14(1):37–50, PMC11787884. Covers benign violation theory (BVT) across 3 studies (n=350/172/172). When a joke involves a moral violation and its target is socially close, appreciation and sharing drop. CC BY.
3. Palmann & Miller 2025, Humor 38(4):643–658, PMC12487772. Uses the SemEval-2017 dataset of 4,030 texts. Lower phonological distance between pun and target goes with higher funniness on 2 of 4 measures. CC BY. It measures funniness, not groans.
4. Gorenz & Schwarz 2025, PLOS One 20(9) e0332911, PMC12449007. Exp 1 (N=214): 66.4% picked the clear-audio comedian as funnier, against 33.6% for the noisy recording. Exp 2: 69.4% against 30.6%. CC BY.
