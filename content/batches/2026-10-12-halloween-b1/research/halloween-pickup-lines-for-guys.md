## Phase 3: sources (all fetched and verified, all open access)
1. **Kreager, Cavanagh, Yen & Yu 2014**, *J Marriage Fam* 76(2):387–410, PMC4043335 (full text via PMC). The data cover N = 8,259 men and 6,274 women over 6 months. Women were 4× less likely to send messages than men. Women who initiate connect with more desirable partners. There were 142,444 messages initiated by men vs 34,960 by women. Only 3% of men's and 7% of women's sent messages reached more than 5 exchanges. Setting: a "mid-sized southwestern city".
2. **Topinkova & Diviak 2025**, *PLOS ONE* e0327477, PMC12286370 (full text). The Brno network had 624 users (20.4% women). Median indegree was 1 for men vs 18 for women. Women are advantaged by the gender ratio and their higher desirability.
3. **Albury, McCosker & Evers 2021**, *First Monday* 26(4), DOI 10.5210/fm.v26i4.11637 (full HTML read). It is qualitative; interviewees were aged 18–28, mean 24. Quote: "Failure and rejection are common and an almost expected experience of dating apps for young male users". Many felt "the odds are stacked against them".
4. **Rerick, Livingston & Singer 2025**, *Behavioral Sciences* 15(8):1101, PMC12382781 (full text, CC BY). The experiment had N = 180 heterosexual men. Men rated attractive women's ambiguous behaviour as showing higher sexual intent only when they themselves were the recipient. The authors note that "direct verbal communication could help to clarify intentions".
