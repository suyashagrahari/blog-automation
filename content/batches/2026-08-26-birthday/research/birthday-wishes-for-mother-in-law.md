## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all eight cover these; the post must not look incomplete without them):
example messages the reader can copy; a warm/affectionate register; a short/simple option;
some acknowledgement that not every in-law relationship is easy; a "heartfelt" variant.

**The gap — three things none of the eight address:**

1. **Time pressure.** Every page is built for a reader browsing a list. The measured behaviour
   is the opposite: median lead time **0 days**, **50.0%** written on the birthday itself,
   **12.6%** after it had passed. Nobody organises the answer by how much time is left.
2. **The audience.** A mother-in-law message is rarely a private two-person text. It gets
   forwarded, screenshotted into a family group, read aloud. A SubhSandesh birthday page is
   opened **11.2 times on average** — the message has readers, not a reader. Not one of the
   eight pages mentions this.
3. **The relationship is negotiated, not given.** The listicles treat "mother-in-law" as a
   warm slot to fill. The research on this specific relationship says the ideal is intense
   ("like her own") and the lived experience ranges from affection to not speaking. That is
   the actual reason the message is hard to write, and it is why 75 options do not help.

**Stale data:** none of the eight cites any data at all — not a survey, not a study, not a
date. There is nothing to supersede; the whole SERP is undated assertion. That is the
opening.

**Unanswered questions the pages raise and drop:** what to do when the relationship is
strained (all of them gesture at it, none gives a sentence you could actually send); whether
to post publicly or send privately; what a **son-in-law** writes, as opposed to a
daughter-in-law — completely absent from all eight.

**Fan-out sub-queries → became H2s or FAQs:**

| Sub-query | Where it landed |
|---|---|
| Why is this message hard to write? | H2 #1 |
| What do I write if I have no time? | H2 #2, H3 #1 |

**Angle (one sentence, recorded as `batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only page that
treats a mother-in-law birthday message as a short text with an audience and an unequal
relationship behind it — organised by how much time the sender has left — using the measured
96-character median, the 50.0% same-day rate and the 11.2 readers per page.

**Lane discipline.** Lane K (`birthday-wishes-for-sister-in-law`) owns kinship *terminology*.
This post therefore says nothing about what to call her — no *saas* / *Mummy ji* / *Aunty*
comparison, no address-vs-reference discussion. One line in the body defers the question
explicitly ("whatever you already call her in person is the right thing to write") and moves
on. Everything else stays on relationship quality.

**Cannibalisation.** No live mother-in-law or saas post. `birthday-wishes-for-bhabhi`,
`-mummy`, `-nani`, `-dadi` are live and are different relations. In this batch,
`birthday-wishes-for-nephew` organises by kin term and `2-line-birthday-wishes` owns length
as a topic — this post organises by **lead time**, which neither uses, and it treats the
96-character figure as a target rather than as its subject.

**No generic context statistic was used at all** — no PIB, TRAI, Census or MEA figure appears

### What each one actually says, verified

**1. Anukriti et al. (2020) — open.bu.edu/handle/2144/42271.** Primary survey of married
women aged 18–30 in Jaunpur district, Uttar Pradesh, 2018. Verbatim from the PDF: "Over 67
percent of the sample women live with the MIL"; a woman who co-resides with her
mother-in-law has "20 percent fewer close peers in her village" and "37 percent fewer close
peers outside her household"; besides her husband and mother-in-law "an average woman
interacts with 1.6 individuals" about things that matter to her. Used to establish that this
one relationship carries a disproportionate share of a young woman's social world.

**2. Allendorf (2015) — europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC4852487.** Semi-structured interviews
with 46 members of 22 families in one Indian village. Abstract states the ideal is that the
two are "like one's own daughter or mother", that in practice quality varies from
"affectionate, high quality relationships" to "hurtful exchanges and not speaking", and that
the pair are "simultaneously strangers and close family members". **Only the abstract was
readable.** PMC serves the author manuscript (NIHMS779887) behind a JS/reCAPTCHA gate, its
XML endpoint returns "the publisher of this article does not allow downloading of the full
text in XML form", and the SAGE version is closed (confirmed via Unpaywall). Note that the
NLM record for PMC4852487 carries an **incorrect DOI and volume** (Family Relations
55(5):588–600); the authoritative citation was taken from Crossref —
`10.1177/0192513X15590685`, Journal of Family Issues 38(15):2102–2127, online 2015-06-09.
The post quotes only what the abstract says.

**3. Gopalakrishnan et al. (2023) — europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13037867.** 200 newly
married women aged 18–25 cohabiting with their mothers-in-law, Nawalparasi district, Nepal,
four rounds at six-month intervals 2018–2020, 770 observations. A 1-unit rise in the
mother-in-law relationship quality score was associated with lower depressive symptom
severity (Coeff −0.019; 95% CI −0.030 to −0.008; p<0.001), and the combined 1-unit rise in
both spousal and mother-in-law relationship quality was larger than either alone (Coeff
−0.0843; 95% CI −0.117 to −0.051). **The inconvenient half, which the post states:** in the
logistic models the same score showed *no* significant association with binary probable
depression (AOR 0.917, not significant), and mean scores *declined* over the first three
rounds. Nepal, not India — the post says so.

**4. Pradhan & Mondal (2023) — BMC Women's Health.** DHS data for four South Asian
countries; India's round is NFHS-5, 2019–21. Table 2 (weighted): 9.27% of currently married
Indian women aged 15–49 live with husband and other family members **including** the
mother-in-law, plus 2.21% living with husband and/or unmarried children and the
mother-in-law — **11.48%, so roughly one in nine, co-reside**. Used for the opposite of the
obvious point: for about nine in ten, the birthday message is the contact, not an addition
to daily co-residence. Verified in Europe PMC's full text of the identical article because
`bmcwomenshealth.biomedcentral.com` returns a bot challenge to non-browser clients. The same
applies to both Europe PMC article pages, which are JavaScript shells — sources 2, 3 and 4
were all read through the Europe PMC REST full-text service and the NCBI E-utilities records
rather than at the URL the post links to. Only source 1 (open.bu.edu landing page plus PDF)
and source 5 (a direct PDF) were retrieved at the cited URL. That is recorded as the post's
one audit failure rather than smoothed over.

**5. Gudipaty (2018) — journalstudiesanthropology.ro.** Ethnographic content analysis of the
top ten Hindi serials of 2016 by BARC rating. Verified from the PDF: *Kyunki Saas Bhi Kabhi
Bahu Thi* ran "8 years and 8 months, amounting to 1,830 episodes"; in the 2000s the
saas–bahu sagas cast "the mother-in-law or the sister-in-law personifying 'evil'" against
"the 'good' obedient suffering daughter-in-law", and by the 2016 sample those plots had
"shifted to the daughters of the house". Used to date the villain script the reader is
writing against — the serials themselves moved on.
