### Verified facts, per source

**1 — ICAR / IJAS 2025** (full text read)
- "traditional flowers" defined as species grown for religious, cultural and ceremonial
  use, mostly loose; marigold named first, with jasmine, chrysanthemum and rose
- 2023–24: floriculture on 285,000 ha, producing **2,284,000 t loose flowers** vs
  **947,000 t cut flowers**
- post-harvest losses of traditional flowers **estimated at 30–40% in India** (abstract;
  the body separately cites 20–30% from Varun & Lakshmi 2018b — the post uses the
  abstract's own headline figure)
- marigold classed **moderately** ethylene-sensitive; rose, jasmine, tuberose, carnation
  **highly** sensitive
- optimal storage: marigold **8–12 °C**, rose 0–2, jasmine 4–7, chrysanthemum 0–2,
  carnation 0–2

**2 — Jergin 2021, TNAU** (full 203-page text read). This source carries the post's core.
- marigold "accounting for more than half of the nation's loose flower production"
- Tamil Nadu: marigold on **1,779 ha**, producing **31,777 t**; state loose flowers on
  34,227.98 ha producing 4,29,922.83 MT; TN ~25% of national flower production
- **Tamil respondents**: "Marigold and Nityakalyani garlands are used only for corpses in
  burial rituals"; and, in the same chapter, marigold at Valaikappu (7th-month baby
  shower, mother garlanded with tuberose, marigold and rose), Pongal *poo kolam*, Mattu
  Pongal cattle garlands, and Gruhapravesam puja
- **Telugu-speaking respondents in Tamil Nadu**: three wedding garlands of chrysanthemum,
  tuberose and **marigold**; marigold also at Ugadi and Bhogi
- **Malayali respondents**: funeral garlands of marigold and rose, petals of paneer rose,
  marigold and chrysanthemum scattered on the street during the procession — *and*
  Shivaratri garlands of marigold, tulsi and rose on Lord Shiva and the temple elephants;
  the Koodalumooduthooku temple function with nerium, marigold and paneer rose; Onam
  *pookolam* with marigold, ixora and bachelor's button

That last set is the whole argument: **one survey, one state, one year — marigold on the
corpse, on the bride, and on the deity.**

**3 — IJTK 2024, Fulaich** (abstract and article record read)
- Fulaich is celebrated in Kinnaur, Himachal Pradesh **between 10 August and 26 October**
- stated purposes: to express respect toward high-altitude wild flowers, **to pay homage to
  departed family members, and to celebrate a new birth**
- 47 plant species recorded (41 angiosperms, 6 gymnosperms), 38 genera, 20 families
- cited **only** for the fact that one documented Indian flower festival honours the dead
  and a birth together. It records no Tagetes and the post claims none from it.

**4 — Kannauj 2025** (abstract only)
- three named temples — Sheetala Chaukiya, Diyawan Mahadev, Markandeya Mahadev —
  "collectively disposing of several quintals of flowers daily"
- n = 500 respondents in the Kannauj perfume industry
- the full-text link served an HTML PDF-viewer wrapper, not extractable text. Recorded as
  abstract-only in `sources` and in the audit.

**5 — IJCBM 2015** (abstract only; remainder paywalled behind "click to purchase")
- one grower's marigold yield had fallen to **4–5 tonnes per acre**
- he received **₹2–3 per kg** before the NAIP intervention
- recorded as abstract-only. This is a single-farmer figure from 2015 and the post frames it
  as exactly that.
