## Ritual details — what is verified, and what is NOT

Everything the post states about the observance, with the line it appears on.

**Verified, and cited in the post:**

| Claim in the post | Source |
|---|---|
| Fast kept by married Hindu women for their husband's health and long life | Maloney 2025, p. 6 |
| No food and no water from sunrise to moonrise | Maloney 2025, p. 6 |
| "not a mainstream ritual across all regions of India" (quoted) | Maloney 2025, p. 6 |
| Observed chiefly among Hindu women from north India | Maloney 2025 (consent/description of scope) |
| The risen moon is viewed through a sieve, then the gaze turns to the husband | Maloney 2025, pp. 6–7 |
| Fast broken "when the husband offers water and food to his wife" (quoted) | Maloney 2025, p. 7 |
| Preparations begin several days earlier, in gatherings that may include an exchange of gifts | Maloney 2025, p. 6 |
| Participant quote on the husband knowing "he needs to get me something like a gift, or many" | Maloney 2025, p. 31 |
| Participants used smartphones to track moonrise times and sent photographs to relatives abroad | Maloney 2025 (adaptations section) |
| IMD's *Indian Astronomical Ephemeris* ≈500 pages, Part III sunrise/sunset/moonrise/moonset tables, Part VI Indian Calendar, compiled for users including panchang makers | IMD page, fetched |

**NOT verified — deliberately left out of the post. A reviewer should check nothing here,
because nothing here is asserted:**

1. **`sargi` / `sargai`** — the pre-dawn meal, traditionally sent by the mother-in-law. Not in
   Maloney (the words do not appear in the thesis at all), and no unspent primary or scholarly
   source for it was reachable. **Omitted entirely** from body, FAQs and takeaways. The brief
   named it; I would not state it without a source.
2. **A date for any year.** No 2026 or 2027 date is stated anywhere. Not verified from a primary
   source, and `drikpanchang.com` is a closed domain.
3. **A moonrise time for any city or year.** Not stated. The post says it varies by city and
   tells the reader to check locally, which is what the brief authorises.
4. **The state list** (Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Rajasthan, UP, Himachal, MP). The brief asserts
   this spread; Maloney supports only "Hindu women from north India" and "certain communities".
   The post therefore says **"chiefly among Hindu women from north India"** and no more. No
   states are named as a factual claim.
5. **Fast duration in hours.** Sunrise-to-moonrise is roughly 13–14 hours in north India in
   October, but that is my arithmetic, not a source. **Not stated.**
6. **"Some couples now fast together."** Stated in the body and FAQ 8 as a *variation* that
   exists and depends on the family, not as a sourced fact with a prevalence. Maloney documents
   adaptation of the fast generally but does not document husbands fasting. If a reviewer wants
   this tightened further, the sentence to cut is "some couples now fast together" in the second
   paragraph of the definition H2 and the second sentence of FAQ 8.
7. **The traditional narrative giving the husband no part to play.** Verified in Gold 2015, which
   I did not cite (see above). Because it is uncited, the post **does not state it**, even though
   it is the sharpest academic support for the angle. This is the single biggest thing the spent
   URL cost the post.

The observance is treated respectfully throughout: no mockery, no framing of one family's
practice as the correct one, and the "practice varies by family… none of those is a correction of
the others" sentence is deliberate.
