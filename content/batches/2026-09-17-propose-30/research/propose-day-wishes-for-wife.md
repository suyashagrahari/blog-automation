## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** — the date, a block of wishes, a husband section, a "what is Propose
Day" definition, short quotable lines. All present here.

**The gap.** Married readers. Every incumbent treats a wife as a sub-heading inside a
page built for a new couple, so the advice is imported unchanged: grandeur, suspense,
ask clearly. None of that is what re-affirmation needs. Nobody writes the sentence
*you are not asking a question, she answered it years ago*, and nobody writes for the
constraint that follows — a working Monday, a shared phone, a wife who would be
embarrassed by a public gesture.

**Stale data / wrong data.** Four ranking incumbents carry **2025** in the title. More
seriously, an incumbent publishes "7 Feb Propose Day", which is Rose Day. The 2027
weekday was verified against the system calendar before drafting: Rose Day **Sunday
7 Feb 2027**, Propose Day **Monday 8 Feb 2027**, Valentine's Day **Sunday 14 Feb 2027**.
2026's Propose Day fell on a Sunday; the 2027 Monday genuinely narrows the window and
no incumbent prints that.

**Unanswered questions** the incumbents raise and drop: does a wife need a gift, is it
odd to propose to someone already married, what to do when she dislikes fuss, whether
any of this repairs a bad stretch.

**Fan-out sub-queries** → became H2s and FAQs: when is Propose Day 2027 · what is a
Propose Day wish for a wife · short propose day quotes for wife · propose day wishes
for husband · page or text · privacy on a shared phone · will a message fix things.

**Angle.** Wins by being the only Propose Day wishes page written for a reader who is
already married — re-affirmation rather than a question — using SubhSandesh's
platform-wide finding that the plain "I love you" page outbuilds every grander
template, 1,138 of 4,567 pages and 24.9% of everything created across 16 page types.

Sibling boundary respected: `propose day wishes for boyfriend` (unmarried, girlfriend →
boyfriend) is another agent's page. This one stays on married couples throughout.

### Access and cap notes

- **No publisher page loaded from this machine.** `nature.com` and
  `bmcpsychology.biomedcentral.com` return a bot challenge; `mdpi.com` returns HTTP 403.
  Anticipated by the BRIEF. Every claim was verified against the article record served
  by the **Europe PMC open-access REST API** (`PMC11387639`, `PMC9271060`, `PMC13072604`,
  `PMC12255064`, `PMC13405681`) — the same deposited article. **Abstract-level claims
  only; no full text was read.** Recorded as a failure on the "fetched and verified"
  checklist item rather than papered over.
- **Europe PMC URLs are deliberately not cited.** `europepmc.org` is a different host
  from `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` but the same publisher, and PMC is at the 3-post cap.
  Using Europe PMC to dodge that cap would be a dodge, so the publisher URL is cited.
- `capcheck.mjs` at time of writing: `nature.com` 1 post (this makes 2, under the 3
  cap); `bmcpsychology.biomedcentral.com` and `mdpi.com` 0 posts. None of the five URLs
  appears in the 54 banned wave-1 citations.
- **Discarded on cap grounds:** *Religious faith, gratitude, conflict resolution styles,
  and romantic love* (Frontiers in Sociology, 2025) and *Building happier bonds:
  gratitude as a mediator between dyadic coping and relationship satisfaction*
  (Frontiers in Psychology, 2024) — `frontiersin.org` is at 3 posts.
- **Discarded on access grounds:** Renny et al., *Industrial Psychiatry Journal*,
  19 Jan 2026 (106 married couples, urban India) — reachable only via `doi.org` (one
  slot left) or `europepmc.org` (capped); `journals.lww.com` returns 403 and the URL
  could not be confirmed.
- **Discarded, not open access:** Lambert & Fincham, "Expressing gratitude to a partner
  leads to more relationship maintenance behavior", *Emotion* 2011, and Gordon et al.,
  "To have and to hold", *JPSP* 2012 — Unpaywall reports no OA location for either, and
