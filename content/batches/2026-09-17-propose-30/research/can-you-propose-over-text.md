## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes (all/most pages carry these — the post must not omit them):**
- A yes/no verdict near the top.
- Timing: send when they have room to reply.
- One message, not five trickled across ten minutes.
- End on an unambiguous question.
- "Don't open with *can we talk*."
- Some acknowledgement that distance/nerves can make text the right call.

**The gap — what nobody on this SERP does:**
1. **Nobody explains *why* a lean channel changes how a heavy message lands.** Every page
   asserts "text removes tone". None cites a single study, and none names media richness.
2. **Nobody distinguishes a positive ask from bad news.** This is the sharpest gap.
   There is 2025 experimental evidence that leaner channels *outperform* face-to-face —
   but only for negative, one-way messages, via emotional detachment. A proposal is the
   exact inverse. Every page that reaches for "digital is fine, people do it all the
   time" is quietly borrowing an argument that belongs to breakups, not proposals.
3. **Nobody separates the two meanings of "propose".** Indian/South Asian English means
   "will you be my girlfriend"; British/American English means marriage. The advice is
   different and the product recommendation is different.
4. **Nobody talks about the artefact.** A proposal is remembered and retold. A chat
   bubble screenshots badly. No page asks what the recipient will show people.
5. **No first-party behavioural data anywhere on the SERP.**

**Stale data:** the Refinery29 piece is 2016; the Infobip survey is December 2022 and is
the freshest number any incumbent carries. The 2025 *Public Relations Review* experiments
supersede the entire "richer is always better" framing that the SERP assumes.

### 1. Tkalac Verčič, A., Verčič, D. & Gerdina, S. (2025) — the load-bearing one

*"Situational dependence of internal communication channels"*, **Public Relations Review**,
published 8 July 2025. CC BY-NC. Open-access copy:
`https://repozitorij.uni-lj.si/IzpisGradiva.php?id=175009` (ScienceDirect returns 403;
**the repository PDF was downloaded and read in full**).

What it actually says: three experiments run in spring 2024 inside a large
telecommunications company, final sample 96 employees (36 / 12 / 48), grounded in Media
Richness Theory and the media affordances perspective. With an emotionally neutral,
two-way message, satisfaction was comparable face-to-face and on MS Teams with video, but
participants rated *quality of communication with others* higher face-to-face. With a
negative, one-way message the ordering inverted: information retention ran
`in-person < text-only ≈ audio-only < video`, which the authors attribute to face-to-face
raising emotional intensity, raising cognitive load, costing recall. Their framing, quoted
in the paper: richer media "may foster engagement and clarity in neutral or positive
contexts yet heighten emotional intensity in negative contexts."

Why it earns its place: it is the only piece of evidence on this topic that *cuts against*
the post's own product interest and then turns out to support it. The lean-channel
advantage is real and it belongs to bad news. Limits disclosed in the body: n=96 in one
firm, topic was workplace eating habits, not a relationship question.

Subject test: pass (channel choice × emotional valence). Swap test: pass — no other
keyword in this batch is about channel choice.

### 2. Holtzman, S., DeClerck, D., Turcotte, K., Lisi, D. & Woodworth, M. (2017)

*"Emotional support during times of stress: Can text messaging compete with in-person
interactions?"*, **Computers in Human Behavior**, 24 January 2017.
`https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563217300559`

What it says: the first randomised controlled studies to compare social and emotional
impact of support given in person versus by text. Two lab experiments — support from a
close friend (n=64) and from a similar-aged confederate (n=188). In-person support was
associated with **significantly higher positive affect** than text in both; in-person also
produced greater satisfaction with support, but only in experiment 2. Authors' conclusion:
there may be emotional costs to relying on digital communication during times of stress.

**Full text not read.** ScienceDirect returned HTTP 403 to every automated fetch attempt
(direct URL, PDF URL, browser user-agent). The claim above is limited to the
publisher-deposited abstract. Recorded in `auditReport.failed` and in
`batchMeta.structuralLimitations`, and flagged "abstract only" in the post's own sources list.

Subject test: pass. Swap test: pass.

### 3. Groggel, A. (2023)

*"The Power of a 😉: Emojis Reducing Ambiguity in Digitally Mediated Romantic Encounters"*,
**Symbolic Interaction**, 31 August 2023. CC BY.
`https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/symb.668`

What it says: 713 college students randomly assigned to read a Snapchat conversation with
or without emojis, then asked to infer the characters' thoughts, feelings and clarity of
intention. Emojis **increased the clarity of the main character's intentions**. The same
study found that emoji presence, participants' cognitive effort and emotional impact all
*reduced* comfort with receiving a similar message — clarity cuts both ways.

**Full text not read.** Wiley returned HTTP 403. Limited to the publisher-deposited
abstract. Disclosed the same way as above.

Subject test: pass (cue restoration in romantic text). Swap test: pass.

### 4. Infobip survey, reported by CBS News (2 December 2022)

`https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/have-you-received-a-marriage-proposal-over-text-youre-not-alone-new-survey-shows-surprising-texting-trends-in-the-u-s/`
**Read in full.**

Survey of more than 1,000 Americans on SMS habits, released for the 30th anniversary of
the first SMS. Figures used: 26% of millennials reported receiving a **marriage proposal
via text**. Figures available but not used: 63% of millennials and 61% of Gen Z call
texting an important part of romance; 27.5% of respondents check messages within one
minute; 27.6% of men and 18.8% of women have been dumped by text.

Not a competitor (mainstream news reporting a vendor survey, not a gifting or card
product). Not a generic context statistic — it is about this exact behaviour. **Zero**
PIB/TRAI/Census/MEA statistics appear in this post, so the "at most 1" cap is unused.

Subject test: pass. Swap test: pass — a text-proposal prevalence figure does not fit a
Propose Day shayari or a proposal-ideas listicle unchanged.

leans on the negative-message experiments and says explicitly what does and does not
