## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (must be present or the page looks incomplete): a real list of
distinct ideas; the video-call option; the send-a-link option; privacy/surprise
handling; some acknowledgement of distance.

**The gap.** Every incumbent presents ideas as an undifferentiated inspiration
pile. Nobody sorts them by the reader's actual constraints, and nobody is willing
to say an idea is bad. The Pinterest boards and the ring-retailer blogs both list
"surprise video call with her family on it" as a charming option; none of them
notes that an audience removes the other person's right to hesitate.

**Stale data.** Four ranking incumbents across three clusters still carry **2025**
in their titles (giftalove, floweraura, Loksatta, Times Now); one YouTube result
carries 2023. Pinterest boards on this cluster are dated 2024.

**Fan-out sub-queries → the H2 map.**

| Sub-query | H2 |
|---|---|
| what is an online proposal / does it count | What an online proposal actually is |
| how do I choose between all these ideas | The four constraints that decide which idea works |
| what are the actual ideas | Nine online proposal ideas, sorted by what they demand of you |
| which one fits my situation | Which idea fits which evening |
| should I do this at all / what could go wrong | When an online proposal is the wrong choice |

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that sorts online proposal ideas by the
constraints that actually kill them — shared hour, audience, certainty, build
time — and names the idea to avoid, grounded in the 39.9% platform-wide
password-protect rate and the 6.4-hour median build sampled on `/love-gf`.

**Lane discipline.** Siblings own `virtual proposal ideas for long distance
relationship` and `proposal ideas for girlfriend at home`. This page handles the
general online case, hands the long-distance case off in one sentence with a
`/train-ticket` link, and never develops either sibling's territory.

**Subject test:** all four are about mediated co-presence — whether a moment
feels shared when the people are on screens. Passing count: 4 (requirement: 2).
**Peer-reviewed and open-access:** all four; #1 and #2 are the load-bearing ones.
**Swap test:** none could sit unchanged in another keyword's post in this batch —
each is attached to a specific idea on the list (voice call vs video call,
camera-on fatigue, co-watching, and the limits of screens).
**Generic context statistics used: zero** (budget was 1).
**Competitor sources: zero.** **Wikipedia body links: zero** (Wikipedia appears
only as verified `sameAs` targets in `structuredData`, which does not count
against the body budget).

**Honest sourcing limitation.** None of the four studies is about romantic
proposals. No study of proposals or romantic milestone announcements over video
surfaced in any of the searches above. The mechanism transfers; the population
does not, and the body says which finding comes from which population
(students, grandparent–grandchild pairs, transnational families).
