## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** — present on essentially every ranking page, so the post must cover
them or read as incomplete: a definition of a virtual/online proposal; a list of
concrete ideas; the video-call option; something about the ring; some acknowledgement
that distance is hard.

**The gap.** Every incumbent list is sorted by *format* — video call, screen-share
slideshow, digital card, delivery service, game. **Not one is sorted by the constraint
distance actually imposes.** The four real constraints in a long-distance proposal are:

1. a time-zone gap, so your evening is her 4 a.m.;
2. she may be physically alone when it lands, with nobody to turn to;
3. you cannot see her face while she reads it;
4. there is no way to hold her in the hour afterwards.

Nobody writes the timing problem, and nobody says out loud that several of the most
popular "ideas" are actively bad at distance — a surprise that arrives at 3 a.m., or
anything that requires her to perform a reaction on camera.

**Unanswered questions the incumbents raise and drop:** should you warn her something
is coming? How long a countdown? What if she is alone? What happens in the hour after?

**Fan-out sub-queries** (each becomes an H2 or an FAQ): what is a virtual proposal ·
what time should it land · video call or something she opens alone · how to handle a
12-hour gap · does telling her spoil it · what if she's alone · is it less real ·
should you just wait for the next visit · what to do after the yes.

**Angle sentence.** *Wins by being the only post that sorts long-distance proposal
ideas by the four constraints distance imposes — the time-zone gap, being alone when
it lands, not seeing her face, and no way to hold her after — and the only one that
uses anticipation research to argue a scheduled countdown beats a surprise at
distance, grounded in SubhSandesh's platform-wide 92.7% publish-and-share rate and
39.9% password-lock rate.*
