### The intent split nobody resolves on the page

Every US result answers "marriage proposal + ring". Every Indian result answers "ask her out".
Neither says which one the reader meant. In Indian English *to propose a girl* means to disclose
feelings and ask her to be your girlfriend; in British and American English *to propose* means a
marriage proposal. This query is overwhelmingly the first sense, which is why the batch's
alternative-template rule resolves to `/love-gf` and not `/proposal` for most searchers.

## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes (all five carry these):** pick the right moment; ask clearly and briefly; consider
whether she likes attention; have a rose or a small gift; be ready for a no.

**The gap — none of them do any of this:**

1. **Nobody quantifies the fear.** Every page says "be confident". Not one cites a single study on
   how badly people misjudge how a disclosure will land. There is a substantial literature and the
   effects are large.
2. **Nobody names the counter-risk.** A staged, public, hard-to-refuse proposal can manufacture a
   yes. Bohns & DeVincent is directly about this and no page selling proposal products will print it.
3. **Nobody tells the reader that most of the advice is aimed at the wrong population.** Two thirds
   of relationships start as friendships (Stinson et al.), while relationship-initiation advice —
   and every listicle above — is written for strangers meeting on a date.
4. **Nobody prints the 2027 weekday.** Propose Day 2027 is a **Monday** (8 Feb), against a Sunday in
   2026, which genuinely narrows the window. Four ranking incumbents still carry 2025 in their titles.
5. **Stale / absent data.** Zero of the analysed pages cite anything at all. There is nothing to
   supersede; there is simply an empty evidence field.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:**

| Sub-query | H2 |
|---|---|
| does propose mean marriage in India / propose day date | What "propose" means when the person asking is Indian |
| will she say yes / what if she rejects me | Your odds are better than you think, and there are numbers for it |
| what should I do / what should I say | The five things that decide whether the ask lands |
| in person vs page vs gift | Four ways to ask, compared |
| should I do a big public proposal | When a staged proposal is the wrong call |
| is this data real | What our own data cannot tell you |

**Angle:** wins by being the only post that separates the Indian-English "will you be my
girlfriend" ask from a marriage proposal, prices the reader's fear with real psychology (48%
underestimation of compliance, the liking gap, the beautiful mess effect), and names the case
where a staged proposal manufactures a yes — grounded in SubhSandesh's platform-wide figures.

risk. Searched the *phenomenon*, not the keyword. Deliberately avoided the long-distance,

## Phase 6 — Audit

48 passed, 2 failed, disjoint, 48 + 2 = 50.

Both failures are the same root cause: the exact query string *"how to propose girlfriend"* is not
grammatical English. It sits in the slug and is quoted verbatim in the first sentence of the body,
but the H1 and `metaTitle` read "How to Propose to Your Girlfriend", which pushes the keyword to
words 1–6 rather than the first five. Forcing the raw string into the SERP title would cost more in
trust and click-through than exact-match placement is worth. This is a deliberate, reported choice,
not an oversight.

The no-proposal-data and empty-pricing-block limitations are in `batchMeta.structuralLimitations`,
not in `failed` — they are not checklist items.
