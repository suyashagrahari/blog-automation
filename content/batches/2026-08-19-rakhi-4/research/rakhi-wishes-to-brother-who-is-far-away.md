### What the SERP teaches, concretely

1. **The winning page is a 2022 list with an exact-match slug and zero data.** Its H1 says 50; the
   title tag says 40. Nobody has updated it in four years and it still ranks first.
2. **Nothing on this SERP has an FAQ block, a table, a byline with a bio, or a single cited
   source.** Table stakes here are low; the ceiling is set by originality, not completeness.
3. **The lists are structurally interchangeable.** Read as writing advice, every line in the
   "across the miles" buckets makes a claim about *the bond* rather than about *the brother* —
   sun-and-moon similes, lighthouse similes, "bytes travelling through the internet". Across the
   five pages I found no line containing a proper noun, a date, or an amount that could belong to
   only one brother.
4. **The competitor states my thesis and then abandons it.** The #1 page's own intro instructs the
   reader to "add a personal memory" — and then never says how, which is precisely the 1,500 words
   this post is for.
5. **One long-emotional-paragraph pattern is worth naming.** The gibox messages run 40–60 words
   each and spend the extra words on a simile *explaining* the feeling instead of evidencing it.
   That observation drives the length section.
