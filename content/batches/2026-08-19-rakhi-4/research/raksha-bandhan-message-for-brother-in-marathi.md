## Phase 2 — the gap

Nothing in the set treats the keyword's own verb. A *wish* is broadcast; a *message* is addressed to one person
and expects an answer. So none of the five covers: how a Marathi text opens and closes as against a card, whether
to lead with his name or a kinship term, or how to leave a line open so he replies instead of reacting.

Second gap: **28 August 2026 is two festivals at once.** Shravan Purnima is also नारळी पौर्णिमा, and in the
Koli fishing settlements of the Konkan coast that is the bigger day of the two. A sister in Versova and a sister in
Pune are marking different things on one date, and no ranking page — nor either Marathi sibling — writes a message
that knows which.

Stale/wrong data to supersede: marathiya.com's 9 August 2026 date.

Fan-out sub-queries → H2s: what is a Marathi rakhi *message* (vs a wish) · is Narali Purnima the same day as Raksha
Bandhan · what do I call him, नाव / भाऊ / दादा / भाऊराया · what do I write to a brother on the coast · nine ready
lines by situation · what if he reads Marathi but types Roman · नारळी भात · how do I get him to reply · where to send it.

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that writes a Marathi rakhi message *as a sent message with a reply
expected* and splits its lines by whether the family keeps नारळी पौर्णिमा — the festival written into Maharashtra's
own trawl-ban rule — using SubhSandesh's 12.1 average opens across 33,007 views.

## Phase 3 — sources

Lane: Narali Purnima / Koli observance from ethnographic, archival or official cultural sources, plus Marathi
digital-messaging conventions. Zero generic context statistics (the batch's single allowance was spent on
`pib.gov.in`). No Census, no PIB, no TRAI. No Wikipedia link in the body.

1. **agris.fao.org** — Salim, Vijayan & Sandhya (2016), "Trade-off between monsoon trawl ban and the livelihood of
   trawl labourers in Maharashtra", FAO AGRIS record. Abstract states the trawl ban was introduced in Maharashtra
   from 1992 "for a period of 65 days from June 10 to August 15 (or Naralipoornima whichever is earlier)".
   **Only the abstract was read** — the full text is not open on this record. Scholarly, subject test passes: it is
   the festival inside a fisheries regulation.
2. **fishercoast.si/narali-poornima/** — ethnographic documentation by Ishita Patil for the FisherCoast exhibition
   (curators Nataša Rogelja Caf and Martina Bofulin, Research Centre of the Slovenian Academy of Sciences and Arts;
   editorial board incl. Ajit Menon, Maarten Bavinck, Nitya Rao, D. Parthasarathy). Palghar, Arabian Sea: in the
   late afternoon every family arrives at the shore with coconut, rice and flowers; boats are painted and decorated
   before the day; groups process through the village performing folk songs; baskets are the women's symbol as oars
   are the men's. No publication date printed on the page (photo assets dated 2021).
3. **utsav.gov.in/view-event/narali-purnima-2** — Ministry of Tourism festival portal. Narali Purnima is observed on
   the Purnima of Shravana, "therefore referred to as Shravana Purnima"; coconuts are offered to the sea god Varun;
   it marks the end of the monsoon and the beginning of the fishing season; nets are repaired and boats repainted
   and garlanded beforehand. The page's worked example is "Monday, August 19, 2024" — a 2024 instance, not 2026, and
   the post does not present it as 2026.
4. **ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/18361** — Malhotra & Pearce, "Facing Falsehoods: Strategies for Polite
   Misinformation Correction", *International Journal of Communication* 16 (2022), published 2022-04-10.
   Peer-reviewed, open access. Interview study, **N = 26** Indian young adults on WhatsApp exchanges with older
   relatives: they chose strategies that "decreased the sense of direct interaction to avoid being viewed as
   disrespectful and questioning the competency of higher status elders". Used for the elder-brother indirectness
   point, not for anything about misinformation.
5. **icsf.net** (International Collective in Support of Fishworkers news archive) — Maharashtra's current monsoon
   ban runs **1 June to 31 July**, under the Maharashtra Marine Fisheries Regulation Act 1989 and the Amendment
   Ordinance 2021, for mechanised and motorised vessels within 12 nautical miles; non-motorised boats exempt. ICSF
   is republishing a *Free Press Journal* report and names it as the URL — a documentation-centre record of a press
   report, not the order itself. Recorded as such.
6. **aa.usno.navy.mil moon-phase API** — Full Moon 28 August 2026, 04:18 UT (09:48 IST). This is the date
   fact-check, and it earns a citation only because the post prints the time.
