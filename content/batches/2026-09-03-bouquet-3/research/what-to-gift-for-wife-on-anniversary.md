## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present in all or most of the ten): named gift categories;
flowers; jewellery; personalisation; the wife-specific framing; something about
delivery.

**The gap.** Nobody answers *whether the gift survives the week*. Ten results, and
not one distinguishes a gift that is used once from a gift that is returned to.
Nobody gives lead time. Nobody makes an India-specific argument about flowers —
`marriage.com` is US-first and the retailers are format-agnostic. Nobody names a
case where their own product is the wrong choice.

**Stale data.** Not applicable in the usual sense: there is no data to be stale.
`marriage.com` cites zero statistics across 4,185 words, and the retailers cite
only their own prices. **Superseding nothing; introducing measurement where there
was none.**

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** whether cash is acceptable; whether
the paper/silver anniversary-year list means anything in India; what to do with
three hours left; whether a rose is too predictable.

## Phase 6 — audit summary

**47 passed, 3 failed, disjoint, 47 + 3 = 50.** Checklist strings copied
byte-verbatim by parsing `publish-checklist.md` programmatically and collapsing
internal whitespace to single spaces.

The three failures, all recorded in `batchMeta.auditReport.failed`:

1. **metaTitle five-word rule — structural.** The keyword is **7 words**. An exact
   placement cannot end inside the first five words of any title. It occupies words
   one to seven; the 50–60 character half of the item passes at **58 characters**.
2. **Slug stop words — deliberate.** `what-to-gift-for-wife-on-anniversary` is
   7 tokens and 36 characters and contains **4 stop words** (`what`, `to`, `for`,
   `on`). Neither "short" nor "no stop words" holds. Kept because this is the
   batch's highest-demand keyword and exact match is worth more than the
   convention.
3. **Swap test — partial and honest.** Two of six sources (Junqueira & Peetz;
   Malviya et al.) would survive a swap into another bouquet-cluster post, because
   the secondary keywords force a flower section. The other four are keyed to this
   post's specific claims.

Word count **1,797** by plain whitespace split, FAQs excluded. 11 FAQs, in
`article.faqs` only. Highest FAQ-to-heading token overlap measured: **0.50**, on
two pairs that are semantically distinct questions ("what to write on a page" vs
"when a page is the wrong gift"); nothing at or above the 0.6 re-examination
threshold.
