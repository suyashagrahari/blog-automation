## Source replacement — 2026-09-03, after coordinator review

The originally-cited **Estudios Gerenciales** personal-values survey
(`icesi.edu.co/.../article/view/3539`, n=1,085) was a **genuine cap breach**, not a
borderline call. `USED-SOURCES.md` line 1014 records that exact URL at a count of 2 —
already spent in `2026-09-02-bouquet/blogs/propose-day-gift-for-girlfriend.json` and
`2026-09-02-bouquet/blogs/rose-day-gift-for-girlfriend.json` — so a third use would
put one URL in 3 posts against a cap of 2. I missed it because my Phase 3 checks
counted the *domain* against the closed and 3-post lists in the batch brief, and
`icesi.edu.co` appears on neither; I did not additionally grep the spent-URL list for
the exact URL. That check is now part of the candidate screen below.

**Replacement:** *Gift-giving, Love Styles, and Sacrifice: An Analysis Among Romantic
Partners*, RAC: Revista de Administração Contemporânea (ANPAD), vol 24 no 1, published
**15 November 2019**, DOI `10.1590/1982-7849rac2020190187`.
`https://rac.anpad.org.br/index.php/rac/article/view/1372`

Why this one rather than another:

- **Same load, better fit.** It is the same *kind* of source — an empirical survey of
  gift-giving behaviour with an n — but it is about **gift-giving between romantic
  partners** specifically, which is this keyword's exact relationship, where the
  displaced paper was about gift-giving in general.
- **Genuinely fetchable.** The abstract page returns HTTP 200 to a server-side fetch
  and the full English abstract was read directly, which is a strict improvement on
  the displaced source (HTTP 503, DOAJ record only). Audit failure 4 improved from
  "3 of 6 abstract-only" to "2 of 6".
- **Unspent.** `rac.anpad.org.br` — and the bare string `anpad` — return **zero** hits
  in `USED-SOURCES.md`. Zero in-batch collisions across the eight sibling blogs.
- Peer-reviewed, open access, DOAJ-indexed.

Verified finding, narrower than the source in every clause: among 257 survey
participants in romantic relationships, the **altruistic love style (Agape)** most
often induces gift-giving and generates a *sacrificial perception* in the giver, while
the **passionate style (Eros)** is the predominant primary style and gives more
frequently through the social motive.

**Where the two claims moved.** The displaced paper supported one paragraph in the
`… for ladies` H2, arguing that a familiar festival gift beats a clever one. That
argument is not what the new paper says, so the paragraph was not re-pointed at it —
it was rewritten twice over instead:

1. The RAC finding went to the **lead of the seven-ideas H2**, where it does real
   work: it grounds the whole list by naming which of the seven read as a sacrifice
   (ideas 5 and 6, the two that ask time of you) and which do not.
2. The `… for ladies` H2 got a **first-party replacement** rather than a substitute
   citation — *"the average registered creator here makes 1.21 pages, so these are
   built one-to-one, not in batches of eight for a room. A page does not multiply; a
   tray of small identical things does."* That keeps a number in the section (needed
   for the per-H2 number item), and it is an **admission against interest**, which is
   a stronger signal than the borrowed claim it replaced. `factsUsed` grew to 9 lines.

**Nothing is left asserted without a source.** The tradition/personal-values claim was
deleted outright, not softened; no sentence in the body now depends on the removed
citation. The Dhaulagiri ritual-giving paragraph, which carried the section's other
argument, was untouched and still supports "small and correct beats large and
surprising".
