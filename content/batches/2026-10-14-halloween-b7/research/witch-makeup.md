## Phase 3: sources (all fetched and read in full)
1. **archive.org/details/photoplaystudies561nati.** *Photoplay Studies* vol. 5 (1939–40), the Wizard of Oz issue, Library of Congress copy (OCR read).
   - Jack Dawn gave Hamilton "a green face and a long, sharp metal nose".
   - Rosson says she "wore black, with hands and face a bright green", usually kept before a dark grey background.
   - Billie Burke's headdress was a lighting problem.
   - The domain is cap-exempt.
2. **gutenberg.org/ebooks/55.** Baum, 1900. The witch "had but one eye", her blood had "dried up", the Emerald City people "had greenish skins", and Glinda has red ringlets, a white dress and blue eyes. The domain is cap-exempt.
3. **fda.gov.** The colour-additive summary, current as of 2025-12-16, gives the eye-area listings for chromium oxide greens, chromium hydroxide green, D&C Green 5, D&C Green 6 and 8, FD&C Green 3, D&C Black 2 and iron oxides. fda.gov is now in 2 posts in this batch.
4. **europepmc PMC8587163.** Pawlaczyk et al. 2021, *Molecules* (MDPI, CC BY), full text read.
   - 94 eyeshadows were tested.
   - Highest cadmium was almost 4 mg/kg, in a pink children's shadow, against a 0.5 limit.
   - Highest barium was 2,154 mg/kg, in a green shadow.
   - Colour was the only parameter that was not significant.
   - This takes the batch's last MDPI slot.
5. **en.wiktionary.org/wiki/hedge_witch.** The term dates to around the 1990s, possibly from Rae Beth's *Hedge Witch* (1990). The domain is cap-exempt.
