## Phase 2 — gap and angle
- Table stakes: short romantic and funny lines for a spouse, and the 31 October date.
- Gap: no ranking page is written for a wife at all. None sorts wishes by what she is doing that night, and none says *why* naming her work matters.
- Fan-out: what to write to a wife who hates Halloween, works nights or loves horror, funny vs romantic, length, timing, sending a page instead, and when a wish isn't enough.
- Angle: sort by her role (costume-maker, trick-or-treat/bedtime captain, horror fan, night shift, Halloween sceptic) and by tone, plus a 3-part specificity formula. This is distinct from 345 husband (delivery channel and marriage stage) and from b4 ideas-for-wife (plans, not words).

## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched 15 Oct 2026)
1. Haney & Barber 2022, Canadian Review of Sociology (Wiley OA), PMC9537987: Statistics Canada CPSS wave 3, 15–21 June 2020. Women with a partner reported housework falling mainly on them at OR 4.92; among parents, OR 10.12 for staying home with children. Full text read.
2. Eckstein et al. 2023, SCAN (OUP OA), PMC9976881: 43 couples, up to 18 compliments each from 23 areas, dual fMRI. Ventral striatum activated particularly when selecting messages for the partner. Limits: highly satisfied couples only, and no vague-vs-specific comparison, which the body states. **This paper is now 2 posts in b7 (also halloween-surprise-for-girlfriend), so it is AT CAP.**
3. ONS 2016, "Women shoulder the responsibility of 'unpaid work'": UK 2015 time-use data, 26 vs 16 hours a week of unpaid work. This is the one context statistic; it is dated, and the body labels it 2016.
4. Smoliak et al. 2023, Family Process (Wiley), PMID 37323080: a conceptual paper on emotion work. **Abstract only**, disclosed in the body. Family Process is now 2 posts in b7.
