## Phase 2: gap and angle
- Table stakes: what the trend is, carving a head, a few poses.
- Gap: nobody dates the trend to a source, weighs real vs foam heads, explains seeing/breathing, or gives twilight times by city and year.
- Fan-out: origin; real vs foam; how to cut eye/neck/breathing holes; poses with no faces; golden/blue hour on 31 Oct; editing and posting; when not to do it.
- Angle: wins by dating the trend to a primary news report, weighing real against foam heads with a neck-load study, and giving 31 October twilight times for four cities, using the 48.3% phone-open and 38.5% password rates to set crop and privacy.

## Phase 3: sources (all fetched and read 2026-10-14)
1. fox13news.com, 30 Oct 2020: Sapountsis shoot, "a hole in the bottom big enough to fit over their heads". Not in the measured top 10 (the ranking copies are the Scripps syndicate).
2. extension.usu.edu, Feb 2020: carving varieties 18–25 lb; mini types 0.5–1.5 lb.
3. Europe PMC PMC11442774, Yin et al. 2024, Experimental Physiology (Wiley/Physiological Society, CC BY), full text: 2.85 kg helmet + NVG for 45 min, n = 20; female fatigue greater, male disc height 5.7 to 4.4 mm.
4. Europe PMC PMC4120707, Otsuka et al. 2014, JEP:HPP (APA, CC BY), full text: head orientation attracts perceived gaze (Wollaston 1824); head outline alone can induce the effect.
5. sunrise-sunset.org API, retrieved 2026-10-14: sunset and civil-twilight end for New York, London, Sydney, Mumbai on 31 Oct 2026 and 2027. USNO API timed out; timeanddate.com returned 403.

Searches tried for occluded-face perception: "emotion recognition body posture face occluded", "full head mask costume perception identity". The relevant whole-body/masked-face papers were Frontiers, PLOS ONE or MDPI (banned or full), so the gaze/head-orientation paper was used instead.
