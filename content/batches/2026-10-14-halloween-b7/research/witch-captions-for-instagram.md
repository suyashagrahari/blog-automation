## Phase 2 — gap
- **Table stakes:** short, funny and cute witch captions, plus costume framing.
- **Gap:**
  - Every competitor sorts by tone and borrows lyrics and film lines. None sorts by witch aesthetic or photo type (green, sea, night-sky, glam, little, pet).
  - None gives a caption-length or hashtag guide.
  - None checks any history.
  - None covers alt text, or the harm of "witch" as a label for a real person.
- **Stale data:** none of them cite data.
- **Fan-out:**
  - How long should the caption be?
  - How many hashtags should it carry?
  - Green witch vs sea witch?
  - Is it okay if I'm not a witch?
  - Is it kid-safe?
  - Where does the broom come from?
  - Should I add alt text?
  - Can I call someone a witch?
  - How do I send it to one person?
- **Angle:** the only witch-caption post that sorts original lines by aesthetic and photo, and fact-checks its history captions.

## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched 2026-10-14)
1. **Michael D. Bailey, The Conversation, 2024-03-05** (Iowa State historian). Full text read:
   - the earliest known image of flying witches is a 1451 manuscript copy of Le Franc's *Le champion des dames*
   - brooms and staffs were household tools, marking the figures as non-elite
   - Le Franc called believers in witch flight lacking "common sense"
   - an early 10th-century church law called night flight an illusion
2. **Reginald Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Project Gutenberg #60766.** A reprint of the 1584 first edition. The title-page phrase "all the conueiances of Legierdemaine and iuggling are deciphered" was checked in the plain-text file.
3. **Meta for Developers, IG User Media (updated 2026-09-28).**
   - Captions: max 2,200 characters, 30 hashtags, 20 @ tags.
   - alt_text: up to 1,000 characters, image posts only, added 2025-03-24.
   - This is the post's single context source, and it fails the swap test.
4. **Gershman, PLOS ONE, 2022-11-23** (via Europe PMC, full text read). 140,000+ respondents in 95 countries and territories; over 40% believe in witchcraft; costs include disrupted social relations and anxiety; India and China were not surveyed. PLOS ONE now sits at 3 posts in this batch (orchestrator note: 2 before this one), which is at the cap but does not exceed it.
5. **Jabłońska, Open Theology 2024** (CC BY 4.0, De Gruyter). **Abstract only**, read via the Crossref API; the publisher page returned a 202 bot check, which was not worked around. Cited for: 724 participants, and the persecuted image set against the modern stereotype of an independent, strong, wise woman.
