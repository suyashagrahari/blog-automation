## Phase 3: sources (all fetched 2026-10-14)
1. McFarland, Jurafsky & Rawlings 2013, AJS 118(6) (University of Chicago Press), author-hosted PDF at web.stanford.edu. Full text read. About 1,100 four-minute speed dates; women clicked with men who used appreciations/sympathy and mirrored their laughter; negative association between questioning and clicking; about 21% of turns were questions.
2. Huang et al. 2017, JPSP (APA), Europe PMC MED/28447835. Abstract only (DASH full text HTTP 429). Follow-up questions increase liking; more second-date agreement for speed daters who asked them; people do not anticipate it.
3. Correction 2025, Europe PMC MED/40111841. Audit confirmed all conclusions.
4. Cornell Chronicle, May 2018, on Bohns & DeVincent: 942 STEM grad students and 385 MTurk adults; 52% of targets avoided suitors vs 7% of suitors who thought so; 33% vs 6% kept thinking about it. The underlying paper is SAGE (banned) and was not cited.
