## Phase 3 — Sources (all fetched 2026-10-14)

1. Wiktionary "pumpkin" — etymology (pompion + -kin, Greek "ripe", first attested 1647), US endearment sense, Australian sense = winter squash. Cap-exempt.
2. Wiktionary "gourd" — 4 pronunciations (UK /ɡʊəd/, /ɡɔːd/; US /ɡʊɚd/, /ɡɔɹd/), slang "a person's head". Cap-exempt.
3. Wiktionary "squash" — drink, sport, crush/compress senses. Cap-exempt.
4. Palmann & Miller 2025, Humor (De Gruyter, CC BY), PMC12487772 — full text read. Homographic 0.60 vs heterographic 0.52 mean funniness; negative correlation phonological distance vs funniness on 2 of 4 measures; ratings by 1,063 US native English speakers, 28,210 pairs (Simpson et al. 2019 dataset). Note: also cited in b2 halloween-love-puns (different batch; caps are per batch).

Konovalova & Petrova 2023, Journal of Eye Movement Research (MDPI, CC BY 4.0), PMC10874607 — full text read. 55 Russian participants; preliminary experiment (summarised, ref 27) rated pun posters more attractive, original, effective, positive; readers start with text and spend longer on text. Note: also used in b3 halloween-puns-for-party-invite.
