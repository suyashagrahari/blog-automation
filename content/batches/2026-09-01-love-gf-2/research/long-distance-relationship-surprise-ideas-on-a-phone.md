## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (must be present or the post reads incomplete): voice notes, video
calls / watching something together, surprise deliveries of food or flowers, "open
when" style sequenced messages, playlists, photo rituals, scheduled calls.

**The gap.** Every ranking page lists surprises that need a courier, a card, or her
address. "On a phone" is asking for the opposite, literally, and nobody answers it
literally. Nobody separates what survives a time-zone gap from what does not, and
nobody addresses execution: the phone is the only channel, so the surprise competes
with the rest of her notifications, arrives while she is in a meeting, and may be seen
by whoever is sitting next to her.

**Stale data.** colorado.edu is from March 2020; nothing else on the SERP cites a
figure at all.

**Unanswered questions.** How do you time it across time zones? How do you check it
renders on her phone before sending? What do you do when she opens it at a bad
moment? When is a link the wrong answer entirely?

**Fan-out sub-queries → structure.**

| Sub-query | Where it lands |
|---|---|
| What counts as a phone-only surprise? | H2 2 (definition) |
| Free / ten-minute ideas | H2 3 (5 H3s) |
| Long distance relationship surprise *gifts* | H2 4 (secondary keyword as H2, 4 H3s) |
| Ideas that need her address or a helper | H2 5 (4 H3s) |
| Which ideas survive a time-zone gap, and what do they cost? | H2 6 (comparison table) |
| When should I send it? What if she's busy? | H2 7 |
| Is a surprise *website* better than a message? | H2 8 (secondary keyword as H2) + objection block |

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that sorts long-distance phone surprises by
what the sender needs — ten minutes, a small spend, her address, or a third party —
and states per idea whether it survives a time-zone gap, using SubhSandesh's own 975
`/love-gf` pages, 14,396 views and 6.9-hour median build time.
