## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes (all six carry these, so the post must too):**
- A large, usable set of ready-to-send lines (30+), quotable verbatim.
- Some grouping so the reader can find the right register quickly.
- Coverage of the obvious LDR beats: missing her, trust, the reunion, goodnight/good morning.

**The gap (none of the six touches any of this):**
1. **The message is the entire channel.** In a same-city relationship the text is a supplement to
   presence; at distance it is the relationship's whole surface for that day.
2. **It arrives without context.** She reads it cold — no face, no tone, no room.
3. **It has to survive a time-zone gap.** A line that assumes she is awake and free to reply is
   asking for something she may be unable to give for eight hours.
4. **The sender is usually managing their own anxiety while writing it.** Nobody separates a message
   that *reassures* from one that *quietly asks for reassurance while pretending to give it* — that
   distinction is the post's whole thesis.
5. **Nobody names the lines that backfire** — tests, guilt-shaped lines, and lines that demand an
   immediate reply from someone who may be asleep.
6. **Nobody addresses permanence.** A message in a chat thread is buried by tomorrow. The
   first-party evidence that a *page* gets re-opened instead of scrolled past is 12.3 average views
   per created page and 14,396 views across 975 /love-gf pages.

**Stale data:** not applicable — no competitor cites data at all, so there is no stale figure to
supersede. That is itself the finding: any dated, verifiable number is a differentiator here.

**Unanswered questions the six raise and drop:** how often to send; what to do when she does not
reply; whether to send the heavy honest line at all; what to send when your night is her morning.
