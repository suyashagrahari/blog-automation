## Phase 2 — gap

**Table stakes** — a large, categorised bank of questions; flirty and deep tiers;
some LDR-specific framing; dares of some kind.

**The gap.** None of them engineers a *round*. They deliver inventory and leave
sequencing, length and stopping rules to the reader — which is exactly the thing
that fails at distance, because an unstructured video call goes flat and that
flatness is the real reason people search this. Nobody handles the actual
constraints either:

1. Lag makes simultaneous reveals and speed rounds impossible.
2. Dares needing physical presence are dead on arrival, and the ranking pages
   still include them.
3. A shared or overheard room caps what can be asked out loud.
4. Someone is usually playing at a bad hour.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:**

- what makes a long-distance round different → `## What makes a long-distance truth or dare round different from a normal one?`
- the question bank → `## 45 truth or dare questions for couples long distance, in five tiers`
- over the phone / no video → `## Truth or dare questions for couples over the phone, when video is off`
- does it work on a bad connection → `## Which tiers survive lag, and which need a private room`
- how to actually play → `## Seven rules that keep a round from going flat`
- are spicy sets a good idea → `## Why the "spicy truth or dare" framing is the wrong default at distance`
- where a page beats a live round → `## Where a shared page beats a live round, and where it loses`

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that engineers the round rather than dumping
a list — five escalating tiers, a 10–12 prompt cap, and a measured ruling on which
tiers survive a 438 ms video-call gap — anchored on 975 `/love-gf` pages (29.2% of
3,335) and 14,396 views (35.2% of 40,864), on a template that actually plays a
Truth-or-Dare round and keeps the answers.

**Stale data.** Nothing to supersede: the competitors cite no data at all. The win
is not a fresher statistic, it is that the post has any evidence in it.

All five sources below were fetched and read in full; none is abstract-only.

| # | Source | Verified claim used | Published |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Temporal Aspects of Turn-Taking in Zoom Conversations](https://www.internationalphoneticassociation.org/icphs-proceedings/ICPhS2023/full_papers/448.pdf) — Qiang Xia, ICPhS 2023, Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin | Berlin Dialogue Corpus, 20 native German speakers in pairs, Diapix spot-the-difference tasks face-to-face and over Zoom. ~50% of face-to-face gaps fell inside 270 ms; the median Zoom gap was ~438 ms. Median overlaps 416 ms (Zoom) vs 319 ms (face-to-face). A ~450 ms threshold is needed to describe common Zoom turn transitions, against ~300 ms face-to-face. | 2023 |
| 2 | [The Effect of Reminiscing about Laughter on Relationship Satisfaction](https://psych.appstate.edu/sites/psych.appstate.edu/files/reminiscingarticle.pdf) — Bazzini, Stack, Martincin & Davis, *Motivation and Emotion* 31:25–34 | 52 couples randomly assigned to one of four reminiscing conditions. Those who reminisced about events involving shared laughter reported higher post-manipulation relationship satisfaction than the three control conditions, and the effect was **not** attributed to mood induction — mood scores were similar across groups. | 2006-10-19 (online) |
| 3 | [When Sharing a Laugh Means Sharing More](https://oveislab.com/s/1010072Fs10919-016-0245-9.pdf) — Kurtz & Algoe, *Journal of Nonverbal Behavior* 41:45–65 | A series of online and laboratory studies gave correlational and causal support for shared laughter promoting relationship well-being, with **increased perceptions of similarity most consistently driving the effect**. | 2016-12-01 (online) |
| 4 | [Extending the study of playfulness in romantic life](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-70979-2) — Brauer, Sendatzki & Proyer, *Scientific Reports* | 332 mixed-gender and 139 same-gender couples, N total = 942. | 5 | [Bad Humor, Bad Marriage: Humor Styles in Divorced and Married Couples](https://ejop.psychopen.eu/index.php/ejop/article/download/210/210.pdf) — Saroglou, Lacour & Demeure, *Europe's Journal of Psychology* 6(3):94–121 | 98 married and 48 divorced couples, N = 292.
