## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present on nearly every page, so the post must have them):
- Complete, copy-ready letters rather than instructions alone.
- Segmentation of some kind (theme, relationship type, or length).
- A statement of *why* a letter beats a gift or a text.

**The gap — what none of them do:**
1. **They sell wishes, not letters.** A wish is a caption or a text; a birthday letter is the one letter of the year with a built-in occasion, it gets kept, and it is read aloud in front of other people as often as alone. Nobody addresses the aloud case at all.
2. **Nobody segments by situation.** tenorshare segments by *who* (wife/fiancée/sister); none segment by *circumstance* — first birthday together, different cities, a milestone, weeks after a fight, seven years in, or a partner who is embarrassed by attention. Circumstance is what actually determines the letter.
3. **No length guidance.** Not one page states how long a birthday letter should be, despite length being the single most common failure (300 words into a three-week-old relationship).
4. **No closing guidance.** Every sample ends on some version of "forever"; nobody names that as a mistake.
5. **No downside section.** Zero pages say when a letter is the wrong call — a very new relationship, or a letter standing in for a gift she named. This is the clearest E-E-A-T gap in the SERP.
6. **No data of any kind.** No statistics, no dated sources, no research, no first-party numbers on any of the eight results.

**Stale data:** nothing to supersede — there is no data on these pages to be stale.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** how long; whether handwritten beats digital; what to do when you cannot be there; whether to acknowledge a recent fight; how not to repeat last year.

## Phase 8 — remediation (1 September 2026)

The write-time record above stands as written. This section records what the fix pass
changed, so the brief no longer contradicts the JSON.

**Failure worked:** *"metaTitle differs from the H1, uses one separator, and carries a
differentiator no competitor could claim."* Closed, and without relaxing the
60-character working limit.

```
was:  Love Letter for Girlfriend Birthday — 7 Letters by Situation      60 chars, 514px
now:  Love Letter for Girlfriend Birthday: 7 Letters, 40–350 Words      60 chars, 525px
```

The differentiator is now the length range, which **Phase 2 gap #3 above already
establishes no ranking page can honestly claim**: not one of the eight results states how
long a birthday letter should be. "by Situation" was the weak half — `ai.tenorshare.com`
segments by relationship type — and it is gone. Keyword still exact in the first five
words; still differs from the H1; still one separator (the colon; the rule names only a
colon, em dash or parentheses, so the comma inside the differentiator is not a second
one); no booster, no year, no brand suffix, one keyword.

**Alternatives tested and rejected**, measured for character count and for Arial-20px
advance width against Google's ~580px desktop title budget:

| Candidate differentiator | Chars | Width | Rejected because |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 Letters by Situation, 258 Pages | 70 | ~600px | truncates — loses the number it was added for |
| 7 Letters and a 6.9-Hour Median | 69 | ~614px | truncates; unintelligible as a click promise |
| 7 Letters, 7.7% of 3,335 Pages | 68 | ~594px | truncates |
| 7 Letters from 258 Pages | 62 | ~550px | fits, but false — the seven letters are written, not drawn from those pages |
| 7 Letters, 258 Pages of Data | 65 | ~560px | fits, but the post carries no "258 pages of data" |
| 7 Letters of 40–350 Words | 62 | ~541px | fits the 70-char Strapi cap but breaches the 60-char working limit |

So the first-party-number route was tested, not assumed away: every framing that fits the
pixel budget misrepresents where the letters came from, and every truthful framing
truncates. A numeric constraint the SERP demonstrably cannot claim was the better trade.
