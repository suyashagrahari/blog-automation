## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes (must include or the post looks incomplete):** multiple complete letters rather than fragments; letters in actual Gujarati script; situational variety; some guidance on how to open and close a Gujarati letter (પ્રિય / વહાલી / લિ.).

**The gap — three things nobody covers:**

1. **Register.** Gujarati's second-person split (તમે respectful, used with elders in Gujarati families / તું intimate) carries real signal in a love letter, and **not one ranking page mentions it.** They print letters without ever saying which pronoun they chose or why.
2. **Script literacy in the diaspora.** Gujarati is heavily a diaspora language and a large share of fluent speakers read the script poorly or not at all. A letter in Gujarati script may be unreadable on her side, or untypeable on yours. **Zero coverage.**
3. **Writer gender.** Gujarati agrees with the *writer's* gender, so every copy-pasted letter online is silently written as a man. **Zero coverage.**

**Stale data:** not applicable — none of the six pages cites any data at all. There is nothing to supersede, which is unusual and is the whole opportunity.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** how to type Gujarati at all; whether romanised Gujarati is "cheating"; how long a letter should be.
