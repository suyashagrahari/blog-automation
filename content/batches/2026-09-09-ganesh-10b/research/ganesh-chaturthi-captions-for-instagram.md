## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present in all five, so the post must carry them): captions for
the sthapana photo, for a family photo, for modak, for the mandap/decoration, for
visarjan, a Marathi line, and something for a reel.

**The gap — four things none of the five say:**

1. **A caption is public, permanent and indexed.** A status disappears in 24 hours
   and a wish reaches one person; a caption sits on a searchable, hashtagged,
   undeletable-unless-you-delete-it post and is read by people you did not choose.
   Not one incumbent draws this line.
2. **Length.** Nobody names the 2,200-character ceiling, the 30-hashtag ceiling,
   or the point where the feed folds a caption behind "… more".
3. **Hashtag counts with evidence.** Every caption farm ends with a hashtag block
   and none of them says how many to use or where the number comes from.
4. **Reel caption vs photo caption.** "Ganpati reel caption" is in the keyword set
   and no incumbent treats a reel differently from a still photo.

**Stale data:** none — there is no data on any of these pages to be stale.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** whether hashtags belong in the caption
or the first comment; whether an edited caption is penalised; whether the same line
can be reused across eleven days.

**Fan-out sub-queries** (each becomes an H2 or an FAQ): what is a Ganesh Chaturthi
Instagram caption · how long should an Instagram caption be · how many hashtags
should I use · caption for a Ganpati reel · caption for a visarjan photo · Marathi
caption for Instagram · should hashtags go in the first comment · can I edit a
caption after posting · who can see my caption.

**Angle (→ `batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only Ganesh Chaturthi caption
page that treats a caption as a public, permanent, searchable post rather than a
list of strings — with the documented 2,200-character and 30-hashtag ceilings, a
peer-reviewed caption-practice study, and SubhSandesh's own 42.1% password-protect
rate as the deliberate opposite of publishing in public.

### Honest gaps in the evidence, stated in the post itself

- **The ~125-character fold is not documented by Meta.** It is a convention
  reported by social-media vendors and repeated everywhere; Meta documents only
  the 2,200-character ceiling. The post says so in the sentence that uses it.
- **The "3–5 hashtags" advice is vendor-published**, from Instagram's own creator
  account, not from peer-reviewed work. The only peer-reviewed hashtag-count
  finding available (source 2) points the other way — more hashtags, better
  performance — on Facebook, on 135 posts, from one brand.
- **No peer-reviewed study of Reels captions specifically was found.** Searched:
  `instagram reels engagement determinants`, `short video reels versus image posts
  engagement comparison`, `caption readability engagement social media posts`,
  `number of hashtags effect on engagement`, `instagram caption length likes`.
  Everything returned is either static-image research, health-communication
  content analysis, or vendor material. The post says the reel guidance is
  reasoning from platform mechanics, not from evidence.

## Phase 6 — Where this post is weak

The two strongest quantitative sources are both imperfect and the post says so in
the body: Gkikas et al. is Facebook, one brand, 135 posts, and was read
abstract-only; Oolo & Siibak is 15 Estonian teenagers in 2013. Neither is an
Indian festival-posting study, because no such study is openly readable. The
domain also has no Ganesh authority — the template shipped days before the
festival and has created zero pages — so every first-party number is platform-wide
and has to be captioned that way.
