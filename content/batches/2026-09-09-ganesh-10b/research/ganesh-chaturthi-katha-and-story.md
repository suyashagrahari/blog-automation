## Verified findings the post is built on

**From Michael 1983 (pp. 109–112), quoting Rao 1968 and D. Chattopadhyaya 1959:**
- "The explanation of the origin of Ganapati in the Puranas is confusing and contradictory."
- Śiva Purāṇa: the origin of Vighneśvara differs from aeon to aeon.
- Skanda Purāṇa: the birth is ascribed to Pārvatī alone; the same text declares him an avatāra.
- Liṅga Purāṇa: an aṃśa of Śiva takes handsome form and is delivered from Pārvatī's womb.
- Matsya Purāṇa: while bathing, Pārvatī forms the oil, ointments and impurity from her body into a man and animates it with Ganges water.
- A further account: Pārvatī makes the elephant-headed rākṣasī **Malinī** drink that mixture; Malinī conceives and bears the child, whom Pārvatī takes.
- Suprabhedāgama: Śiva says he and Pārvatī coupled in the form of elephants, hence the elephant face (Michael cites Getty 1936: 9).
- Dating: Bhandarkar — the cult must have come into practice between the end of the fifth and the end of the eighth century; Kane — the characteristics were fixed before the fifth or sixth century; Coomaraswamy — Gaṇeśa does not appear in iconography before the Gupta period.
- Michael's own thesis: elephant totem versus rat totem, the victory of one tribal confederacy over another.
- The Gaṇeśa Purāṇa and Gaṇapati Upaniṣad were "composed exclusively for the purpose of praising him".

**From Śiva Purāṇa, Rudrasaṃhitā, Kumārakhaṇḍa 13 (Shastri trans.):**
- v. 5: because kalpas differ, the birth of Gaṇeśa is told in different ways; in one account he is born of the great lord, his head is looked at by **Śani**, is cut off, and an elephant's head is put on.
- v. 6: the text then announces it will narrate the **Śvetakalpa** version, in which Śiva cuts the head.
- v. 20: Pārvatī creates the boy from the **dirt of her own body**, after her friends Jayā and Vijayā tell her that every gaṇa in the house answers to Śiva and she has no attendant of her own.

**From Gaṇeśapurāṇa (Sanskrit e-text, verified from colophons):**
- Upāsanākhaṇḍa runs to adhyāya **92** (`dvinavatitamo 'dhyāyaḥ`); Krīḍākhaṇḍa runs to adhyāya **155** (`pañcapañcāśaduttaraśatatamo 'dhyāyaḥ`). 247 chapters in total, counted from the text rather than taken from a reference work.
- Upāsanākhaṇḍa **50, 58, 59, 60, 69, 70, 73, 75, 76** are all caturthī-vrata chapters. 58 and 76 are explicitly **Saṅkaṣṭa Caturthī**; 60 is **Aṅgāraka Caturthī**.
- Upāsanākhaṇḍa **61** is `chandraśāpānugrahavarṇanam` — "the description of the moon's curse and its lifting". Content read in full:
  - the quarrel is over **a fruit** that Śiva gives to Kumāra instead of Gaṇapati (vv. 1–4);
  - Gajānana takes a terrifying form and obstructs Brahmā's creation; **the moon laughs at him** (vv. 5–6);
  - the curse (v. 7): *adarśanīyas trailokye* — "you shall be unfit to be looked at in the three worlds", and whoever sees you `mahāpātakavān bhavet`;
  - v. 25: `bhādraśuklacaturthyāṃ yo jñānato 'jñānato 'pi vā / abhiśāpī bhavec candradarśanād bhṛśaduḥkhabhāk` — on Bhādrapada śukla caturthī, knowingly **or unknowingly**, seeing the moon brings the curse and great suffering;
  - v. 31: `bhādrapadacaturthyāṃ tvaṃ na draṣṭavyaḥ kadācana`;
  - vv. 46–47: the penalty is named as `abhiśāpa`, `pāpa`, `hāni` and `mūrkhatā` — accusation, sin, loss, foolishness;
  - v. 48–49: on the **kṛṣṇapakṣa caturthī** the moon must on the contrary be looked at with effort, `viparīte vrataṃ vṛthā` — reverse it and the vrata is wasted;
  - v. 51: the moon takes his place on Gaṇeśa's brow and the sages name the form **Bhālachandra**.
  - **`syamantaka` appears zero times in either khaṇḍa of the Gaṇeśapurāṇa.**
  - Note the divergence from the popular telling: the cause here is a **fruit dispute**, not a fall from the mouse after eating modaks, and no tusk is thrown.
- Krīḍākhaṇḍa 106 is `śivalalāṭagatacandraharaṇam`; Krīḍākhaṇḍa 40 `vināyakāvataraṇam`, 130 `gajānanāvirbhāvaḥ`.

**From Bhāgavatapurāṇa 10.56–57 (GRETIL e-text, extracted and searched):**
- The Syamantaka narrative occupies exactly these two chapters, 10,666 characters in the GRETIL transliteration.
- Occurrences inside that span: `caturth` **0**, `gaṇeś` **0**, `gaṇapati` **0**, `vināyak` **0**, `bhādra` **0**. The single `candra` hit is `candrabhāgāṃ durgāṃ` (the goddess Candrabhāgā Durgā, 10.56.35) and the single `śaś` hit is the verb `śaśaṃsuḥ` (10.56.5). **There is no moon, no fourth lunar day and no Gaṇeśa in the Bhāgavata's Syamantaka story.**
- Conclusion the post draws, honestly limited: the two halves of the familiar katha come from two different texts, and their joining is the work of the later vernacular vrata-katha tradition, not of either Purāṇa.

**From Adhikary 2024 (Ruhuna 12(2): 93–98):**
- The Swasthani Vrat Katha is observed month-long during Māgh; the observance "involves fasting, prayer, and **recitation of the text**", and it is the recitation that "strengthens individual faith and communal bonds".
- The structured ritual, with its specific timings and recitations, works as "a living archive of cultural memory". Used as the generic evidence that in the vrat-katha genre the reading is the ritual act, not an accompaniment to it.
