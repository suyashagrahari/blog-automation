## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present on all or most): a list of Gujarati wishes; ગણપતિ બાપા મોરિયા; the
શુભેચ્છા formula; a nod to ten days and visarjan.

**The gap — four things nobody in the SERP does:**

1. **Roman transliteration + an English gloss per wish.** Every competitor gives Gujarati only. A
   second-generation Gujarati abroad who speaks the language but cannot read the script is locked
   out of all five pages, and so is any answer engine trying to explain what a wish means.
2. **The diaspora send.** The Gujarati diaspora is the audience these pages are written *for* and
   none of them address it — no time-zone note, no "they can read the script but their kids can't",
   no acknowledgement that the greeting is the maintenance mechanism.
3. **The script, mechanically.** Gujarati is Devanagari **without the shirorekha**, and no page says
   so or explains why that matters when your wish arrives as a JPEG instead of text.
4. **The calendar, correctly.** The received wisdom — including the framing handed to this post —
   is that Gujarat's Ganesh observance "runs straight into Navratri". **Verified false for 2026.**
   Pitru Paksha sits in between. See below.

**Stale data:** all five pages are undated or annually recycled; none cites anything.

### 1. Panchal & Shah (2025), "A survey on Gujarati NLP research work", *AiBi Revista de Investigación, Administración e Ingeniería* 13(1)
`https://revistas.udes.edu.co/aibi/article/view/4445` — DOAJ-indexed, peer-reviewed, open access.
**Subject test: PASS** (digital Gujarati). Verified from the article record:
- "Around 62 million people speak Gujarati worldwide, which places it 26th among the most spoken

- The survey covers GNLP research papers from **1999 to August 2024**.

### 2. Suthar & Thakkar (2022), "CNN-Based Optical Character Recognition for Isolated Printed Gujarati Characters and Handwritten Numerals", *IJMEMS* 7(5), 643–655
`https://ijmems.in/cms/storage/app/public/uploads/volumes/42-IJMEMS-22-0221-7-5-643-655-2022.pdf`
doi:10.33889/IJMEMS.2022.7.5.042 — peer-reviewed, open access, **full PDF read**.
**Subject test: PASS** (Gujarati script). Verified:
- "It is generally written from left to right and top to down **without shirorekha** on top of words
  like other languages Devnagri and Hindi."
- "A total of 34 Consonants 13 Vowels 15 Gujarati modifiers are present in Gujarati Script."
- "more than 46.1 million people in India Speak Gujarati"
- Recognition rates: **98.08%** printed characters, **95.24%** handwritten numerals.
- **Internal inconsistency flagged:** the abstract says "36 consonants, 13 vowels", the introduction
  says "34 Consonants 13 Vowels 15 Gujarati modifiers". The post cites the vowels-and-modifiers
  figure and the shirorekha statement, and does **not** state a consonant count.

### 3. Roberts, Mary Lucy (1999), *Immigrant Language Maintenance and Shift in the Gujarati, Dutch and Samoan Communities of Wellington*, PhD thesis, Victoria University of Wellington
`https://openaccess.wgtn.ac.nz/articles/thesis/…/16970086` doi:10.26686/wgtn.16970086 — open access.
**Subject test: PASS** (Gujarati diaspora language maintenance). Verified from the repository record:

### Verified but NOT cited (fact-check only, kept out of `sources` and `citation`)
Drik Panchang Pitru Paksha 2026 for Ahmedabad —
`https://www.drikpanchang.com/shraddha/pitru-paksha-shraddha-dates.html?geoname-id=1279233&year=2026`.
The post states dates from it but no time, so per `research-sources.md` it stays out of the citation
list. Verified: Purnima Shraddha **Saturday 26 September 2026**, running to **Sarva Pitru Amavasya,
Saturday 10 October 2026**.

**This is the finding that corrects the brief.** Ganesh visarjan is Friday 25 September 2026; Pitru
Paksha opens the very next day and runs sixteen days; Navratri's Ghatasthapana follows Sarva Pitru
Amavasya. So Garba begins in the **second week of October 2026, about four weeks after the Ganesh
wish** — Gujarat's Ganesh observance does **not** run straight into Navratri. What it runs straight
into is a fortnight of ancestor rites during which Gujarati families do not send festive greetings
at all. Stated in the post as the corrected version.
