## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present on all five; this post must cover them or read as
incomplete): worked reply text a reader can copy; separate handling of funny vs
sentimental; something for a short reply; the phrase "Happy Raksha Bandhan" itself
answered back; a note on Hindi/Hinglish phrasing.

**The gap — five things no ranking page touches:**

1. **The reciprocity mismatch.** She wrote three paragraphs; he wants to send two
   words. Every page hands him more quotes; none tells him the *size* of his reply is
   the message.
2. **Being late.** Nothing on the SERP distinguishes a reply sent at 10 AM from one
   sent the next evening or a week on. The floweraura page's replies are written as
   if the reader is answering instantly.
3. **Register matching.** All five sort replies by the *sender's* mood, not by the
   register *she* used. A funny sister answered with a sentimental paragraph is a
   mismatch, and it reads as a template.
4. **Family group chat vs direct reply.** Where the reply is visible to everyone
   changes what it must do. Not mentioned anywhere on the SERP.
5. **Distant or strained relationships.** All five assume warmth. None offers a
   reply that is honest when a warm one would be a lie.

**Stale data:** none of the five cites a statistic at all, so there is nothing to
supersede — but that also means the "current data" advantage here is unusually cheap
to take.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** "how late is too late?", "should I match
her length?", "is a voice note better than text?", "what if we barely speak?".

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2/H3/FAQ targets:** what to reply to happy raksha bandhan ·
