### The finding that decides the post

Every page treats "Hindi and English" as **two columns of the same list** — the same
sentiment printed twice, once in Devanagari and once in flat English. Not one of them says
what a shayari actually is (a *sher*: a self-contained two-*misra* couplet), names **radeef**
or **qaafiya**, or acknowledges that the English column is a different artefact rather than
a translation. The bilingual request is a *form* problem and the entire SERP treats it as a
formatting problem.

Two also carry a **third** script option none of them mentions: Roman transliteration, which
is how a large share of Hindi speakers actually read on a phone.

## The couplets — an honest note

**The five shayari are original compositions written for this post**, not sourced or
forwarded couplets. Three are in Devanagari with transliteration and an English rewrite, one
is Hinglish, one is composed in English. Item 3's rhyme (*hota* / *hote*) is deliberately
loose assonance rather than a strict qaafiya, and the body says so.

They have had **no native-speaker review**. A Hindi reader may find a phrase such as *"tu
hai meri nishaani"* closer to poetic licence than idiom. This is flagged in
`honestAssessment` as the single thing most likely to embarrass the post, and a native-speaker
pass is recommended before publishing.
