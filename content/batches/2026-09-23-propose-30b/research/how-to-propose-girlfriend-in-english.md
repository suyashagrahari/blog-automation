## Phase 2 — the gap and the angle

The searcher is usually an Indian speaker whose first language is not English, asking how to say
something enormous in a language they use for work and study and almost never for intimacy. They
are not asking for a translation. They are asking what natural English sounds like here, and how
not to sound like a greeting card or an exam answer.

**Fan-out sub-queries mapped to H2s / FAQs:** what does "in English" mean in this query · what do
I actually say · how much should I rehearse · what if I blank · in person or on a call · is
Propose Day 2027 a holiday · does "propose" mean marriage · which page fits which ask · what if
she needs time.

**Angle:** the only post that treats "in english" as a second-language *performance* problem —
rehearse the first sentence only, because preparation buys fluency and not accuracy, and the rest
will not survive contact.

## Phase 3 — sources

Search budget: **4 of 5 WebSearch calls used** (three for open-access locations of planning and
anxiety literature, one for the SERP pass). Everything else came from
`findpapers.mjs`, the ERIC API, the DOAJ API and Crossref. `capcheck.mjs` was run before choosing
sources and again immediately before writing the JSON; nothing used is at or over cap.

1. **Choi, Honeycutt & Bodie (2015)**, *Communication Education* 64(1), 25–44 —
   <https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1047692>. Rehearsal reduced **silent** pauses but **not vocalised**
   pauses; higher communication apprehension went with more total disfluencies.
   *Abstract read via ERIC; the Taylor & Francis full text is paywalled and was not read.*
2. **Véliz-Campos, Villegas & Veliz (2023)**, *Language Teaching Research Quarterly* 35, 37–56 —
   <https://www.eurokd.com/doi/10.32038/ltrq.2023.35.03>. n = 38 Chilean undergraduates; oral
   performance × FL learning-situations anxiety **r(7) = −.51, p = .032**; in-person anxiety
   higher than online on all five FLCAS dimensions but never significantly (ps ≥ .133). Full PDF
   read.
3. **Park (2021)**, *TESL-EJ* 25(2) —
   <https://tesl-ej.org/wordpress/issues/volume25/ej98/ej98a5/>. n = 48 Korean college students,
   10 minutes' pre-task planning. Solitary planning gave the highest pruned speech rate,
   **122.58 syll/min vs 111.83** for teacher-led (p = 0.027); **planning had no statistically
   significant effect on accuracy** in any condition. 80.56% found planning helpful; 58.33% of
   solitary planners rehearsed. Full text read.
4. **Sun & Révész (2021)**, *Canadian Journal of Applied Linguistics* 24(2) —
   <https://journals.lib.unb.ca/index.php/CJAL/article/view/31382>. 40 Chinese **child** EFL
   learners retold one task three times; task repetition improved fluency and accuracy, and
   first-performance trade-offs had decreased by the third retelling. Abstract and record page
   read. **Child participants — the body says so and frames the transfer as an inference.**
5. **Ariamanesh, Barati & Youhanaee (2023)**, *International Journal of Language Testing* 13(2) —
   <https://www.ijlt.ir/article_180547.html>. 96 Iranian TOEFL iBT candidates; the **online**
   planning group beat the pre-task group on accuracy and fluency across all four tasks; authors
   argue the 15–30 s prep windows are too short to help and stress-making. Full PDF read.

Subject test: all five are about rehearsal, planning or speaking anxiety in a second language.
Swap test: none could sit unchanged in any other keyword's post in this batch. Generic context
statistics: **zero**. Wikipedia body links: **zero** (four entity `sameAs` pairs live only in
`structuredData`, each verified against the Wikipedia API with its Wikidata QID).

Five distinct publisher domains; none appears in waves 1–2's 194 banned URLs. **One cap note:**
`capcheck.mjs` shows `files.eric.ed.gov` at 2 posts. This post cites `eric.ed.gov` — a different
hostname but the same publisher — which puts ERIC at exactly **3**, at the cap and not over it as
of the run immediately before saving. A fourth ERIC citation anywhere in this batch would breach

**Searched and found nothing usable:** `rehearsal difficult conversation study` and
