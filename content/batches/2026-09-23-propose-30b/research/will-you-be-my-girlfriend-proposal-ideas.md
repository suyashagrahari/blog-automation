### What each source actually carries

1. **Hoplock & Stinson.** 374 first-person accounts of accepted and rejected
   marriage proposals from online forums. 63% happened with only the couple present.
   Odds of rejection 1.73× higher when public (45% of 105 rejected accounts had others
   present vs 32% of 182 accepted). Ring offered in 76% of accepted vs 40% of rejected;
   kneeling in 90% vs 51%. 100% of accepted accounts that mentioned it had discussed
   marriage in advance, against 40% of rejected. 31% of couples kept dating after a
   rejection. **Correlational, Western, and about marriage — stated in the body.**
2. **Cast & Schweingruber.** Vignette study: couples conforming to the traditional
   engagement proposal script were rated stronger and more likely to last. Used as the
   *counterweight* — the script has a real payoff for the event it belongs to, which is
   why people reach for it.
3. **Brinig.** Diamond rings rare before 1930; 80 percent of brides choosing one by
   1965; the DeBeers campaign starting 1939, four years after the first states
   abolished the breach-of-promise action. Establishes the script's centrepiece as a
   recent, commercial, engagement-specific artefact.
4. **Moss.** Eight women aged 18–22; family cited as the most influential source of
   romantic relationship expectations, then peers, then media. Small and British —
   presented as a pointer, not a rate.
