**How this post is different — four separate axes, all deliberate:**

1. **Artefact.** Theirs is a *wish*: composed and addressed, no editing step. Mine is a
   *quote*: copy-text that arrives from outside and carries an explicit adaptation
   instruction — every line has a square-bracket slot and a "How to adapt it" field.
   The definition H2 states the distinction outright and links their slug for the
   reader who wants the other artefact.
2. **Organising principle.** Theirs groups by the sender's situation. Mine groups by
   **what the line asks the receiver to do** — remember something, accept credit, stay
   quiet, be seen, close a distance. No group in this post maps onto a group in theirs.
   The group that came closest (a "keep the volume low" set, which overlapped their
   "short and plain, for a boyfriend who hates performance") was **cut during drafting**
   for that reason; the post ships 15 lines in 5 groups rather than 18 in 6.
3. **Research lane.** Theirs is the *sender's* side — how a woman expresses. Mine is the
   *recipient's*: what lands, why an effusive line misfires, why specificity beats
   intensity. Zero source overlap: none of their five URLs appears here, and no
   affection-exchange, gratitude, quotation/formulaic, responsiveness, chronemics,
   choice-overload, greeting-obligation, emoji or calendar source is used.
4. **Line inventory.** All 15 lines are newly written for this post. None is a reworded
   version of any of their 20.

Where their page covers the adjacent case, this post links it rather than re-covering

## Phase 2 — angle

> Wins by being the only propose-day-quotes page for a boyfriend that groups its lines
> by the reception side — what the recipient has to do with the line — rather than by
> the sender's situation, grounding that split in memory-display experiments
> (N = 404 and N = 156, *British Journal of Psychology*, 27 February 2025) and a
> null-result Chennai study of 151 urban Indian men, alongside SubhSandesh's
> platform-wide 38.7% password-protection rate across 20 page types.

The three load-bearing claims that make the reception frame more than a rhetorical
device:

- **Sent ≠ received.** The LRMT paper's author describes watching one partner send
  authentic love messages the other does not see at all. That is the whole premise of
  sorting by reception.
- **Specificity beats intensity.** Memory display conveyed value *and* made other
  attempts at conveying value more effective — and people underused it unless prompted.
  That is why every group carries an adaptation instruction rather than a finished line.
- **Do not assume he cannot take it.** The Chennai null result undercuts the
  Gender-Role-Strain assumption these lists usually run on. It is one city, one snowball
  sample and a null, and the post says so.
