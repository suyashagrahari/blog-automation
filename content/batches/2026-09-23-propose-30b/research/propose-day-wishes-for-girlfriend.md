## Phase 3 — Research lane: perceived partner responsiveness

Assigned lane: **feeling understood, validated and cared for**. Usable thesis: what
makes a message land is evidence the sender was paying attention, not the elegance
of the phrasing — which is exactly the instruction a reader needs in order to adapt
a template rather than paste one.

Queried `findpapers.mjs` with all four assigned queries: `perceived partner
responsiveness study`, `feeling understood relationship satisfaction research`,
`responsiveness intimacy process model`, `validation in couple communication study`.
**Zero WebSearch calls used** — the SERP was already measured, so the whole search
budget was left for other agents.

Four sources kept, all 2026, all open access, all read in full text (not abstract
only), all verified against the 194-URL ban list with `capcheck.mjs` before and
after drafting.

1. **Emond, Ramos, Léonard, Péloquin & Daspe (2026)**, *Journal of Marital and Family
   Therapy* 52(3), doi:10.1111/jmft.70149 — observational dyadic study,
   **178 Canadian young adult couples** (n = 356). Communicated perspective-taking
   during conflict was positively associated with the speaker's **own and the
   partner's** relationship satisfaction, through greater positive relational
   experience during the discussion. Read at
   `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13238303/` (Wiley blocked, 403).
   *Carries the thesis:* saying out loud that you took her view registers.
2. **Conradi, Jonker & Noordhof (2026)**, *Family Process* 65(3),
   doi:10.1111/famp.70191 — two independent Dutch samples, **N = 848 and N = 938**.
   Relationship satisfaction explained **60%–71%**, need fulfilment **88%–89%**,
   support-giving 51%–53%. Extending the model with emotion regulation and repair
   added little; **extending it with validation did**, "emerging as an important
   novel finding". Read at `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13578553/`
   (Wiley blocked, 403). *Carries:* validation is the load-bearing ingredient.
3. **Yu & Li (2026)**, *BMC Psychology* 14(1), doi:10.1186/s40359-026-04257-3 —
   fNIRS hyperscanning of female dyads. The **responder's** left inferior frontal
   gyrus activation **mediated** the path from state-level empathy to intimacy.
   Read at `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13077900/`
   (bmcpsychology.biomedcentral.com returned a bot challenge). *Carries:* the
   active ingredient sits in the response, not the disclosure.
4. **Roos, Postmes & Koudenburg (2023)**, *PLOS ONE* 18(11),
   doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0292865 — two population surveys, **N = 194 and
   N = 1,000**. Feeling heard is defined as **five components at two levels**:
   having *voice*, and receiving *attention*, *empathy* and *respect*, plus
   *common ground* collectively. The resulting eight-item Feeling Heard Scale was
   **the strongest predictor of intentions for conflict behaviour among 15 related
   variables**. Read in full at
   `https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0292865`.
   *Carries:* the counter-case — a wish cannot manufacture the attention it proves,
   and the five components say which part is missing.

   **Source swap, recorded honestly.** This slot originally held Soyer & Ömür
   Sünbül (2026), *Frontiers in Psychology* 17, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1846877 — a
   survey of 446 adults on perceived partner phubbing, read in full and verified.
   It was dropped mid-run because three sibling agents wrote frontiersin.org
   sources concurrently, taking that domain to **4 posts against a 3-post cap**.
   The replacement is a better lane fit as well as a cap fix: feeling heard is the
   assigned phenomenon, phubbing was adjacent to it. Domain state at the final
   check: frontiersin.org 3, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov 3, journals.plos.org 1 — all at
   or under cap, and `pone.0292865` is not among the 194 banned URLs.
