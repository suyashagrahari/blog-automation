## Hindi verification — exactly how

Two corpora and one dictionary, all fetched, none taken on intuition.

**Instrument 1 — UD Hindi HDTB** (48 MB, `hi_hdtb-ud-train.conllu`). Every token, lemma
and `FEATS` bundle indexed, then each word of each drafted line looked up for form count,
lemma count and morphological features. This confirmed e.g. `बढ़ना` as `VERB|VerbForm=Inf`
(lemma ×88), `जवाब` as `NOUN|Gender=Masc|Number=Sing`, `बढ़ेगी` as
`Gender=Fem|Number=Sing|Tense=Fut`.

**Its limitation, found and worked around.** HDTB is a *news* corpus: `तुम`, `तू`,
`तुम्हारा`, `सुनो`, `चाहोगी` and `मानूँगा` all return **zero** occurrences, because news
prose is third-person. A treebank alone therefore cannot verify a proposal, which is
entirely second-person.

**Instrument 2 — Tatoeba Hindi**, 16,475 conversational sentences, tokenised and counted
the same way. This is what verified the second-person paradigms: `तुम` ×944, `तुमसे` ×41,
`तुम्हारा` ×93, `तू` ×57, `सुनो` ×9, `चाहोगी` ×3, `करूँगा` ×4, `सोचिए` ×2, `मज़ाक` ×26.
Two attested frames decided two of the published lines outright:
*क्या तुम कुछ खाना चाहोगी?* and *तुम जो भी कहो, मैं तो उससे ही शादी करूँगा.*

**Instrument 3 — McGregor, *The Oxford Hindi-English Dictionary*,** queried through the
Digital Dictionaries of South Asia. Every content word checked at the headword for
attested sense, not just existence: `पूछना` "to ask, to enquire (of, **से**)" — which is
why the line is `तुमसे`, not `तुमको`; `बढ़ना` sense 8 "to go forward or ahead"; `मज़ाक़`
"a joke; ~ करना, to joke"; `सोचना` "to think, to reflect"; `चाहना` "to wish, to desire …
to like, to love"; `तुम` "pron. (reduced hon. grade)"; `प्रस्ताव` "introduction;
commencement; **proposal (for discussion); motion; suggestion**".

That last entry, plus the 230 HDTB sentences containing `प्रस्ताव` — all parliamentary or
commercial — is the lexical finding the post is built on: **Hindi has no native verb for
the romantic ask**, which is why Indian English borrows *propose karna*.
