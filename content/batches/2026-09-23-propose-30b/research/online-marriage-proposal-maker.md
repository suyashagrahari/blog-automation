## Phase 3 — sources

Lane: **ritual, ceremony and what makes an act feel binding.** Queries run
through `findpapers.mjs` and Crossref/DOAJ/Unpaywall: `ritual commitment ceremony
study`, `wedding ritual meaning research`, `ceremonial acts social recognition
study`, `rites of passage transition research`, `public commitment ritual effect
study`, `psychology of rituals integrative review`, `collective ritual social
bonding empirical study`, `betrothal engagement ritual anthropology`, `ritual
formality witnesses commitment`, `virtual wedding ceremony zoom ritual pandemic
study`, `van Gennep rites of passage separation liminality incorporation review`.

Four sources, all peer-reviewed or scholarly, all open access, all fetched.

1. **Hobson, Schroeder, Risen, Xygalatas & Inzlicht — "The psychology of rituals:
   an integrative review and process-based framework"** — `https://osf.io/98v3f`,
   posted 31 March 2017, preprint of the article in press at *Personality and
   Social Psychology Review*. Full preprint PDF downloaded and read. Used for the
   definition of ritual (rigid, formal, repetitive action sequences; symbolic
   meaning; goal-demotion), the three regulatory functions, and the finding that
   group rituals shifted attitudes only when the ritual was sufficiently
   elaborate.
2. **Cho, Escoffier, Mao, Ching, Green, Jong & Whitehouse — "Groups and Emotional
   Arousal Mediate Neural Synchrony and Perceived Ritual Efficacy"** —
   `https://pure.coventry.ac.uk/ws/files/21824042/Binder1.pdf`, *Frontiers in
   Psychology* 9:2071, 26 October 2018, CC BY published PDF in Coventry
   University's repository. Full text read. Used for: 36 Chinese adults in
   Singapore rated ritual clips alone or in threes; arousal predicted efficacy
   ratings and "in groups, efficacy ratings converged".
3. **Mäder — "Promising images of love: a qualitative-ethnographic study about the
   mediatised memories of weddings"** —
   `https://open-research-europe.ec.europa.eu/articles/3-198/v2`, *Open Research
   Europe* 3:198 v2, 5 December 2024. Full text read via the PDF galley. Used
   for: 27 semi-structured interviews in Italy, Germany and Switzerland;
   photographing the wedding has become part of the rite of passage itself, and
   the record is what the day is remembered through.
4. **Kundtová Klocová, Kundt, Puryag & Lang — "Estimated costs and benefits of
   participation in an extreme ritual in Mauritius"** — Cambridge Core,
   *Evolutionary Human Sciences* 7:e29, 22 August 2025. **Abstract only** — the
   article page returned metadata and abstract but no body text from this
   machine, and the PDF galley timed out. Used only for what the abstract states:
   N = 369 Tamil and Christian participants; ingroup participants estimated the
   benefits of Kavadi participation as larger than outgroups did.

**Tests.** All four pass the subject test (ritual, ceremony, weddings as rites of
passage) — none is merely "about India" or "about the internet". None passes the
swap test: each is load-bearing for the specific claim that a page can supply
form and a record but not witnesses, and would be meaningless in a quotes or
wishes post. Zero generic context statistics. Zero Wikipedia links in the body.
Zero competitor citations.

**Caps, checked twice.** `capcheck.mjs` was run before choosing sources and again
immediately before writing the JSON. None of the four URLs appears in the 194
banned wave 1–2 URLs. `osf.io` stands at 1 other post in this batch (different
preprint: `v39tk`), `cambridge.org` at fewer than 2, `pure.coventry.ac.uk` and
`open-research-europe.ec.europa.eu` at none. No capped or crowded publisher is
used: no PMC / Europe PMC, no `frontiersin.org` (the Coventry repository copy is
cited instead of the Frontiers URL), no `journals.plos.org`, `arxiv.org`,
`sagepub`, `wiley`, `springer`, `nature.com`, `mdpi.com`, `dsal.uchicago.edu`,
`archive.org`, ERIC, `universaldependencies.org` or `doi.org`.
