## Phase 2 — cannibalisation: the three siblings, read in full

This is the most crowded cluster in the project. All three were read end to end before
a line was drafted.

| Sibling | Batch | Organising axis | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `virtual-proposal-card` | propose-12 (wave 1) | **Artefact format** — file vs link | Should I send an image/GIF or a hosted link, and which survives? Link rot, digital keepsakes, screenshot durability. |
| `online-proposal-link` | propose-12 (wave 1) | **Post-send control** | What happens to the URL after I send it — expiry, forwarding, chat preview leakage, deletion, shoulder surfing. |
| `will-you-be-my-girlfriend-proposal-page-online` | propose-30 (wave 2) | **Page composition + ask size** | Which six blocks go on the page, and why this phrasing wants `/love-gf` rather than `/proposal`. |
| `love-proposal-website-template` | propose-30b (this batch) | **Template literalism** | The seven slots a template hands back to the sender; mass-customisation and the IKEA effect. |

**The differentiator claimed here, and why it is real.** All four siblings assume a
reader whose relationship exists in a shared world and who is choosing a *delivery
mechanism*. None of them asks how the relationship was formed, and none changes its
advice based on the answer.

"Online" describes where the artefact lives. "Virtual" is the word people reach for when
the **relationship itself** is conducted at a distance — couples who met online, who have
never lived in the same city, sometimes who have never been in the same room. For that
reader the page is not a delivery mechanism, it is the **venue**: there is no in-person
moment afterwards to make it real, and no mutual friend group that watched the
relationship happen and can vouch for it.

That changes the content of the page, not its packaging. It is why this post organises
by **how the relationship was formed** (four rows in the table) rather than by format,
by block, or by template slot — an axis none of the four siblings uses — and why it
argues for an origin story told without embarrassment, ordinary-day detail as evidence
against idealisation, a question answerable alone across time zones, a dated plan behind
the yes, and a lock as the only private room the couple has.

**Verdict: distinct, and worth writing.** The research lane is also disjoint —
relationship *formation* online, which no sibling touches.

**Cross-links rather than competition.** The link/privacy mechanics belong to
`online-proposal-link`; the file-vs-link format question belongs to
`virtual-proposal-card`; the girlfriend-sized ask belongs to
`will-you-be-my-girlfriend-proposal-page-online`; the slot-by-slot template walkthrough
belongs to `love-proposal-website-template`. This post covers none of them in depth.

## Phase 3 — sources

Lane assigned: **relationships that formed online — how they start and how they fare.**
Deliberately avoided: digital possessions/virtual gifts, privacy calculus, mediated
co-presence, anticipation/savouring, LDR maintenance / media multiplexity,
mass customisation, ritual/ceremony, script theory, planning fallacy.

Search budget: **5 WebSearch calls used of 5 allowed** (1 SERP, 4 source hunting).
Thereafter `findpapers.mjs` (Crossref + Europe PMC), the DOAJ article API and the
Crossref journal API were used directly from the sandbox.

Queries run through `findpapers.mjs`: `relationships that began online outcomes study`,
`online dating relationship formation research`, `meeting online marital satisfaction
study`, `internet initiated relationships research`, `online relationship development
stages study`, `couples who met online marriage outcomes`, `online matrimonial websites
India partner search study`, `modality switching online dating partners first offline
meeting`, `self disclosure online relationship formation hyperpersonal`.

**Four sources, all open access, all read.** Every publisher is an institutional
repository, a university press or a fully-OA society journal — none of the crowded
publishers named in the brief, and `capcheck.mjs` was re-run immediately before saving
with all four domains clear.

1. **Rosenfeld, Thomas & Hausen (2019)**, "Disintermediating your friends", *PNAS*
   116(36) — read in full from the author's open copy at `web.stanford.edu`
   (`pnas.org` returns HTTP 403 from this machine; the PMC copy was unusable because
   `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` is at the 3-post domain cap).
   Used: 89.5% of couples who met online in the 2017 HCMST survey were previously
   strangers (81.0% in 2009); third-person intermediation fell 11.2% → 3.7%; about 39%
   of heterosexual couples formed in 2017 met online. Peer-reviewed.
2. **Kuperberg, Daniels, Curington & Lundquist (2026)**, "Partnering in turbulent
   times", *Demographic Research* 54(37), 10 June 2026 — full PDF read.
   Used: 11,993 students at 34 US colleges, 2019–2024; romantic relationship partners
   met online 20% (2019–20) → 25.8% (2020–21) → ~24% in the final year. Peer-reviewed,
   gold OA (Max Planck Institute for Demographic Research).
3. **Dröge & Voirol (2011)**, "Online dating: The tensions between romantic love and
   economic rationalization", *Journal of Family Research / Zeitschrift für
   Familienforschung* 23(3), 337–357 — full PDF read from the University of Bamberg
   press OJS. Used: couples can "become a couple before they actually met outside the
   Internet"; ~20 emails a day for months before any meeting; and the counterweight,
   one participant's later description of it as a "huge bubble" — "strange how one can
   be obsessed with an image of somebody". Peer-reviewed, DOAJ-listed.
4. **Tian (2026)**, "Love at First Swipe: Online Dating and Relationship Outcomes in
   Europe", SocArXiv, 15 August 2026 — **abstract read via the OSF API; the full
   preprint PDF was not fetched, and it is a preprint, not peer-reviewed.** Both facts
   are stated in the body prose and in the sources list. Used: across nine European
   countries in the Generations and Gender Survey, unions formed through online dating
   were associated less with poorer relationship quality than with how unions convert
   into marriage and shared childbearing.

**Subject test:** all four are about relationships formed online. **Swap test:** none
could sit in another post in this batch — quotes/wishes/shayari posts, language posts
and the template posts have no use for couple-formation data. **Generic context
statistics: zero.** **Wikipedia body links: zero.**

Searched and rejected: Cacioppo et al. (2013, PNAS) — the only readable copies are
`pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` (domain at cap) and ResearchGate (not citable); Danielsbacka,
Tanskanen & Billari (2022) — `tandfonline.com` returns 403; Sharabi & Caughlin (2017)
and the modality-switching literature — no open copy reachable; Walsh, Wolak & Mitchell
(2013, *Cyberpsychology*) — on-lane but an adolescent-victimisation study, wrong frame
for a proposal post; "Exploring Prejudice Toward Tinder" (2026) — apt, but `mdpi.com`
has one slot left in this batch and the source-cap race made it not worth the risk.
