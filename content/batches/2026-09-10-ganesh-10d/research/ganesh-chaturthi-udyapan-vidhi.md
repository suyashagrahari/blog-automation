## 0. The question the assignment said to settle first

> Verify from a citable source that udyapan is genuinely distinct from visarjan in this
> festival's practice. If sources treat them as the same act, abort.

### Finding: they are distinct, and one primary text settles it

**The settling source:** the *Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudrasaṃhitā 2.4.18 ("Gaṇeśa crowned as the
chief of Gaṇas"), in J. L. Shastri's English translation (1950), read in full at
<https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226138.html>.

Verses 38 and 47–53 were fetched and read verbatim. Śiva, laying down Gaṇeśa's worship,
prescribes:

- **v. 38** — "At the bidding of us both the Vrata shall be performed till the fourth day
  at the end of a year." The Gaṇeśa vrata is a **year-long, chaturthi-by-chaturthi
  observance**, not a ten-day one.
- **v. 47** — "he shall feed the brahmins joyously with sweets with due honour. He himself
  shall take sweets and avoid salt."
- **v. 48** — "Then the rites shall formally be dismissed. Then he shall remember Gaṇeśa.
  Thus the Vrata shall be concluded auspiciously."
- **v. 49** — "**When thus the Vrata is duly completed in a year, the devotee shall perform
  the rite of formal dismissal for the completion of the Vrata.**"
- **v. 50** — "At my bidding twelve brahmins shall be fed. After placing a jar your image
  shall be worshipped."
- **v. 51** — "After making the eight-petalled lotus diagram on the ground in accordance
  with Vedic injunctions a sacrifice shall be performed by the liberal people who have no
  disinclination to spend money."
- **v. 52** — "Two women and two students shall be worshipped and fed in front of the idol
  duly."
- **v. 53** — "The devotee shall keep awake at night and perform worship."

The phrase translated "rite of formal dismissal" is **udyāpana-vidhi**. That identification
is not mine: wisdomlib's Ganapatya glossary entry for the headword states it explicitly —
"Udyāpana (उद्यापन) refers to '(the rite of) formal dismissal' (for the completion of the
Vrata of Gaṇeśa), according to the Śivapurāṇa 2.4.18" —
<https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/udyapana>.

**Why this settles the distinction, concretely.** Nothing in the prescribed udyāpana touches
water or the murti's departure. It is a *year*-terminating rite made of: feeding twelve
brahmins, placing a kalasha, worshipping the image, drawing an eight-petalled lotus diagram,
a homa, feeding two women and two brahmacharis, and a night vigil. Immersion is absent from
the list. Conversely, visarjan is the murti's leave-taking at the end of its stay — the
sibling post `ganpati-visarjan-2026` documents six immersion days in 2026 and the water
chemistry, and never mentions a vow being terminated. **They conclude two different things:
udyāpana closes a vow; visarjana sends home a guest.** Same festival, two different clocks
(a year vs. one-and-a-half to ten days).

The same page's Molesworth Marathi–English entry gives the plain-language sense —
udyāpana, "The ceremony closing and concluding any religious observance" — which is a
category definition, not a Ganesha-specific one, and that is the point: udyāpana is the
generic terminator of a *vrata*, applied here to Gaṇeśa's.

**What I did NOT find, and it matters.** No source treats udyapan and visarjan as the same
act. But a large share of the live "udyapan" material is about **other chaturthi vratas** —
notably the monthly Sankashti Chaturthi cycle (janbhakti.com's page is entirely Sankashti)
and the fourteen-year Anant vrat. The assignment's warned failure mode is real in the
opposite direction from expected: the risk is not that the distinction is invented, it is
that readers searching this phrase are served udyapan instructions for a **different vow**.
That is now the post's second claim.

## 3. Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present across the pages that genuinely address udyapan): what udyapan
means; that a vrata's fruit is held to depend on concluding it properly; a samagri list;
brahmana-bhojana and dāna; reading the katha and doing aarti; when to do it.

**The gap — what none of them do:**

1. **Nobody cites a text.** Not one page in either SERP names a source for the Gaṇeśa
   vrata's udyāpana. The Śiva Purāṇa 2.4.18 prescription — one year, twelve brahmins, a jar,
   an eight-petalled lotus diagram, two women and two students fed, a night vigil — appears
   nowhere in the live results.
2. **Nobody separates the three endings.** Uttar puja (closing the day's worship), visarjan
   (the murti leaves), udyāpana (the vow ends) are collapsed or simply not mentioned
   together. A reader cannot work out which one they are being told to do.
3. **Nobody flags that most "udyapan" instructions on this query are for a different
   vrata** — Sankashti's monthly cycle or Anant's fourteen years.
4. **Nobody has any measurement** of the behaviour the post is about: what people actually
   do after the visible day is over.

**Stale data**: not applicable — none of the pages cite dated data of any kind, which is its
own finding.

**Unanswered questions the pages raise**: how many chaturthis make a completed vow; whether
udyapan is required if you never formally took a vow; whether a household visarjan on day
1½ ends anything; what to do if the year lapsed.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s/FAQs**: what is udyapan · udyapan vs visarjan · what the text
prescribes · how many vrats before udyapan · which month · samagri · can it be done at home
without a pandit · what if you missed it · is udyapan compulsory · Hindi phrasing.

**Angle (recorded as `batchMeta.angle`)**: wins by being the only page that prints the Śiva
Purāṇa 2.4.18 udyāpana prescription verse by verse, separates udyāpana from uttar puja and
visarjan in one table, warns that most ranking "udyapan vidhi" instructions are for the
Sankashti or Anant vrata instead — and sizes the after-the-day audience with SubhSandesh's
own festival lead-time measurement (11.2% of 89 Raksha Bandhan 2026 pages built after the
festival had passed).
