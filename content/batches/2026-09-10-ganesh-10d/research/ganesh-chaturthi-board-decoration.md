## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present across the image feeds and the one listicle, so the post must carry
them or read as incomplete): a Ganesha silhouette or cutout; marigold/paper flowers; a
greeting line in Devanagari or English; paper-craft elements (folded fans, quilling, cutwork);
a border treatment; the eco-friendly framing.

**The gap** — nothing on this SERP, or in any of the three siblings, states the constraints a
notice board actually imposes:

1. It is **unattended in a public corridor**, which rules out flame (diya, agarbatti) and
   water (fresh flowers in any reservoir) outright. Every home guide assumes both.
2. It must survive **ten days** — 14 to 25 September 2026 — of people brushing past it at
   shoulder height in a corridor.
3. The reader is **walking**, so the binding constraint is a word budget inside a glance, not
   a letter height.
4. It is built by **students or staff in about an hour**, from a stationery cupboard, not by a
   decorator with a budget.
5. **More decoration is worse.** Classroom-display research points the opposite way from every
   festive-decor listicle, and no page on this keyword mentions it.

**Stale data:** not applicable — the incumbent set cites nothing at all. There is no statistic
anywhere on this SERP to supersede, which is unusual and is the clearest signal that a
constraint-led post is the differentiated move.

**Unanswered questions the SERP raises and drops:** how much of the board to leave empty; how
to fix paper so it survives ten days without pin-tearing; what to do about the fire rule;
whether anything on a corridor board can be shared with someone not in the building.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:**
- What is a Ganesh Chaturthi notice board actually for, and how big is it?
- Why no diya and no water on a board?
- How many words can a walking reader take in?
- How much of the board should stay empty?
- What holds paper up for ten days?
- What can be built in an hour from a stationery cupboard?
- What does a board cost?
- How does anyone outside the corridor see it?
- When is a board the wrong answer?

**Angle (recorded as `batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only post on this keyword that
treats a notice board as a constrained public surface rather than a small mandap — no flame,
no water, ten days of corridor traffic, a word budget set by glance duration and a
wall-coverage ceiling from classroom-display research — against SubhSandesh's own measurement
that **no Ganesh Chaturthi page-creation data exists at all** and that occasion-dated pages
are 144 of 3,894.

### The search that came back empty, and the terms tried

Constraint two — surviving ten days of corridor traffic — has **no citable research behind
it.** Searched: *pin retention soft-board display*, *paper display fixing longevity*,
*flame spread combustible wall lining paper corridor fire growth*, *school fire safety
compliance India buildings*. The fire searches returned only NIST/NBS combustion reports
(source 5 above) and nothing on decorative paper specifically; the pin searches returned
nothing at all. So the pin spacing in the body (every 100 mm along the top, 150 mm down
the sides, through a folded 15 mm hem) is stated as workshop practice, not as a finding,
and the corresponding checklist item is recorded as **failed** with the condition that
would close it. That is a real result about the lane, not a gap papered over.
