## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all substantive results carry these, so the post carries them too): sample letters in Malayalam script; occasion variants (long distance, anniversary, everyday); a short/one-line option; text a reader can lift.

**The gap.** Nothing on the SERP covers any of:
1. **Second-person register.** No result mentions നീ / നിങ്ങൾ / താങ്കൾ. tenorshare's samples simply use നീ without saying so, which means a reader adapting them has no idea which knob they are turning.
2. **Script versus Manglish.** No result asks whether to write in Malayalam script or in romanised Manglish, or ties that to who the reader actually is.
3. **Whether she can read the script at all.** Speech and script literacy are separate skills; nobody raises it.

## Language discipline

Register chosen: **നീ** (*nee*), the intimate second person singular, and the choice is stated and justified in the body against താങ്കൾ (*thaankal*, honorific — elders, superiors, formal correspondence) and നിങ്ങൾ (*ningal*, polite plural).

Every Malayalam string was decomposed to Unicode codepoints in the sandbox and run through a deterministic ISO-15919-style transliterator, then compared with the hand romanisation. No string produced an unmapped codepoint, so script and romanisation agree character by character. The reader-facing romanisation is the everyday Manglish convention (doubled long vowels, ത = *th*, ച = *ch*, ം = *m*), and the post says so and says it is not a standard.

What that check does **not** establish is idiomaticity or register. Eight lines are flagged for native review in `auditReport.honestAssessment`, including the vocative എന്റെ പ്രിയപ്പെട്ടവളേ, the sign-off എന്നും നിന്റെ സ്വന്തം, the ഏറ്റവും ഇഷ്ടം superlative and the കൂടെയുള്ളപ്പോൾ temporal form.
