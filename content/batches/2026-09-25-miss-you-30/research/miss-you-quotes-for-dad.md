## Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all or most of the nine carry them): lines for a father who has died;
lines split from a daughter and from a son; something for the death anniversary; something
for Father's Day; short lines for a caption.

**The gap.** Nobody decides which reader you are. The query has two populations behind it
and every ranking page silently picks one — bereavement — without saying so in the title,
and three of them without saying so anywhere above the first list. A reader whose father
is alive and posted to another city lands on a page about a dead man with no warning; a
grieving reader on three of the nine lands in a product list with no warning either.
**The gap is the signposting, not the lines.**

**The second gap, specific to fathers.** Both merchifies pages gesture at "The Psychology
of Father Loss" and "The Continuing Bond", but none of the nine names the register
problem: a reader may be borrowing sentences their father never spoke in. If care arrived
as fees paid on time and very little said out loud, "you meant everything" is not a
sentence the two of them ever exchanged. That is the genuinely unwritten thing here, and
it connects to a real construct — unfinished business.

**Stale data / wrong claims.** Nothing dated is cited on any of the nine, which is itself
the finding: they cite no research at all. merchifies asserts "fifteen years of helping
families navigate grief" with no author page behind it.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s.**

| sub-query | H2 |
|---|---|
| what do I send my dad who lives far away | If your father is alive and living somewhere else |
| what do I write when my dad has died | If your father has died |
| why is it hard to say this to my father | The words he never used |
| is the death anniversary the hardest day | The hardest days, and what the record shows |
| are these quote pages any good | What the nine results on this search are doing |
| where does this site not help | Where this runs out |

**Angle sentence.** *Wins by being the only page on this SERP that separates the bereaved
reader from the reader whose father is alive and living elsewhere in the first 150 words —
0 of 9 results address a living father at all, 3 of 8 signpost neither reader — and by
refusing to put a celebration-product CTA on the bereaved half, citing the finding that
externalised continuing bonds predicted higher grief.*

**Shape bans obeyed** (BRIEF-WAVE2 §0): no mood or tone buckets; no "how to write your own"
workflow (handed off by cross-linking the live page, which already owns it); **no
attributed literary lines** — `blogs/miss-you-quotes.json` owns verified provenance with
book and year, and this post prints no author names at all and says why.

## Phase 4–5 — draft decisions

**The split is the post.** First 150 words state the two readers, name the split, and say
that the first-party data cannot see a bereaved sender. No scene-setting.

**`/missyou-gf` placement.** Mandatory batch-wide. Placed **once, in the living-father
section only**, after four borrowable lines have established what a short line does and
why a longer form might be wanted. Two caveats stated at the link: (a) it was built for a
partner, so the default register is romantic and has to be stripped out for a father; (b)
the median letter on it is 88 words, shorter than the wall of text a father of that
generation will read to the end. **Not placed on the bereaved half**, and the body says so
explicitly and gives the reason — a page you send is meaningless when nobody is there to
open it, and a page addressed to someone who has died is the externalised bond that source
2 associated with more grief, not less.

`/dedication` is the genuine alternative from `oneOfLinks`: for a father who has never
been a words person, a page that plays a song in your own voice asks him to listen rather
than to answer in a register he never used. `/templates` closes the "where this runs out"
section, attached to the admission that a memorial page is not on the site.

One internal blog cross-link: `/blog/miss-you-message-for-dad`, fetched 2026-09-25, HTTP
200, ~7,300 words. **It is NOT the banned mood-bucketed bank** — it is a compose-and-send
workflow ("How to say 'I miss you' to your dad in one clear line", "Tone examples by
relationship style", "When he's traveling for work", "For a military dad on deployment"),
with grief as **one subsection out of roughly twenty**. The split is therefore real and is
stated: that page is for a reader who has someone to send to; this page decides which
reader you are first.

**Sibling de-confliction.**
- `miss-you-appa-quotes-in-kannada.json` already splits bereaved from long-distance **for a
  father**. Its axis is lexical (Kittel's headword covering both distance and death) and
  its lines are dictionary-checked Kannada. This row's axis is the measured SERP failure
  plus the borrowed-register problem, in English. Different post, same duty of care.
- `-for-sister`, `-for-best-friend` and `-for-husband` all use "sorted by why they are
  away". **This post deliberately does not** — for a bereaved reader there is no
  reason-they-are-away to sort by, and forcing the shape would flatten exactly the
  distinction the post exists to make.
- `miss-you-quotes.json` owns verified literary provenance; this post prints no
  attributions and states why.
- `miss-you-quotes-for-mom` and `-for-grandmother` were **not on disk** when this was
  written, so no H2 comparison against them was possible. Flagged for whoever writes them:
  do not reuse "If your father/mother is alive…" / "If … has died" as H2s verbatim.

**Corrections to my own task prompt**, per BRIEF §0 — both carried in
`structuralLimitations` and in the body:
1. The prompt said the quote farms *skip* anniversaries, birthdays and Father's Day.
   **They do not** — measured H2s include "Death Anniversary Message", "Father's Day in
   Heaven" and "Dad In Heaven Quotes". What they skip is the **run-up** to the date, which
   is what the Swedish study actually points at.
2. The prompt framed continuing-bonds theory as straightforwardly "genuinely useful to the
   reader". It is the right literature, but the findings are split: sources 2 and 4 both
   associate stronger or externalised bonds with **worse** outcomes. The post reports the
   split.
