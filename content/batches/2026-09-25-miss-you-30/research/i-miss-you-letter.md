wave's BRIEF bans for me anyway.

**Decision: differentiate.** The split sentence, stated once in the post:

> The generator page produces a draft in seconds; this page is about what belongs in a
> letter you cannot take back once it is sent.

Cross-link: the generator page is named as the destination *after* the reader knows what
they are writing (`https://subhsandesh.in/blog/i-miss-you-letter-generator`). `/blog/`
links are excluded from the verifier's internal-link count, so this does not consume one

## 3. Correction to the prompt's "genuinely unwritten thing"

The prompt asserted that *"nobody on that SERP treats what you must not put in it"* as the
question. **That is half wrong and I am not printing it as stated.**

- `lovetale.app` (#9) carries an explicit **"Avoid This"** list: *don't guilt-trip them for
  being away · don't focus only on sadness without hope · don't make them feel responsible
  for your loneliness · don't be vague · don't forget to tell them you're proud.*
- `openyourletter.com` (#7) carries: *"Avoid making them responsible for fixing the feeling
  or using the letter to ask them to prove they miss you, too."*

So "what not to put in" **is** on this SERP, twice. What survives the check is the narrower
claim: **both frame it as tone advice that is equally true of a text.** Neither derives the
prohibition from the form — that a letter is finished before it is read, arrives once,
cannot be amended, and is kept. That derivation is the gap, and the post says so in the body
rather than claiming nobody mentions avoidance.

Also checked, as the prompt required, our two live long-form paragraph pages:
`/blog/long-miss-you-paragraph-for-her` (H2s include "8 simple steps", 25 tone-bucketed
examples, "Common mistakes and how to avoid them") and `/blog/miss-you-paragraph-for-him`
(8 steps, 55 examples in 11 tone buckets). **The "how to write" workflow is thoroughly owned
by live pages**, so this post must not be one. It is about the letter as an object.

Sibling boundaries held: `blogs/miss-you-quotes.json` owns verified literary provenance
(no attributed literary lines here — none printed); `blogs/how-i-miss-you-meaning.json` owns
the recipient side and the tells table (not restated).

Final four papers, all open access, all distinct journals, none previously spent in this
batch (checked by PMCID against all 35 sibling files):

1. `PMC9992917` — *Mindfulness* (Springer), 2023-03-08. RCT, n = 68 undergraduates in the
   top quartile for shame; a 16-day self-compassionate **letter-writing** intervention
   produced "medium-to-large reductions in global shame, external shame, self-criticism,
   and general anxiety at post-assessment", sustained at one-month follow-up. Used for the
   point that the letter form does measurable work on the **writer** — and that those
   letters were never sent to anybody.
2. `PMC8013690` — *Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being* (Wiley), 2021-02-17.
   Randomised online experiment, n = 738; **letter-writing to a future self** produced an
   "immediate decrease in negative affect and increase in positive affect", mediated by
   temporal distancing. Used for the open-when letter, which is addressed across time.
3. `PMC8749114` — *Pastoral Psychology* (Springer), 2022-01-11. "Viral Epistolary":
   **340 digital letters** collected across Italy during the first COVID lockdowns,
   thematically analysed; letters used "as a mediating, meaning-making, and
   (auto)biographical tool whereby people could share their experiences of domestic
   isolation and physical distancing". Subject test: letters under enforced separation.
4. `PMC13077900` — *BMC Psychology*, 2026-03-06. fNIRS hyperscanning of female dyads;
   "dyads in the self-disclosure condition reported higher levels of intimacy", with the
   **responder's** left inferior frontal gyrus showing the activation difference. Used for
   the point that disclosure buys intimacy through the other person's response — which is
   exactly what a letter defers.

5. `en.wiktionary.org/wiki/letter` — reference instrument, cap-exempt under this batch's
   `verify.config.json`. Read via the MediaWiki `action=parse` API.
