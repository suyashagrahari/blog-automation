## Phase 2 — gap

**Table stakes** (present on all or nearly all nine): a long bank of borrowable lines; a
long-distance grouping; a bereavement grouping; short/status-length lines; some funny
lines; a coping or "how to deal with missing him" section.

**The gap, after measurement.**

The wave brief — and my task prompt — asserted that no result on these SERPs asks *why*
the person is away. **On this SERP that is false.** momjunction, firstcry and siblingwish
all sort by structural reason ("the brother who lives away", "a brother who's grown
distant", "the brother you lost"); lifesuccessjournal runs fourteen such buckets including
long distance, military families, sibling rivalry, mentors, regret and reconciliation. The
situation axis is **occupied here**. Recorded in `structuralLimitations` and stated in
body prose.

What is genuinely unoccupied, verified across all nine:

1. **The register problem.** Nobody names it: between adult brothers the relationship often
   runs on banter and logistics, so a sincere line has no established register to land in
   and can read as an alarm ("is something wrong, are you ill, is it about money"). Our own
   #7 goes the other way and advises matching the existing tone, which is precisely the
   advice that keeps the channel closed. This is the most useful thing available here and
   it is brother-specific.
2. **A living/bereaved split placed where a grieving reader meets it first.** 6 of 9 mix
   the readers; 0 of 6 signpost in the first 150 words.
3. **Recipient-side and sender-side measurement.** No competitor publishes what happens
   after the line is sent.

**Angle sentence.** Wins by being the only page on this SERP that names the obstacle
specific to adult brothers — a relationship running on banter has no register for a
sincere line, so sending one reads as an alarm — and by splitting the bereaved reader from
the living-brother reader inside the first 150 words.

**Differentiate vs abort (BRIEF-WAVE2 §1).** **Differentiate.** Three clean splits against
our #7: it composes / this borrows; it sorts by mood / this sorts by what blocks the line;
it serves one reader / this splits two and gives the bereaved half no product link.
Cross-linked both ways.

**Lane held against siblings.** `miss-you-quotes-for-sister` owns married-and-moved and the
romantic-register misfire, so this row takes post-marriage drift between **two brothers'
households** instead, plus forces/merchant-navy, plus the banter register — none of which
is in the sister row. `for-dad` and `for-mom` own the bereaved/living split *method*, which
is inherited deliberately; neither is about siblings and neither uses these sources.

**Fan-out sub-queries** → H2s: is it weird to say this to a brother · what to say to a
brother who moved abroad · what to send a brother in the army · brother I have drifted from
after marriage · missing a brother who died · Raksha Bandhan lines · how long should it be.

**Bans observed** (§0): no mood/tone buckets; no "write your own" workflow (handed off to
our #7 by cross-link); no attributed literary lines — `blogs/miss-you-quotes.json` owns
verified provenance, and the body says why no line here carries an author's name.
