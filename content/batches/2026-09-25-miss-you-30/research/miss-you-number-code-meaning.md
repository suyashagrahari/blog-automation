## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present on 4 of 5): a list of codes with glosses; 143 as the
anchor; the letter-count derivation stated at least once; some reference to
"secret" codes between couples.

**The gap — three things, none of which appears on any reachable result:**

1. **No page verifies a single code against a reference work.** Nothing checks a
   dictionary, a statute or a corpus. Every gloss is asserted, and several are
   copied between pages (calendar-australia's 823 entry reproduces slang.net's
   house phrasing verbatim, down to "This is the most common definition for 823 on
   Snapchat, WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and Twitter").
2. **Nobody notices the collision.** "I miss you" letter-counts to **1-4-3** —
   the same number as "I love you". The scheme that produced the only attested code
   in the family physically cannot distinguish the two phrases. That single piece
   of arithmetic explains the whole SERP: why page one returns four different
   answers, why the dedicated glossary has none, and why the #1 result has to fall
   back on "trust me".
3. **Nobody looks at another language.** Mandarin numeric homophony is a real,
   dictionary-documented system, and it is the only place where a code for missing
   someone could plausibly exist. Checking it properly is the differentiator — and
   the honest result is a negative one (below).

**Stale data:** the #1 result is from 2012-09-18 and is about pagers. slang.net is
2023-08-10. Neither has been revisited.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:** is there a code for "I miss you"? / what does 143
mean / does 143 also mean I miss you / what is 607 / what is 823 / what does 187
mean / is 520 Chinese for I love you / what is 530 / what are the TikTok number
codes / what should I send instead.

**Angle:** wins by being the only page that verifies each code against a named
reference instrument, shows by letter-count arithmetic that "I miss you" and
"I love you" both encode to 143 so the scheme cannot separate them, and reports
the four incompatible answers its own page one gives — against 214 real miss-you
pages where the median one is edited over 2.6 hours.

- *"823 = thinking of you"* — arithmetic consistent, no dictionary attestation.

- *"187, 459"* — both confirmed, but as **California Penal Code sections**, from the
  statute itself, not as affection codes.
- *"07734"* — confirmed by construction, not by a dictionary.
