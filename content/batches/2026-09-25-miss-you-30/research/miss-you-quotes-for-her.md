## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (all ten cover): actual lines you can send; short vs long; a nod to
long-distance; something about timing.

**The gap.** Not one of the ten says anything about what happens *after* you copy a line.
They are supply-side documents. None of them mentions that the supply is public, indexed,
symmetrical across genders, and therefore recognisable. None recommends crediting the
line. None names a case where borrowing is the wrong move at all.

**Stale data.** None of the ten cites any data. There is nothing to supersede — which also
means there is no data floor to clear, only an evidence floor none of them reach.

## Phase 3 — Sources

Route used: Europe PMC REST search API (`ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search`),
zero WebSearch calls spent. **The `europepmc.org` article landing pages return HTTP 403 to
a scripted user agent**, so every title, journal, author list, date and abstract below was
verified through the REST API record rather than the HTML page. **All three research
sources were read as abstract and metadata only; no full text was parsed.** Recorded in
`batchMeta.sources` and in the audit.

Cap position checked before committing, per `verify.config.json`: `europepmc.org` is a
cap-exempt **repository**, and the cap applies to the journal named in the citation.
Journal counts across the batch after this post: Cognitive Research: Principles and
Implications = 1, Language and Speech = 1, **Frontiers in Psychology = 2** (cap 3 —
race risk, recorded in `honestAssessment`). `journals.plos.org` and `doi.org` were avoided
as instructed; no `frontiersin.org` URL is used.

1. **https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7656226** — Nadarevic, Reber, Helmecke & Köse,
   *Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications* (BMC), 2020-11-11.
   Four experiments. Source credibility and repeated exposure had strong **additive**
   effects on perceived truth; **congruent (verbatim) repetition increased** perceived
   truth while **semantically incongruent repetition decreased** it; statements presented
   **without source information were judged less true** than the same statements from a
   credible source. Presentation format (picture or not) had no effect.
   *Subject test:* about how a received statement is judged — the exact mechanic of a
   borrowed line. *Swap test:* could not sit in a language-etymology row in this batch.
   *Carries the post's central recommendation:* no-source statements fared worst, which is
   the evidence behind "credit the line".

2. **https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8406370** — Kessler, Weber & Friedrich,
   *Language and Speech* (SAGE), 2020-07-27.
   Eye-tracking plus two ERP experiments on German idioms with the final word excised.
   Participants **fixated the correct completion well before the fragment ended**, with an
   early bias toward semantic associates; ERPs showed facilitated processing of correct
   completions. Formulaic language is predicted ahead of its arrival.
   *Used for:* why a ready-made opener is safe in first position and useless in last.

3. **https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7251183** — Bonensteffen, Zebel & Giebels,
   *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2020-05-20.
   58 students took the victim role in a fictitious crime scenario and watched a video of
   a remorseful apology. **Prior expectations**, attitudes toward resocialisation
   programmes, and inferences about the speaker's suffering and responsibility-taking all
   positively predicted **perceived sincerity**; gaze fixation duration was **not**
   directly associated with those inferences.
   *Used for:* half of how the line lands is what she brings to it — a simulation with
   students, not couples, and the post does not claim otherwise.

4. **https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/miss** — reference instrument, cap-exempt, fetched in
   full via the MediaWiki API. Noun from Old English *miss* "loss, absence"; verb from Old
   English *missan* "to miss, escape the notice of a person", from Proto-Germanic
   \*missijaną. Cognates: Danish/Norwegian Bokmål *miste*, Icelandic *missa*, both "to
   lose". The verb also carries "to become aware of the loss or absence of; to feel sadness
   at the absence of somebody" **and** "to fail to notice; overlook". No publication date
   is stated by the source, so none is recorded.

**Search terms tried that returned nothing usable**, recorded so the next agent does not
repeat them: `perceived effort AND gift`, `self-disclosure AND romantic AND text
