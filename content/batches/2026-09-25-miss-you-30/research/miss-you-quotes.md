## Phase 1 — SERP

Head-term flag, per the skill's own rule. `miss you quotes` is a 3-word near-head term
in band 5 (10,000+/mo EST). It is **not** a long-tail keyword and it is not treated as
one here. The measured US-served SERP of 2026-09-25 was supplied in the brief and was
re-run once to confirm it. It came back identical, position for position:

|---|---|---|
| 1 | rd.com | 53-item editorial listicle |
| 2 | marriage.com | 150+ item listicle |
| 3 | brainyquote.com | quote database topic page |
| 4 | pinterest.com | user board |
| 5 | thoughtcatalog.com | 66-item listicle |
| 6 | adobe.com/express/discover/quotes/i-miss-you | template-marketing quote page |
| 7 | ibme.utk.edu | off-intent university page ("we will miss you quotes") |
| 8–9 | goodreads.com ×2 | tag/aggregator pages |

Per `references/competitors.md` none of these is cited, linked or paraphrased. No
structure or section order was taken from them; they were used only to establish that
every ranking page is an undifferentiated, unsourced list of lines.

Two of them are genuine authorities (rd.com, brainyquote.com). The weak slots are
Pinterest, the two Goodreads aggregators and the off-intent `.edu`. Overall weakness
4/10 — contested.

**A useful confirmation fell out of the SERP run itself.** The search summary surfaced
six sample lines, and four of them are exactly the failure mode this post is about: a
Toni Morrison line that cannot be checked against an openly readable text, a 1995 song
lyric presented as a quotation, an unattributed line, and a Lamartine line given in an
English wording Lamartine never wrote.

## Phase 2 — gap and angle

**Table stakes:** a substantial number of quotable lines, grouped so a reader can scan;
short lines for a status or caption; something for a partner and something for a friend.

**The gap:** not one ranking page states where a line comes from. No book, no year, no
edition, no link to a text the reader can open. Quote listicles are the single most
misattribution-prone genre on the web, and the entire SERP is built on trust.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:**

| Sub-query | H2 |
|---|---|
| what makes a quote trustworthy | What makes a miss you quote verified |
| give me the quotes | Ten miss you quotes with the book, the year and the check |
| which famous ones are fake | The five quotes I cut, and what was wrong with each |
| how long should my message be | How long should the message around the quote be? |
| does anything come back | What the recipient does after they read it |
| text vs page | A quote in a text versus a quote on a page |
| when not to use a quote | When a quote is the wrong thing to send |

**Angle:** wins by being the only post on this SERP that prints the book, the year and
an openable primary text for every line, records the five it cut for failing that check,
and sets the quotes against 214 measured miss-you pages.

Every printed line was located by exact string search inside a full copy of the named
work. Eleven primary texts were fetched and searched; six are linked in the body, which

Two further findings that survived the check but needed a **context** correction rather
than a cut: Barrett Browning XXXIII and Tennyson XXVII are both about the dead, and the
Dickens line is a character's hope, not the author's verdict. Both are stated in the body.

**Sources (6).** Subject test: all six are about either quotation accuracy or the exact
texts the quotes come from. Peer-reviewed open-access: the BMC meta-analysis (CC BY).
Generic context statistics: zero. Wikipedia body links: zero.

1. `researchintegrityjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s41073-025-00173-z` —
   "Systematic review and meta-analysis of quotation inaccuracy in medicine",

   quotations incorrect across 46 studies and 32,000 quotations/references; ~8.0% major
   errors; no improvement over time. **Abstract only** — the publisher page returned a
   bot-challenge interstitial, so the abstract was read from the Europe PMC record of the
   same article. Recorded as a failure against checklist item 25.
2. Gutenberg 1105 — *The Sonnets*, Shakespeare
3. Gutenberg 70950 — *In Memoriam*, Tennyson
4. Gutenberg 6686 — *The Gardener*, Tagore
5. Gutenberg 67098 — *Winnie-the-Pooh*, Milne (negative check)
6. fr.wikisource — *Méditations poétiques* (1820 first edition), "L'Isolement"

## Cannibalisation

81 live slugs carry `miss`. `miss-you-quotes` itself is free in Strapi (checked). The
five closest live English posts carry "miss you quotes" as a *secondary* keyword:
`cute-miss-you-messages`, `heart-touching-miss-you-messages`,
`miss-you-message-for-long-distance`, `miss-you-messages-for-her`,
`miss-you-message-good-morning`. Five more are the language axis
(`miss-you-quotes-in-bengali/kannada/malayalam/tamil`, `-telugu-for-love`) and are not
duplicates of an English post.

The three closest were fetched from Strapi and read in full:

| Slug | Words | What it is | Attributed quotations |
|---|---|---|---|
| `heart-touching-miss-you-messages` | 1,760 | 120+ original lines to copy, plus a writing formula | 0 — the string "quote" does not appear |
| `miss-you-messages-for-her` | 1,390 | 150+ original lines by tone, plus a three-part formula | 0 |
| `cute-miss-you-messages` | 1,737 | 120+ original short lines by situation | 0 |

None of the three prints a single attributed line from a published work; all three are
message generators, and none contains the word "quote" in its body. The honest
difference is a content-type difference, not a tone difference: those posts supply lines
for the reader to send as their own, this one supplies other people's lines with the
book, the year and a text the reader can open, and documents what it threw away. The
merge recommendation was considered and rejected on that basis.
