## Phase 2 — Gap

**Table stakes** (present across the result set, so the post must cover them or
read as incomplete): a concrete list of things to send; the "will they even get
it?" worry; the distinction between romantic and platonic use; what to do when
the hint lands badly.

**The gap.** Every ranking page answers "say it with different words." Nobody
answers "carry it without words." The whole literature that *is* about this —
nonverbal cues substituting for verbal content, affectionate communication,
symbolic gifts — is absent from the SERP entirely.

**Stale data.** Not applicable: there is no data on the SERP to supersede.

**Fan-out sub-queries** → these became H2s and FAQs:

- what does "saying it without saying it" actually mean → H2 2
- does an indirect signal actually read as affection → H2 3
- what specifically do I send → H2 4 (seven H3s)
- which one should I pick → H2 5 (table)
- what if it makes them uncomfortable → H2 6
- when should I just say it → H2 7
- where do I put it so it survives → H2 8

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that treats "saying it without the
sentence" as the measured phenomenon it is — nonverbal cues raising *perceived
responsiveness* (Cohen's *d* = 1.05, N = 260) — and pairs it with behaviour no
competitor can measure: 13.6% of 214 SubhSandesh miss-you senders record a voice
note, 28.0% set a reunion countdown, and 1,434 hug taps came back across 138 of
those pages.

## The line between this post and its batch sibling

`how-to-say-i-miss-you-without-sounding-desperate` answers a question about
**tone**: how do I phrase the sentence so I do not read as needy. It quantifies
that with Starr & Davila's meta-analytic *r* = .32 (reassurance-seeking ↔
depression) against *r* = .14 (reassurance-seeking ↔ rejection), and with the
RoSS scale's directness-versus-hinting result.

This post answers a question about **channel**: the searcher has already decided
not to use the sentence. None of the sibling's four sources appears here, and
the sibling's central finding is referenced once, as a pointer, and not
re-explained.

## Phase 3 — Sources

Four sources. All four fetched and read in full text — none paywalled, so no
abstract-only disclosure is needed.

1. **Huh, E. (2025). "The impact of emojis on perceived responsiveness and
   relationship satisfaction in text messaging." PLOS One 20(7): e0326189,
   published 2025-07-02.**
   `https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326189`
   N = 260 MTurk participants, US, 15 text conversations, two conditions.
   Verified in full text: emoji messages rated more responsive (*M* = 4.43,
   *SD* = .71) than text-only (*M* = 3.57, *SD* = .93), *t*(258) = 8.90,
   *p* < .001, Cohen's *d* = 1.05. Mediation (PROCESS Model 6): indirect effect
   of emoji use on closeness through perceived responsiveness *b* = .52, 95% CI
   [.44, .61]. **H2 was rejected** — perceived responsiveness did *not*
   significantly predict likability, *b* = 0.18, *p* = .10. Sample is 93.5%
   White/Caucasian.
   *Subject test:* yes — nonverbal cues substituting for words in text.
   *Swap test:* could not sit in a Bisaya, Korean, Spanish or Kannada post.

2. **Kocur, D., Jach, Ł., Sitko-Dominik, M., Dhakal, S., Sharma, N., Harsono,
   Y. T., et al. (2025). "To hug or not to hug? Public and private displays of
   affection and relationship satisfaction among people from Indonesia, Nepal,
   and Poland." PLOS One 20(6): e0326115, published 2025-06-25.**
   `https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326115`
   N = 461 (170 Indonesian, 120 Nepali, 171 Polish), aged 18–49, heterosexual
   relationships only. Verified in full text: both private and public displays
   of affection correlated positively with relationship satisfaction in all
   three samples, **weakest in Nepal**; Nepali participants reported the
   *highest* relationship satisfaction despite lower displays than Poland.
   *Subject test:* yes — wordless affection display ↔ satisfaction, with a
   South Asian sample.

3. **Shahrier, M. A. & Khatun, S. (2025). "Development and validation of the
   gift reciprocation anxiety scale (GRAS) for youths and adults in intimate
   relationships." Heliyon 11(2): e41956, published 2025-01-14. Both authors,
   Department of Psychology, University of Rajshahi, Bangladesh.**
   `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787505/`
   N = 763 Bangladeshi participants, 17–36, convenience sample, 67.76% male.
   Verified in full text: 7-item single-factor scale, Cronbach's α = 0.82,
   McDonald's ω = 0.82, composite reliability 0.87. Depth of attachment ×
   GRAS *r* = −0.43, *p* < .001, 95% CI (−0.37, −0.49). Convergent validity
   correlations with DASS-21 were weak: depression *r* = 0.10 (*p* < .01),
   anxiety *r* = 0.09 (*p* < .05).
   *Subject test:* yes — the obligation a sent object creates between partners.

4. **Clark, A. N. & Lefkowitz, E. S. (2024). "Sexual and Affectionate Behaviors
   and Satisfaction for Adults in Romantic Relationships: A Latent Profile
   Analysis." Archives of Sexual Behavior 54(1): 175–188, published online
   2024-10-15.**
   `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11782417/`
   N = 336, 85.4% LGBTQ+, 91.5% White, *M*age = 29.07, committed relationships
   of 6+ months. Verified in full text: three-profile solution. Profile 2,
   "Affection-focused Behaviors," is 35.84% of the sample (*n* = 119) — frequent
   affectionate behaviours, infrequent sexual behaviours. Adults in the
   "Infrequent Physical Behaviors" profile (15.96%, *n* = 53) were *less*
   relationally satisfied than adults in the other two profiles.
   Only the affection half of this paper is used.

### Searches run and what they did not turn up

`findpapers.mjs` (Crossref + Europe PMC, no WebSearch cost) was run on:
`affectionate communication nonverbal partners`,
`relational maintenance mediated channels`,
`media multiplexity tie strength`,
`symbolic gift giving romantic relationships`,
`affection exchange theory affectionate behavior`,
`music sharing social bonding relationships`,
`social media relational maintenance romantic partners`,
`voice message vocal cues social connection humanizing`.
OpenAlex was queried directly for open-access status on each candidate DOI.

**Media multiplexity has no open-access empirical base, and that is a real
finding.** The task brief called media multiplexity "the empirical backbone of
'send the song instead'." The three papers that actually test it are all closed:
Ledbetter (2011) *Communication Reports*, doi:10.1080/08934215.2011.555322,
`is_oa: false`; Ledbetter & Kuznekoff (2016) *New Media & Society*,
doi:10.1177/1461444816638458, `is_oa: false`; Ledbetter (2023) *New Media &
Society*, doi:10.1177/14614448231207100, `is_oa: false`. Stafford & Canary's
relational-maintenance typology is likewise book- and paywall-bound, and the one
open dyadic long-distance maintenance study found (Interpersona 17(2), 2023,
doi:10.5964/ijpr.9771) sits behind a login wall on both the article page and the
PDF endpoint. Rather than cite papers the reader cannot open, the post drops
both constructs and leans on the emoji/responsiveness result, which is fully
open and directly about substituting a nonverbal cue for words.

Floyd et al. (2022), `iris.unil.ch/handle/iris/190955`, was **available** to
reuse (the domain is at 1 of 3 posts in this batch) and was deliberately not
used: the evidence-base ceiling it documents is already visible in this post's
own sources — 93.5% White in the emoji study, 91.5% White and 85.4% LGBTQ+ in
the affection-profile study — so citing it would have been decoration.

recommendation is **not** a merge.

sentences and internally linked here, rather than two pages on the same domain
