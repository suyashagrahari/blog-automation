## Phase 2 — gap and angle

**Table stakes** (all nine): a bank of short lines; a long-distance grouping; an
after-a-fight grouping; a note on length; an FAQ block.

**The gap.** Not one of the nine is written from the seat of the person who is
*away*. Every page assumes the searcher is at home and the wife is elsewhere. In
the Indian case the reverse is the common one: he is the migrant, she is at home,
frequently inside his parents' household, and she is busier than he is.

**Also unwritten:** nobody asks what the recipient is doing when the line lands.
Mood buckets describe the sender's feeling; none of the nine describes the
recipient's room, audience or remaining minutes.

**Angle.** Wins by being the only page on this SERP written from the position of
the person who left, sorting borrowed lines by **what she is doing when the line
arrives** rather than by mood, grounded in 214 measured miss-you pages and in the
1688 coinage of *nostalgia* as a diagnosis of the one who went away.

**Axis de-confliction across the cluster:**

| Row | Axis |
|---|---|
| `for him` (anchor) | why the person is away (fixed trip / open-ended / fight / no absence) |
| `for her` | delivery (bare / credited / framed / do-not-borrow) |
| `for sister` | structural reason for absence (married out / relocated / not speaking) |
| `for husband` | why *he* is away (contract / weekday city / moved date / co-resident) |
| **`for wife` (this)** | **what the recipient is doing when it arrives** |

Only this one is keyed to the recipient's circumstances rather than the sender's.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:** who actually sends these; what a quote is and cannot
do; which line for which moment; how long; song vs. text; what the recipient does
next; what the ranking pages are doing; where the advice runs out.

**Inherited lane sentence** (from the anchor, not re-invented): *a message is
something you compose and send; a quote is someone else's exact words that you
borrow and pass on.* Grounded here on `nostalgia` rather than by re-citing the
anchor's `wiktionary/quote` entry for a third time.

## Templates and the link-placement call

- `/missyou-gf` — mandatory, and the thing all 214 numbers measure.
- `/dedication` — **the husband row also uses it, so it is justified differently
  here.** Behavioral Sciences 2025 (n=2,493) found off-work music listening
  *strengthened* the homesickness→burnout link, so the post argues that looping the
  music of home alone is the wrong instinct and a dedication is the opposite
  operation — handed over, with the sender's own recorded voice on the front. That
  argument is only available on the leaver's row.
- `/watch` — chapters, for a recipient with four minutes now and the rest after the
  house is asleep. Same axis.
- Plus one blog cross-link to `/blog/miss-you-message-for-wife`. Four internal
  links total.

**Phase 0 ↔ Phase 5 tension, recorded.** Phase 0 requires two first-party numbers
inside the first 150 words; those numbers measure `/missyou-gf`; Phase 5 wants
internal links after value is established. **Call: separate the number from the
link.** The intro names "our own miss-you template" in plain prose and carries both
numbers; the `/missyou-gf` link itself is held to the recipient-behaviour section,
after the hug-tap figure establishes why the page format matters. The item is left
in `failed` rather than claimed as a pass.

## Framing claim in the task prompt — checked

The prompt asserted (a) the person missing a wife is usually the one who left, and
(b) nobody on either SERP writes the leaver's side.

**(b) is measured and true** — none of the nine readable pages is written from the
position of the person who is away. **(a) is a plausible inference from
male-dominated labour migration but was not measured by this post**, so the body
states it as "most likely" / "usually" rather than as a statistic, and an FAQ
explicitly covers the reversed case of a wife who travels for work.
