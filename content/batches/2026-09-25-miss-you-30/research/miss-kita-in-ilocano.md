## Phase 3 — Verification log

### The pronoun question — answered NO

`en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Template:ilo-personal_pronouns` carries **two** grids. The first is
the ordinary Ilocano personal-pronoun paradigm; the second is headed **Fused enclitics** and
is an actor × patient matrix.

| Actor → Patient | Form |
|---|---|
| *siak* (I) → *sika* (you sg) | **-ka** |
| *sika* (you sg) → *siak* (me) | **-nak** |
| *isu* (he/she) → *sika* (you sg) | **-naka** |
| *dakami* (we excl.) → *sika* (you sg) | **-daka** |
| *siak* (I) → *dakayo* (you pl) | **-kayo** |

**No cell produces `kita`.** Ilocano's I-to-you fused enclitic is `-ka`. The Tagalog
portmanteau `kita` has no Ilocano counterpart of the same shape.

Supporting entries fetched individually:

- `-ak` — "First-person singular **absolutive** enclitic; I, me". Examples *Pilipinoak*
  ("I am a Filipino"), *Kinagatak ti aso* ("The dog bit me").
- `-ko` / `-k` — first-person singular **ergative** enclitic and possessive marker.
- `-mo` / `-m` — second-person singular ergative enclitic and possessive marker.
- `-na` — third-person singular ergative enclitic.
- `kenka` — "Second-person singular **oblique** pronoun; to you, for you", etymology
  *ken* + *-ka*, synonym *kaniam*, example *Intedda kenka* ("They gave it to you"). Fetched
  and read, then **not listed in `sources`** to stay inside the 4–6 cap (see cuts).

### What `kita` is in Ilocano

`en.wiktionary.org/wiki/kita` → Ilocano section, **two noun etymologies, no pronoun**:

1. *kíta* (pl. *kitkita*), from Proto-Malayo-Polynesian: "kind; class; species"; "look;
   shape; appearance". Derived: *agkita, kumita, makita, kitaen, ipakita, agpakita,
   pagkita, pakakitaan, pakakitkitaan*.
2. *kíta* (pl. *kitkita*): "salary; wages", synonym *sueldo*. Derived: *kumita, kitaen,
   kumitaan*.

Every derived term in set 1 concerns seeing or showing. `miss kita` read as Ilocano is an
English verb plus a noun meaning "appearance".

### The native sentence — `Mailiwak kenka` CHECKS OUT

Tatoeba API, `from=ilo`:

- Ilocano corpus total: **1,000 sentences**.
- Query `mailiwak` → **1 hit**, sentence **4632744**, contributor `saluyot`, licence
  **CC BY 2.0 FR**: **`Mailiwak kenka.`** English translation: **"I miss you."**
- Query `mailiw` → 0 hits. Query `iliw` → 0 hits. Query `malagipka` → 0. Query `iliwek` → 0.
- Query `kenka` with `from=ilo` → **35 hits**, including:
  - `Mamatiak kenka.` → "I believe you." (4632773)
  - `Umanamongak kenka.` → "I agree with you." (4632782)
  - `Agpannuray kenka ti ragsakko.` → "My happiness depends on you." (4680618)
  - `Ania ti adda kenka?` → "What do you have?" (4674455)
  - `Asino ti nangawag kenka?` → "Who called you?" (4668687)

So the `V-ak … kenka` frame is **productive**, not idiomatic to this one sentence. That is
the structural finding the post is built on: because `-ak` is absolutive and the second
person sits in the oblique, **Ilocano does not use a transitive frame here at all.**
