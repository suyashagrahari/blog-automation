### Verdict recorded

The intent is **genuinely split**, and the memorial half currently lives on
platforms — Instagram, Pinterest, TikTok, YouTube — rather than in articles. The
orchestrator's framing ("the SERP is dominated by memorial/death-anniversary content")
was **not confirmed** by the unbiased search. The post therefore serves both readers
and separates them into two explicitly labelled H2 sections, leading with the
bereaved reader because that reader is the one the article-format web is failing.

## Phase 2 — Gap

**Table stakes** (what the aggregators all carry): Kannada script lines, a
transliteration, a father-affection register, short shareable length.

**The gap, and it is large.** Not one ranking result:

- says what any of its lines means according to a source anyone can check;
- distinguishes a line for a dead father from a line for a living one;
- explains *why* the query's intent is split.

**Fan-out sub-queries** an answer engine would decompose this into, each of which
became an H2 or an FAQ: *what does appa mean in Kannada* · *appa vs tande* · *how do
you say I miss you in Kannada* · *does Kannada have a word for missing someone* ·
*Kannada word for death separation* · *quotes for father's death anniversary in
Kannada* · *is appa Tamil or Kannada*.

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that separates the bereaved reader from the
long-distance reader for a query Kannada itself does not separate — Kittel files
ಅಗಲಿಕೆ under one headword meaning both "to go to a distance" and "to leave" the body —
printing only lines checked against a named dictionary entry or an attested corpus
sentence, and saying plainly that the `/missyou-gf` template whose 214 pages supply
its first-party numbers is built for a partner and misfits a memorial.

## Phase 3 — Language verification (the method, and its cuts)

Every fetch below was made this session. Nothing here is from memory.

### Verified and printed

| Item | Source fetched | What it actually says |
|---|---|---|
| ಅಪ್ಪ | Kittel p. 78, entry 2 | "father", with Tamil and Malayalam cognates. **Entry 1 under the same spelling is a relative present participle of ಆಗು, "becoming, being."** |
| ತಂದೆ (as ತನ್ದೆ) | Kittel p. 729 | "father", glossed through Sanskrit ಜನಕ / ಪಿತೃವಪ್ಪ / ತಾತ; "occasionally it is an endearing appellation (like ಅಪ್ಪ)" |
| ನೆನಪು | Kittel p. 954 | "thought, reflection; recollection, remembrance". Every example is dative-experiencer: ನಿಮಗೇನಾದರೂ ನೆನಪುಂಟೋ, ಅದು ನೆನಪಾಯಿತು, imperative ನೆನಪಿಡಿರಿ, ಮಱೆಯಬೇಡಿರಿ |
| ಅಗಲು / ಅಗಲುವಿಕೆ | Kittel p. 11 | "to go to a distance; to go away", sense 3 "to leave" (ಜೀವಂ ತನುವನ್ ಅಗಲಿದ — the life departed the body). Noun glossed ಪ್ರೀತಿಯುಳ್ಳವರ ಅಗಲುವಿಕೆ, Skt ವಿಪ್ರಯೋಗ / ವಿಯೋಗ |
| ನಿಮಗೆ ನೆನಪು ಇದ್ಯಾ? | Tatoeba sentence 4784802 | attested Kannada, no English translation linked |
| ಅಪ್ಪ etymology | en.wiktionary.org | Proto-Dravidian \*appa; Malayalam അപ്പൻ, Telugu అప్ప, Tamil அப்பா |
| codepoints | Unicode UCD 18.0.0 | Kannada block U+0C80..U+0CFF; U+0CCD VIRAMA, U+0C82 ANUSVARA |

**Register answer, as asked.** ಅಪ್ಪ is the intimate/colloquial term, ತಂದೆ the formal
one — and the decisive evidence is in the *ತಂದೆ* entry, not the ಅಪ್ಪ one. Kittel
p. 729 lists ತಂದೆ's Sanskrit-register synonyms and then says it is only "occasionally
… an endearing appellation (like ಅಪ್ಪ)", i.e. the dictionary treats ಅಪ್ಪ as the
endearment baseline that ತಂದೆ sometimes borrows. The ಅಪ್ಪ entry has no Sanskrit
synonym list at all — only kinship compounds and household proverbs.

**Corpus finding.** Tatoeba's entire Kannada corpus held **326 sentences** on
2026-09-25, against 2,045,262 for English. Two contain ತಂದೆ, one contains ನೆನಪು, and
**none contains ಅಪ್ಪ**. Anything a translation tool emits for "miss you appa" in
Kannada therefore has no attested sentence behind it.

### Cut, and why — 7 items

1. **ಹಂಬಲಿಸು** *hambalisu*, "to long for" — the obvious verb an English-first writer
   reaches for. **Cut.** Kittel p. 1707 attests it glossing a cry of longing for
   sexual intercourse (ಮೈಥುನವನ್ನು ಕುಱಿತು ಹಮ್ಬಲಿಸಿ ಕೂಗಿ ಮೊಱೆಯಿಡುವದು, ಆಕ್ಷಾರಣೆ Si. 57).
   Exactly the ಸ್ನೇಹ / ରହ failure mode the BRIEF warns about.
2. **ಹಂಬಲ** the noun — cut with it, same entry family.
3. **ತಂದೆ as the headline word of a printed line** — cut, demoted to a register
   contrast. A "miss you appa" searcher wants the endearment.
4. **Kittel's first sense of ಅಪ್ಪ** — cut from any line. It is a verb form, not "father".
5. **ಕಣ್ಣಿಗೆ ಕಾಣುವ ದೇವರು ಅಪ್ಪ** (from the #3 YouTube title) — cut. Competitor-origin,
   and no dictionary or corpus attests the phrase as a unit.
6. **Every line reachable through the two MyMemory SERP results** — cut unread.
   Unreviewed translation memory is not attested Kannada.
7. **Every multi-clause image-card "quote" on Instagram and Pinterest** — cut. No
   author, no checkable source.

**Orthography trap worth recording.** Kittel returns **zero** results for the modern
spelling ತಂದೆ, and it would have been easy and wrong to report "not in Kittel". The
dictionary predates anusvara normalisation and files the word as ತನ್ದೆ. Same for
ಹಂಬಲ → ಹಮ್ಬಲ.

### The source I wanted and could not use

Baruch, Ohayon & Yehene, *"Beyond 'normative' loss: social context, loss integration,
and adaptation to parental bereavement in midlife"*, **BMC Psychology**, 2026-06-03.
n = 423 adults aged 35–65 (dual-parental loss 122, single 122, non-bereaved 179);

JavaScript shell or HTTP 403 to an automated fetch, so it could not be honestly
recorded as verified. If a slot frees on that domain, this belongs in the objection

**1,768 words** excluding FAQs. **11 FAQs**, in `article.faqs` and the renderer-built
FAQPage only.

- `categorySlug`: **family-and-continuity**, not `miss-you-across-miles`. "Across
  miles" misdescribes half the readership — a bereaved reader is not separated by
  distance.
- `templateUrls`: `/missyou-gf` (mandatory), `/dedication`, `/catch`. Three internal
  links, all from `TEMPLATE_LINKS`, all placed after the section that earns them.
- **`/missyou-gf` is linked honestly and argued against.** It is a girlfriend/partner
  template whose centrepiece is a reunion countdown; 28.0% of its 214 senders set a
  date, and a bereaved reader has none. `/dedication` — a radio show with your own
  recorded voice introducing a song — is named as the more honest memorial
  alternative, with the reason: only 13.6% of miss-you senders record a voice note,
  the least-used feature on the romantic template and the one a memorial actually
  wants.
- No price or "free" language anywhere. No surprise framing, no urgency, no
  engagement bait.

**The three mandatory first-party caveats appear in body prose**, not only in the
audit: pickers-with-defaults (city, background music, "together since" excluded and
the sender-chosen fields named), `viewCount` as page views not unique visitors
(2,417 across the segment), and n = 214 over two months since 2026-07-28 with no
seasonal claim. The body also states, before the table and again in the objection
section, that these numbers describe **romantic-template senders and not bereaved
senders**.
