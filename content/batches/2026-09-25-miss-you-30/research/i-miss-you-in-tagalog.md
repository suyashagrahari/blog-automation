## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** — the phrases themselves (*Miss kita*, *Miss na kita*,
*Namimiss kita*), the fact that *kita* fuses I and you, a note that Filipinos
mix English in.

**The gap**, and it is unusually wide:

1. Nobody explains *how* Tagalog conjugates a borrowed English verb. The `ma-` /
   `na-` paradigm behind `na-miss` → `namimiss` → `mamimiss` appears on no page.
2. Nobody checks the national dictionary. It has the decisive entry.
3. Everybody repeats "Tagalog has no word for miss" and nobody tests it.
4. Nobody separates *aspect* from *politeness* — the five forms are presented as
   synonyms of varying romance rather than as different tenses.
5. Nobody answers "how long should the message be".

**Stale / unsupported data** — none of the pages cites data at all, so there is
nothing to supersede; the stale thing is the folk claim in point 3.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s**
- what does "miss kita" literally mean → *The Tagalog ways to say it*
- miss na kita vs namimiss kita → same section + the comparison table
- how do you conjugate "miss" in Tagalog → *How Tagalog conjugates a borrowed English verb*
- is there a Tagalog word for "miss" → *Does Tagalog have a native word for missing someone?*
- what does the Filipino dictionary say → *What the national dictionary does not record*
- how long should an "I miss you" message be → *What 214 miss-you pages show*
- is Tagalog the same as Bisaya → *Tagalog, Bisaya, or neither*

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that shows how Tagalog conjugates the
borrowed English verb *miss* — and that the KWF national dictionary lists *miss*
in six senses, none of them longing, while the native *pangungulila* and *sabik*
name the feeling but take no object — set against 214 first-party miss-you pages.

## Phase 3 — Language verification (fetched 2026-09-25)

Every line printed in the post is glossed from one of these. Nothing from memory.

### Confirmed and printed

| Item | Source | What it actually says |
|---|---|---|
| `kita` (pronoun) | en.wiktionary.org, Tagalog section | "Replaces **ko ka** or **ko ikaw** (I or my and you)" — derived terms include *mahal kita* |
| `kita` (other senses) | same entry, etymologies 2–3 | *kità* = visibility / seen / obvious; *kità* = earnings, income, profit |
| `na` (particle) | en.wiktionary.org, Tagalog, ety. 2 | "already; now; anymore … expresses the event when following a verb" — *Tapos na* |
| `ma-` (prefix) | en.wiktionary.org, Tagalog | trigger prefix; usage note: "*ma-* implies that the action performed is unintentional", vs *-in* for deliberate — *Nabasag ko ang salamin* "(unintentionally)" |
| `na-` (prefix) | en.wiktionary.org, Tagalog | "used to form complete aspects of verbs prefixed with *ma-*" |
| `din` / `rin` | en.wiktionary.org, Tagalog | *din* = "too; also"; becomes *rin* after a vowel, ⟨w⟩ or ⟨y⟩ |
| `Namimiss ko na siya.` | tatoeba.org, tgl→eng, #743922 | "I miss her so much." |
| `Namimiss ko na ang pamilya at bayan ko.` | tatoeba.org #3160596 | "I miss my family and my country." |
| `Mamimiss din kita.` | tatoeba.org, tgl | "I will also miss you." — the only attested sentence carrying *kita* with the loan verb |
| `pangungulila` | diksiyonaryo.ph (KWF) | png, [pang+u+ulilà]: "1: matindi at matagal bago mawalang lumbay 2: lumbay bunga ng pithayang umuwi dahil sa matagal na pagkawalay sa bayan, pamilya, at minamahal" |
| `sabik` | diksiyonaryo.ph (KWF) | pnr: "2: matinding paghahangad na makíta o makaulayaw ang isang matagal nang hindi nakakasáma" |
| `sabik` derived terms | en.wiktionary.org | *panabikan*, *pananabik*, *manabik*, *sumabik*, *kasabikan* … |
| `ulila` | en.wiktionary.org + diksiyonaryo.ph | noun "orphan; orphanhood; (fig.) state of being alone" — synonym of sense 3 is *pangungulila*; adj. "(fig.) alone; lonely" |
| **`miss`** | diksiyonaryo.ph (KWF) | **pnd** (verb), tagged **Ing**: six senses, all "mabigong…" — fail to hit/reach, fail to catch a vehicle, fail to experience/see/attend an event, fail to meet a person, fail to seize an opportunity, fail to hear or understand. **None is "to long for someone absent."** |

### Cut, with the reason (7)

1. **`namimiss` / `namiss` / `nami-miss`** — en.wiktionary.org returned **HTTP 404**
   for all three. No dictionary adjudicates the hyphen, so the post says exactly
   that rather than picking one.
2. **`nangungulila` / `mangulila`** — Wiktionary 404; diksiyonaryo.ph returned an
   empty headword. `mangulila` is listed inside Wiktionary's derived-terms box for
   `ulila`, which is not an entry. Neither inflected form is printed.
3. **`nasasabik` / `nananabik`** — Wiktionary 404, diksiyonaryo.ph empty. Cut.
4. **`hanap-hanap`** — not a headword. diksiyonaryo.ph returned the root `hanap`,
   whose first listed sense is a Cebuano adjective *malabò*, "blurry". Dropped
   entirely rather than glossed from a near-match.
5. **`pinananabikan`** — printed as a form but deliberately **not** glossed as a
   headword: neither Wiktionary nor KWF lemmatises it. Only the root and two
   derived terms are attested.
6. **`Miss na miss kita`** — circulates everywhere, attested nowhere fetched. Absent.
7. **`Miss din kita`** — appears in one FAQ with its attestation stated honestly:
   Tatoeba carries *Mamimiss din kita*, not *Miss din kita*, and the *din*/*rin*
   rule comes from Wiktionary.

## Cannibalisation

The hub was fetched on 2026-09-25 (5,549 words). Its **only** Philippine content is
`Tagalog (Filipino): Miss na kita / Nami-miss kita (miss na kee-tah)` inside a 50+
language list, plus a one-line FAQ. No grammar, no aspect, no affix, no citation.
This post links up and restates none of it.

The sibling `miss-kita-in-bisaya` is **Cebuano**: *hidlaw*, *mingaw*, *gimingaw*,
the Tatoeba sentence *Samot pa kong gimingaw nimo ron*, and the Cebuano senses of
*kita*. This post is **Tagalog**: the *kita* portmanteau, the *na* particle, the
`ma-`/`na-` loan-verb paradigm, *pangungulila*, *sabik*, the KWF `miss` entry.

**The division line:** the sibling establishes that "miss kita" is *not* Bisaya.
This post establishes what it *is*, and how the borrowed verb inflects. Not one
Cebuano finding is retold here as this post's own — *Gimingaw ko nimo* appears once,
in the downside section, as a pointer that the question is covered elsewhere. The
sibling is named but not linked because it is not yet live. Language is one of the
three non-duplicate axes the BRIEF names, so this is a spoke, not a merge candidate.
