### The lexical finding (Platts, full-text search, 2026-09-25)

- Full-text search for **`miss`** → **68 results.** Only **three** gloss missing a
  *person*, and all three are Sanskrit-derived nominals, not Urdu verbs:
  `utkaṇṭha` p.17 ("eagerly longing for; regretting, missing"), `utkaṇṭhita` p.17
  ("one who longs after an absent love"), `lālasā` p.947 ("eager longing for an
  absent person or object"). Every other hit is *miss a target* or *be mislaid*:
  `ūknā` p.106 "to miss, slip, blunder", `burd` p.146 "to lose, miss, forfeit",
  `bāl-bāṅdhī kauṛī mārnā` p.124 "to shoot at without missing", `gum` p.914,
  `mafqūd` p.1053, `harānā` p.1224.
- Full-text search for **`to miss`** → **42 results**, same pattern.
- Headword entries used: `yād` p.1247 — "Remembrance, recollection; memory", with
  `yād ānā` "to come to recollection or mind; to recur to memory"; `hajr, hijr`
  p.1221 — "Separation; disjunction; desertion (of country or friends); absence";
  `firāq` p.777 — "Separation… anxiety, grief, regret (on account of absence)";
  `tanhāʼī` p.340 — "Loneliness, solitude".

The consequence, which is the post's second differentiator: English "I miss you"
puts the subject on the person doing the missing. The Urdu construction `yād ānā`
puts it on the person remembered — *you come to my memory*. That is a change of
grammatical subject, not a synonym, and it is why the phrase does not round-trip.

## Structural limitations

1. **First-party differentiation is thin, and no amount of writing fixes it.**
   `facts-snapshot.md` carries 12 miss-you lines for 30 posts. Measured across the
   35 sibling files: the two least-reused lines are already in 7 posts each, and
   the most-reused are in 35 of 35. I open on the two least-reused (88.8% published
   and shared; 2.6-hour median edit gap) as the `for wife` row did, but the facts
   themselves are not differentiating within this batch. This closes with a new
   database probe — specifically a probe of whether miss-you letters that quote
   someone behave differently from letters that do not — not with more prose.
2. **No first-party data on quoting at all.** The database has no field recording
   whether a letter contains a borrowed line, so every claim in this post about
   *quoting* behaviour is reasoning, not measurement, and is written as such.
3. **Only one scholarly source was both on-subject and reachable, and only as an
   abstract.** The UC Press Luminos chapter on translating a Ghalib ghazal is
   confirmed gold OA by Unpaywall and still 403s to a fetch, on both the chapter and
   the book landing page. PDFs do not parse in this environment, which removed the
   two DOAJ hits as well.
4. **Transliteration is a convention, not a standard.** There is no single
   romanisation for Urdu in popular use; the post says so rather than implying its
   spellings are canonical.
5. **The brief's framing that `rekhta.org` is cap-exempt is wrong.** The prompt
   lists rekhta.org among "instruments (cap-exempt)". It is **not** in
   `verify.config.json → capExemptDomains`. It happens to be at 0 posts so nothing
   breaks, but the claim as written is false and a later row relying on it could
   breach the cap without noticing.
6. **Three couplets, not four.** A fourth verified Ghalib couplet
   (*shab-hā-e-hijr ko bhī rakhūñ gar hisāb meñ*, from the same ghazal as couplet 2)
   was drafted, verified and then cut to bring the body under the hard 1,800-word
   ceiling. It is the most redundant of the four — same source page as couplet 2 —
   but the cut is a word-budget decision, not an editorial one.
