## Phase 2 — gap
- Table stakes: short lines for kids, teacher→student, kid→teacher/grandparents, Bhai Dooj.
- Gap: nobody grades by age with a reason, nobody separates greetings TO children from greetings BY children with different length rules, nobody ties length to what Indian children can read, nobody prints Diwali 2027, and the "safe Diwali" lines elsewhere carry no evidence.
- Fan-out: simple greeting for a toddler; message length by age; teacher to students; child's card to grandparents; mixed-faith class; crackers in kids' wishes; can a child use WhatsApp; Diwali/Bhai Dooj 2027 dates; opening a page on a tablet.
- Angle: wins by being the only post that grades greetings both ways by age against ASER 2024 reading results and child emotion-vocabulary research, with an evidence-based cracker-safety section.

## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched)
1. Grosse et al. 2021, Affective Science, CC BY — europepmc PMC9382957. n = 123 children aged 4–11; broad emotion words (happy, sad) come first; 4–5-year-olds lump positive emotions. German-speaking sample (limitation). **Peer-reviewed, subject test passed.**
2. ASER 2024 National findings PDF (asercentre.org) — Std III govt 23.4% read Std II text; Std V govt 44.8%, private 59.3%; Std VIII govt 67.5%; 649,491 children; reading tested in own language. **Subject test passed** (children's reading). No printed date; PDF metadata 13 Feb 2025.
3. Bhasin, Shah et al. 2026, Indian J Ophthalmol (CC BY-NC-ND; cite and link only) — europepmc PMC13674918. 67 patients, Diwali 2023–24; 62.7% children; median age 14; 34.3% bystanders. **Peer-reviewed.** Journal now used by 2 posts (choti-diwali cites PMC2854452).
