# Research brief: diwali-card (row 14)

Keyword: **diwali card**. Secondaries: digital diwali card, diwali greeting card, diwali card for family.
Phenomenon (PLAN): digital vs paper greeting cards; history of printed festival cards in India.
Planned angle: card TYPES (paper, handmade, digital, interactive) and when each fits. Not what to write; that is the old post `what-to-write-in-a-diwali-card`.

**Head-term flag.** "diwali card" is a 2-word head term with commercial and DIY intent. A low-authority domain is unlikely to win it. Long-tail alternatives: "which diwali card to send to parents abroad", "digital diwali card vs paper card", "when to post a diwali card for 2027". Proceeded per PLAN; the post targets the type-choice intent.

- **Gap:** no page helps someone choose between the types. Nobody gives postage cost, a posting deadline for the festival, or a keepability comparison, and nobody mentions a paper card that carries a link.
- **Stale data:** none to supersede. The SERP cites no data.
- **Fan-out sub-queries:** what counts as a Diwali card now; types of Diwali card; cost to post a card in India; when to post for Diwali 2027; digital vs paper for family; can grandparents open a digital card; what size a card is; whether recipients mind digital.

## Phase 3: sources (all fetched 2026-10-22)
1. V&A E.840-2002, https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O85161/. A 1998 Diwali card by Kiran Ahmad for Setu Ltd, England: gold embossing, 16.3 × 11.7 cm, Lakshmi with coins, which the catalogue links to Gujarati practice. Read via api.vam.ac.uk. The record has no publication date. Passes the subject test.
2. V&A E.281-2009, https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1172046/. A 2000 card from The Publishing House. The catalogue says it shows the British card industry widening its festival range. Passes the subject test.
3. PIB / Department of Posts, 26 Sep 2025, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2171937. Speed Post documents up to 50 g cost ₹19 local and ₹47 for every other distance band, GST extra, from 1 Oct 2025. This is the post's one context source, and it fails the swap test (recorded in the audit).
4. Coleman et al., BMJ, 14 Dec 2021, CC BY, https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8669570. A randomised trial of Christmas card vs no card, 1,469 analysed: 85.3% vs 85.4% retention, £0.76 per participant, about 140 g CO2e per card. Peer-reviewed and open access. Passes the subject test (posted greeting cards).
5. Reshadi, Givi & Das, Psychology & Marketing, 7 Jan 2023, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mar.21790. Givers are less likely to choose digital gift cards than recipients are to prefer them, because givers overestimate the norm violation. **Abstract only**, read via the Crossref record. The Wiley page returns 403.
