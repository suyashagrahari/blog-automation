## Phase 2 — gap
- Table stakes: Rama's return; Lakshmi; Narakasura; Jain and Sikh stories; a kid version.
- Gap: no top-5 page names a text, book, canto or translation, and none says where the popular telling departs from the text (lamp rows absent from Griffith's Canto CXXIX; Lakshmi before Dhanwantari in Ganguli; no count of kings and no Diwali date in Macauliffe's release chapter; Digambara version without illumination).
- Stale data: peekaabookids prints 2025 dates. Post uses 2027 dates verified at drikpanchang (date reference only).
- Fan-out: what is the Diwali story; which story is which day; Ramayana and lamps; 52 kings; kid version; Hindi version; Govardhan.
- Angle: wins by being the only post that retells all five stories from the named public-domain texts with chapter/section and states what each text leaves out.
- Sibling overlap: choti-diwali-2027 retells Narakasura from Vishnu Purana (Gutenberg #66208); govardhan-puja-2027 retells Govardhan from the same; lakshmi-puja-vidhi uses Vishnu Purana Bk I ch. 9 (archive.org india.history.resource.77336); diwali-essay cites Jacobi's Kalpa Sutra (archive.org sbe22) and BBC for Bandi Chhor. This post uses none of those texts or URLs. Govardhan is a one-line pointer only.
