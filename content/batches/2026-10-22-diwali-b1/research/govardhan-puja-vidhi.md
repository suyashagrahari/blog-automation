## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched 2026-10-22)

1. **Crooke, The Popular Religion and Folk-Lore of Northern India, Vol. 2 (1896, 2nd ed.)** — Project Gutenberg #43682 (cap-exempt host), full text read. Section "The Govardhan": NWP women build a mud hut with Gauri and Ganesa images on a platform outside, place the parched grain offered on Diwali night, thorny grass, wave a rice pounder; Panjab quotation: Govardhan of cowdung = Krishna lying on his back, cottage-loaf mounds as mountains, grass stems with cotton/rag tufts as trees, dung balls as cattle; churn-staff, five white sugar-canes, parched rice and a lamp in the middle; cowherds salute and are fed rice and sweets; nobody eats, cuts or presses cane till the Brahman tastes; bullocks' horns dyed; Cawnpur "Dang" Diwali: cowherds worship a heap of cowdung decorated with cotton, dance to two sticks and a drum, collect grain, cloth or money. Public-domain primary ethnography.
2. **Growse, Mathurá: A District Memoir (1883)** — archive.org mathurdistrict00growrich, OCR text read. pp. 300–301: the hill "in earlier literature is more frequently designated the Anna-kut"; Mathura festival calendar: "Kartik Sudi 1. — Anna-kut. The same observances as at Gobardhan, but on a smaller scale." Public-domain primary.
3. **Telangana Fire, Disaster Response, Emergency and Civil Defence Department, Public Safety Advisory for Diwali 2025 (press release, 18 Oct 2025)** — fire.telangana.gov.in PDF, full text read: "Place diyas and candles on stable surfaces, away from curtains or decorations"; "Don't leave candles or diyas unattended."
