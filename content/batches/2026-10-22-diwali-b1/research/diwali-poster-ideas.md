## Phase 2 — gap
- Table stakes: poster ideas/themes, colours, slogans/wording, school use.
- Gap: legibility at distance (no page gives sizes), measured contrast of festive colour pairs, a judging rubric, original slogans with a measured fact line, materials with cost.
- Stale data: bluestone and template.net are dated to Diwali 2026; our dates use 2027.
- Fan-out: what makes a poster work → layouts → letter size → colours → slogans → materials/judging → WhatsApp version → when not to use a poster.
- Angle: wins by being the only post that gives letter heights by viewing distance and WCAG contrast ratios for Diwali colour pairs, with a 20-mark rubric, using SubhSandesh's 48.3% phone-open share for the WhatsApp version.

## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched; capcheck run)
1. Grabowska-Chenczke et al., Behavioral Sciences 2026 (PMC13023464) — eye-tracking, N = 96: pictures caught attention 3–7× faster than logos, 2–7× more fixations. Full text read. CC BY.
2. Legge & Bigelow, Journal of Vision 2011 (PMC3428264) — fluent x-height 0.2°–2°; 1.4–14 mm at 40 cm. ABSTRACT ONLY (PMC page and Europe PMC full text unavailable).
3. de Jong, Eye 2022 (PMC10764321) — 5 arcmin visual angle accepted as the acuity unit (Snellen/Landolt). Full text read. CC BY 4.0.
4. W3C WCAG 2.2 Understanding 1.4.3 — 4.5:1 / 3:1; 18 pt or 14 pt bold = large. Cap-exempt.
5. Shah et al., Lung India 2019 (PMC6625239) — peak PM2.5 by firecracker type (sparklers 10,390; snake tablets 64,500 µg/m³). Full text read. CC BY-NC-SA: cited, not reproduced.
6. Parija et al., Indian J Ophthalmol 2021 (PMC8837315) — 65 cases; 60% bystanders; 95.4% no eye protection. Full text read. CC BY-NC-SA: cited, not reproduced.
