## Phase 2 — gap analysis
- Table stakes: a list of kid-safe Halloween puns grouped somehow; ghosts/witches/vampires/mummies/bats/pumpkins.
- Gap: no ranking page (a) filters strictly for kids (Romper mixes 21+ drink puns), (b) says whether a pun works heard or read, (c) gives an age per pun type, (d) addresses children learning English (large share of IN/global audience), (e) cites anything.
- Stale data: Romper updated 2022; no stats cited anywhere.
- Fan-out: what is a pun for kids; which puns for which age; spoken vs written; why kids miss puns / EAL; classic kid puns; can I send one to a child; when to skip.
- Sibling separation: b1 halloween-puns (sorted by monster + mechanic labels) → this post sorts by *delivery channel* (ear/eye/drawing) using Lems's four categories and adds texting puns + draw-the-pun, none of which siblings use. b1 halloween-jokes-for-kids (age bands, knock-knocks, riddles, lunchbox notes, make-your-own template) → no knock-knocks, riddles, lunchbox or template here. b3 toddlers, food puns, b2 love puns, b1 work puns, b4 teens: no overlapping lines (5-gram check against all 250 Halloween b1–b4 blog bodies + FAQs: zero pun-line hits; prose collisions on stock template phrasing were reworded). Draw prompts avoid "hat trick", "paws", "sheet music" (used by siblings); close-sounding avoid "witch-ful", "hocus focus", "tomb-orrow" (used by siblings).

**Angle:** Wins by being the only post that sorts original, gore-free Halloween puns for kids by how a child catches them (heard, read, or drawn) using Lems's four pun categories plus draw-the-pun prompts, with a starting age per type, open-access research on why children and English learners miss double meanings, and SubhSandesh's 48.3% phone-open share and 11.1 views per page.
