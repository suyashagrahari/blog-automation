## Phase 3 — Sources (all fetched via Europe PMC REST full text, 13 Oct 2026)

Aron et al. 1997 (PSPB) is SAGE — banned in this batch — so it is named as background only and never linked; its procedure is described from the two open-access papers that report it.

1. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13647488 — Fornari et al., *Psychophysiology* 63, 1 Oct 2026 (Wiley, CC BY-NC-ND; cited, not reused). 123 stranger dyads (N=246) randomised to no interaction / small talk / Fast Friends; closeness rose across the three; induced closeness did not affect collaborative performance or inter-brain synchrony. Methods: Aron's set is 36 questions in three 12-item lists; original 45 min; they used Sprecher's 24-min version of three 8-min blocks. Full text read.
2. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7901865 — Sprecher, *Current Psychology* 42:1044–1057, online 23 Feb 2021. 71 dyads, structured self-disclosure task over Skype in three 5-min sets increasing in intimacy, turn-taking on who asks first; participants underestimated how much they were liked. Full text read. (Springer.)
3. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12638239 — West, Huston, Chandler, Zhou & Fredrickson, *Communications Psychology* 3:165, 21 Nov 2025 (CC BY). Total N=646 across two studies; verbal validation and follow-up questions predicted social connection (observer, self and partner reports); 10-min "deep talk" in Study 1. Full text read.
4. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12876889 — Kleinert et al., *Communications Psychology* 4:23, 14 Jan 2026 (CC BY). 492 participants, text-based modified Fast Friends; AI labelled as human outperformed humans in closeness during deep talk, an effect that "appears to stem from" the AI's higher self-disclosure. Full text read.
