### Was the paper convention for year one sourceable?

**Yes, and better than expected.** John H. Young, *Our Deportment; or, The Manners,
Conduct and Dress of the Most Refined Society* (1881), Chapter XXIV "Anniversary
Weddings", pp. 285–290, states verbatim: *"The first anniversay of the wedding-day is
called the Paper Wedding"* (the OCR carries Young's or the scanner's misspelling of
"anniversary"), that invitations *"should be issued on a grey paper"*, and that
presents *"if given should be solely articles made of paper."* The same chapter also
undercuts itself usefully: the earliest anniversaries are *"almost too trivial
occasions"* on which to repeat the marriage ceremony.

Route: the Internet Archive advanced-search JSON endpoint
(`archive.org/advancedsearch.php?...&output=json`) to find nineteenth-century American
etiquette manuals, then the plain-text derivative
(`archive.org/download/<id>/<id>_djvu.txt`) to grep the chapter. Sherwood's *Manners
and Social Usages* (1884) was checked first and has **no** anniversary-material list —
a dead end worth recording. `mdpi.com`, `academic.oup.com` and `journals.sagepub.com`
all returned 403 to plain fetch; the PMC mirror and a Dutch institutional repository
supplied the same articles.

**One claim deliberately not made:** that paper appears at year one in *every* version
of the list. One 1881 manual cannot support "every", and Emily Post 1922 is off-limits
for this post, so the body asserts only what Young says plus the honest caveat that no
edition of the list covers a dating anniversary.

   first anniversary "is the one year that genuinely does have a surviving convention —
   paper — present in every version of the list including Post 1922." The first half is
   sourceable and now sourced. The second half — **"every version of the list"** — is

   off-limits to this post, so the claim could not be carried into the body at the
   strength the brief states it. The body asserts only the 1881 manual. If the "every
   version" framing is load-bearing for the cluster, it needs its own source.

   `journals.sagepub.com` all return 403 to a plain `fetch`/`curl` even with a browser
   UA and referer, while the PMC mirror and Dutch/US institutional repositories serve
