## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes (all nine touch, so the post has to cover them):** a list of surprise
ideas; something for long distance; something last-minute; the fact that a personal
gesture beats a generic one.

**The gap nobody fills.** Every page answers *what to buy*. None of them notices that
her birthday is **a date she has known for a year** and **a day already congested with
other people's wishes**. Nothing in the SERP discusses:

- Which *hour* of the day is actually available to you.
- That surprise cannot come from the date, only from the information.
- That the giver wants the reveal more than the recipient does.
- What is still doing work on the 2nd.
- Any measurement of what people report wanting on their own birthday.

**Stale / absent data.** Not one of the nine cites a study, a survey or a dated
figure of any kind. There is no stale number to supersede; there is no number at all.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s.** Each H2 below answers one sub-query standalone:

| Sub-query | H2 |
|---|---|
| Can a birthday even be a surprise if she knows the date? | What "surprise" can still mean on a date she already knows |

**Angle (recorded as `batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only post that treats her

| Source | Publisher | Why it is here | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|
| [A survey of some aspects of birthday celebration](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6130922/) (2018) | *Acta Medica Lituanica* / Vilnius University | The only measured data found anywhere on what people want on **their own** birthday | Full text read via PMC; CC BY |
| [Asymmetries in Gift-Exchange: The Role of Surprise](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/16/9/1522) (29 Aug 2026) | *Behavioral Sciences*, MDPI | Givers value surprise more than recipients — the narrow, instance-specific version of the claim the brief bans in its general form | Full text read in a Chromium session (plain fetch 403s); CC BY |
| [A Little Good Goes an Unexpectedly Long Way](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/3yast/) (15 Nov 2021) | PsyArXiv, accepted manuscript for *J. Exp. Psychol.: General* | The counterweight: givers underrate a small gesture's impact | Full PDF read; Exp 2a figures checked line by line |
| [Understanding the processes that regulate positive emotional experience](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/18) (30 Jan 2011) | *International Journal of Wellbeing* | Savouring's three temporal windows, and the reminiscence > moment > anticipation ordering | Full PDF read; CC BY-NC-ND, so cited and linked only |

**Scope statements written into the body, not hidden here.** The Vilnius sample is 309
Lithuanian medical students (70.2% women, mean age 22.9), not Indian couples. The MDPI
experiments are young consumers in México answering in assigned roles. Kumar & Epley's
recipients were strangers, not partners. The savouring sex difference is
self-*perceived* capacity, replicated in the US, Canada, Australia and Japan — India is
not among the listed countries. Each of those caveats is stated in the paragraph that
uses the finding.

**Deliberately not asserted.** That givers systematically mispredict what recipients
*want*. The canonical papers are closed-access and the brief is right about it; what is
used instead is the specific, open, replicated finding that givers weight *surprise*
more heavily than recipients do.

**Sourcing failures worth recording.**
- `ee.openlibhums.org` (Ethnologia Europaea, *Bonds, Bottles, Blat and Banquets:
  Birthdays and Networks in Russia*, CC BY per Crossref) is behind an Anubis
  JS challenge, and its DOI actually resolves to `berghahnjournals.com`, which serves a
  human-verification wall. Abandoned rather than cited unread.
- Foster & Keane's surprise-as-explanatory-difficulty paper is OA only at
  `researchrepository.ucd.ie`, which serves a CAPTCHA.
- Reis, Lemay & Finkel's felt-understanding review is listed as publisher-OA but Wiley
  returns 403 to fetch and `ERR_ABORTED` in Chromium; the Utrecht DSpace mirror is
  JS-only.
- `journals.sagepub.com`, `sciencedirect.com` and `pure.buas.nl` PDFs all 403.
- Semantic Scholar's API rate-limited (HTTP 429).
