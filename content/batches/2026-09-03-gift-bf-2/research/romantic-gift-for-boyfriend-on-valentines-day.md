1. **Mäkelä, Bettany & Stevens (2018)** — "Crunch my heart! It falls for you", chapter in *Gifts, Romance, and Consumer Culture* (Routledge), accepted peer-reviewed version on the University of Bath research portal. Multi-sited netnography across English, French and Finnish sources. Verified quotation: chocolate "coded as a feminine food … and men encouraged to buy women the gift of chocolate as an expression of love". Verified count: 38 French forum threads analysed, eighteen carrying an index of indulgence, self-pleasure or seduction. **Subject test: pass** (romantic gift-giving and its gendering). **Load-bearing for the angle.**
2. **Lai & Huang (1 Feb 2013)**, *HortTechnology* 23(1) 28–37 — peer-reviewed, open access. 366 valid questionnaires. Verified: flowers ranked third in Taiwan behind apparel and chocolate as a Valentine's romantic purchase; consumers more satisfied with their relationship were **less** likely to buy their partner fresh flowers. **Subject test: pass.** Reached via a Chromium session — plain `fetch` to `journals.ashs.org` returns a 1.4 KB JS shell for both the article and the PDF endpoint, and a cookie-jar retry did not help. Recording this because it is exactly the failure mode correction 4 in the brief warns about.
3. **Chan & Mogilner (29 Dec 2016)**, *Journal of Consumer Research*, author-hosted full PDF at UCLA Anderson — peer-reviewed, openly readable. Four studies. Verified: experiential gifts strengthen relationships more than material gifts "regardless of whether the gift giver and recipient consume the gift together"; study 2's identical mug, inscribed "my coffee time" vs "my coffee mug", produced 7.47 vs 6.92 reported relationship strengthening, t(107)=1.99, p=.05, with no difference in liking. **Subject test: pass.** Note: the study's monetary parameters were deliberately left out of the post, per the no-price rule.
4. **Zajonc (2023)**, "Valentine's Day and the Eventisation of Slovakia's Festive Culture", *Slovenský národopis* 71(4) 403–425 — peer-reviewed, Creative Commons open access, Slovak Academy of Sciences. Verified quotation: Valentine's Day is "particularly a couples' feast day, the main practice of which is gifting". Verified figures from the representative 2018 survey it cites: 31% celebrate / 36% do not celebrate / 31% do not accept it as a holiday (2% did not know of it). **Subject test: pass.** Explicitly labelled as Slovakia in the body — no Indian inference drawn from it.
5. **West Virginia University (13 Feb 2023)** via phys.org, reporting Givi, Reshadi & Das in *Psychology and Marketing*. Verified: pilot study, 96 participants, **94.8%** said a physical gift card is more socially normative than a digital one; givers overestimate how much recipients see digital gift cards as violating gift-giving norms. **Subject test: pass.** This is the narrow, instance-specific version of the divergence claim; the post explicitly says it is "a narrow finding about gift cards, not a licence to claim givers always misjudge", per correction 1 in the brief.

## Things in the brief I found wrong or worth flagging

1. **A third possible brief error, offered as requested.** The brief's own framing of my row — "the SERP is `caratlane.com` and `chococraft.in`, i.e. jewellery and chocolate aimed at women" — is correct but understates the finding. Jewellery and chocolate are not merely *aimed at* women in general marketing; the peer-reviewed chapter I found shows the chocolate case is a documented **gender-coding of the gift category itself**, with men positioned as the buyers and women as the recipients. That makes the gap stronger than "wrong audience": the top-ranking pages are selling the giver's conventional role back to a searcher who is standing in the other one. Worth writing into the next brief as a sourced claim rather than an inference.
2. **A caution on the LEGAL-TO-CITE list.** Two of its entries point at the *same underlying research* as entries on the spent-50 list — `research.wur.nl/.../give-me-your-self-gifts-are-liked-more-when-they-match-the-givers` is spent, while `sfu.ca/fass/news/2017/02/study--giving-a-gift-that-reflects-you-rather-than-your-valentin/` is legal and appears to report a closely related finding. A URL-level cap does not catch that, and the same trap the brief flags for `doi.org` applies to press releases about spent papers. I avoided both.
3. **`journals.ashs.org` needs the browser note recording.** It returns HTTP 200 with a 1,444-byte JavaScript shell for the article, the PDF and the DOI redirect alike, and cookie-jar retries do not clear it. It is not dead; it needs Chromium. Adding it to correction 4's list of known-browser-only hosts alongside Wiley and SSRN would save the next writer the detour.
