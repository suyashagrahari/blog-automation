## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes (all or most of the five):** the occasion is emotionally loaded; rituals differ by
family; there is a gift exchange; north/south variation exists; the bride is meeting new customs.

**The gap — nothing on the SERP does any of this:**

1. **Whose house**, named as the actual question, with a number attached to co-residence.
2. **Any 2026 date.** Not one page dates the festival. Diwali 2026 = 6–10 November, Lakshmi Puja
   Sunday 8 November — and the Sunday changes travel and leave planning.
3. **The internal conflict in the calendar**: Padwa (9 Nov) is a husband–wife day; Bhai Dooj
   (10 Nov) pulls a new bride toward her brother. Two consecutive days, two families.
4. **The husband.** Five pages, five brides. No page tells a husband to do anything.
5. **The reverse default.** Thalai Deepavali sends the couple to the *bride's* home — #4 mentions
   it as colour, not as a fact that inverts the premise of the other four pages.
6. **Worked wording** for the four audiences (spouse, in-laws, own parents, the couple).
7. **Honest product limits** — no page says "there is no template for this".

**Stale data:** none of the five cites data at all, so there is nothing to supersede. The staleness
is in the framing: #1 (2024) still writes as though the bride's job is to impress an audience.

**Fan-out sub-queries → headings:**
- where do you spend your first Diwali after marriage → H2 "Whose house…"
- when is Diwali 2026 / how many days → H2 "What the first Diwali after marriage is called…"
- what is Thalai Deepavali / pehli Diwali → same H2 + FAQ
- what is Diwali Padwa / Bhai Dooj for a newly married woman → H2 "Padwa on 9 November…"
- what rituals will I be expected to do → H2 "Learning someone else's rituals…"
- what should the husband do → H2 "If you are the husband…"
- what gifts for in-laws on first Diwali → H2 "Gifts, seeru…" + table
- what to write / wishes → H2 "Four messages worth writing this week"
- when is a digital greeting the wrong call → H2 "When a shareable Diwali page is the wrong thing"

**Angle (recorded as `batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only post that treats "whose house"
as the real question — dating the five days of Diwali 2026, showing that Padwa (9 Nov) and Bhai
Dooj (10 Nov) pull a newly married couple toward two different families, and pricing the honest
product limit that SubhSandesh has no Diwali template among the 13 page types that produced
2,739 pages.

## Phase 3 — Sources (5). All fetched and verified; publisher URLs, no DOI redirects

Lane: marital transition / newlywed adjustment / in-law relationship quality. Other agents' lanes
(gift psychology, homesickness, honorifics, Maharashtra's Diwali days, expressive writing,
workplace behaviour, dormant ties, grief, B2B gratitude) avoided.

1. **gatesopenresearch.org** — Bietsch, LaNasa & Sonneveldt, "Women living with their
   mothers-in-law", *Gates Open Research* 5:170, 17 Nov 2021, CC-BY, open peer review (3 approved).
   Verified in full text: 250 DHS surveys across 75 countries; co-residence ranges from 1%
   (Rwanda) to 49% (Tajikistan); **India is among nine countries above 30%**, and among six where
   the share has *statistically increased* over time. Peer-reviewed, open, subject test ✓.
2. **research-information.bris.ac.uk** — Charsley, "Unhappy husbands: masculinity and migration in
   transnational Pakistani marriages", *Journal of the Royal Anthropological Institute* 11(1),
   2005 (author accepted manuscript, full text read, not paywalled). Verified: brides are
   "instructed from a young age" on the adjustments of moving into the husband's household, while
   uxorilocal husbands (*ghar damad* / *ghar jamai*) are usually unprepared and stigmatised; and
   under patri-virilocal residence a bride's relationships with her husband's relatives are "of
   fundamental importance to the quality of her married life".
3. **scielo.br** — Sonawat, "Understanding families in India: a reflection of societal changes",
   *Psicologia: Teoria e Pesquisa* 17(2), 2001, open access. Verified: "The mother-in-law occupies
   a dominant position and plays an important role in the social life of the daughter-in-law. This
   is one area where very few studies have been done." (Also, in a survey it reviews, 22% of women
   workers reported unsatisfactory relationships with in-laws — read but not used in the body.)
   Old, and cited *as* a research-gap statement rather than as current data.
4. **malaysia.news.yahoo.com** (Malay Mail, 31 Oct 2025) — "Thalai Deepavali explained".
   Verified: in Tamil culture the newly-wed bride returns to her *own* family home with her
   husband for their first Deepavali; couple arrives on the eve, oil bath, temple, feast for the
   son-in-law; *seeru* of new clothes, sweets, jewellery, often a gold ring for the groom.
   The Daily Express Malaysia reprint of the same piece **403s** and was therefore not cited;
   malaymail.com's own URL also 403s, so the syndicated copy that actually loads is cited.
5. **lokmattimes.com** (21 Oct 2025) — "Diwali Padwa: why husband gives gifts to wife".
   Verified: Diwali Padwa / Balipratipada "celebrates the bond of husband and wife"; the wife
   performs the ritual and the husband gives a gift; the Vamana–Bali story explains the day.

## Tone rules applied

Customs are described, not endorsed: the in-laws' house is called a "default worth naming rather
than a rule worth defending"; the post says in as many words that nobody owes their in-laws a
flawless performance; and it does not assume the reader is a bride — one H2 addresses the husband
directly. One Hindi line is included with transliteration and gloss; **one further Hindi line was
drafted and dropped** because I could not vouch for its idiomatic register (a Marathi Padwa line
was also considered and dropped for the same reason).
