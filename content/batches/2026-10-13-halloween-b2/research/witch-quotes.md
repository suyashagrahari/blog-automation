## Phase 2 — gap analysis
- **Gap:** none quote the classics exactly with act/chapter; none separate book from film; none give any history numbers. None mention Salem death toll or the European scale; everydaypower asserts persecution without a figure.
- **Stale/wrong data:** the "eight million burned" myth persists in witch content generally; Leeson & Russ 2018 (≥80,000 tried, ~half executed) supersedes it.
- **Fan-out sub-queries → H2/FAQ:** where do famous witch quotes come from; exact Macbeth wording; Oz book vs film; short witchy captions; funny/cosy/dark lines; witch-friend (coven) lines; Salem deaths; European witch-hunt toll; why broomsticks/hats; kid-safe witch quotes; can I use film quotes.
- **Angle:** wins by being the only witch-quotes page that verifies its classics against the public-domain text (and flags the "my pretty" film line), pairs 60 credit-free originals with a sourced Salem/Europe history that corrects the "eight million" myth, and sizes lines for a phone using the 48.3% phone-open rate.

## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched; what each verifiably says)
1. **famous-trials.com/salem/2070-asal-dea** (Douglas O. Linder, UMKC School of Law; undated): 19 hanged on Gallows Hill 10 June–22 Sept 1692 (named, by date); Giles Corey pressed to death 19 Sept for failing to plead; Osborn, Toothaker, Dustin, Foster died in prison; "as many as thirteen others may have"; sources conflict.
2. **salem.lib.virginia.edu/people?group.num=all** (UVA Salem Witch Trials Documentary Archive; undated): Bridget Bishop "was the first person to be executed during the Salem witchcraft trials".
3. **peterleeson.com/Witch_Trials.pdf** — Leeson & Russ, *Economic Journal* 128 (Aug 2018) 2066–2105, Wiley/RES; peer-reviewed; full text read (author-hosted). "No fewer than 80,000 people had been tried for witchcraft, half of them executed"; "the precise number … is unknown"; data on 43,000+ tried across 21 countries.
4. **europepmc.org PMC10271903** — Kwaschik, "We Witches." *NTM* 31(2) 171–199, 24 May 2023, Springer, CC BY 4.0; peer-reviewed open access; full text read. 1970s feminists reappropriated the witch as symbol of alterity, revolt or victimhood; quotes Firestone 1970 "eight million women were burned at the stake"; W.I.T.C.H.'s first zap action "Up Against the Wall Street" on Halloween 1968.
5. **theconversation.com** — Mari Ellis Dunning (PhD candidate, Aberystwyth), 13 Oct 2025, DOI 10.64628/AB.ny5pqy7ym: first image of women on broomsticks in Le Franc's *Le Champion des Dames* (1485); *Malleus Maleficarum* (1486, Heinrich Kramer) alludes to flying on anointed broomsticks.
6. **gutenberg.org/ebooks/55** (exempt) — Baum 1900. Quotes verified: ch. 2 (lines "But I am a good witch, and the people love me." / "But I thought all witches were wicked," / "Oh, no, that is a great mistake."), ch. 12 melt line, ch. 23 Glinda Silver Shoes line.
Also verified, cited by eBook number without link: Macbeth #1533 (1.1 "When shall we three meet again? / In thunder, lightning, or in rain?"; 4.1 "Double, double, toil and trouble; / Fire, burn; and cauldron, bubble." and "Eye of newt, and toe of frog, / Wool of bat, and tongue of dog,"), Mosses from an Old Manse #512 ("Had Goodman Brown fallen asleep … witch-meeting?"), Grimms' Fairy Tales #2591 (Taylor & Edwardes; "Witches have red eyes …", "Nibble, nibble, gnaw, / Who is nibbling at my little house?").

Rejected: Gershman 2022 PLOS ONE (modern beliefs, no European estimate); White et al. 2025 Ambio (says only "thousands"); Oster 2004 JEP (403 on fetch); The Conversation 2021 Webster "Halloween hag" (second Conversation piece, not needed); nps.gov Salem URLs (404), mass.gov / smithsonianmag (403).
