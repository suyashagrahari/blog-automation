## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes.** A large body of quotable lines; sub-categories a reader can jump between; some coverage of the "make her cry" intent; short, sendable phrasing.

**The gap — and it is the whole post.** Not one page explains *why* any message works. Every one is a flat inventory. Consequences none of them address:

- **The superlative problem.** The genre's default move is scale ("more than all the stars in the sky", "more than all the grains of sand"). Scale is generic, so it reads as copied, because it was. Nobody says this out loud.
- **No mechanism, so no transfer.** A reader copies a line and learns nothing. There is no way to write the next one.
- **No fill-in requirement.** Every page hands over finished lines. None demands the one thing that makes a message land — a detail only the sender has.
- **No failure modes.** Zero pages name what backfires (an ex, pressure, length past a phone screen).
- **What happens after sending.** Universally unaddressed. A message in a WhatsApp thread is buried within a day and never re-read. This is where first-party data does work nobody else can.
- **Stale data:** not applicable — there is no data on any of these pages to supersede. That is itself the finding.

**Fan-out sub-queries → H2s:** what makes a love message land / how to say it without sounding cheesy / messages to make her cry / how long should it be / what not to send / where should I send it / when is a message the wrong move.

**Angle.** Wins by being the only post that organises 35 messages by the five mechanisms that make one land, gives every line a blank the reader must fill, names the three categories that backfire, and uses SubhSandesh's 12.3 views per created page and median 6.9 hours of editing (n=975) to show what happens to a message after the scroll buries it.

**Stated opinion, plainly:** superlative messages fail and specific ones work. "More than all the stars in the sky" is worse than naming the thing she did on a Tuesday.
