## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present across the readable set, so the post must cover them): personalised digital letter/page, subscriptions, online experiences/classes, e-gift cards, custom digital creations (art, playlist, scrapbook), a long-distance framing, a free option.

**The gap:**

1. Nobody distinguishes a digital gift that is *a file she has to download and manage* from one that is *an experience she opens*. "Digital gift" means a downloadable PDF to a retailer and something else entirely to the person searching.
2. Nobody prices anything. Not one rupee figure across the readable results, in a market where the query is Indian.
3. Nobody states the recipient's workload — download, unzip, redeem, accept an invite, hand over a login.
4. Nobody names the failure modes: the gift card that reads as "I forgot", the free builder whose link expires in 24 hours, the free trial that later bills *her* card, the 40 MB PDF that will not open in a chat app.
5. Nobody publishes an honest ranking, and nobody will say which digital gifts read as low effort — because most of these pages are selling one of them.

**Stale data:** none to supersede. There is no data on these pages at all, which is the more interesting finding.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** how much to spend; whether free is genuinely free; whether a digital gift is enough for a birthday; whether she will open a link at all.

**Rejected and why:**

- Waldfogel, *Deadweight Loss of Christmas* (1993) — only a slide deck was openly readable, the data is a 1992 Yale student survey, and cash-like gifts score *well* on his yield measure, which cuts against the honest reading.
- Steffel, Williams & LeBoeuf gift-card working paper on Wharton's server — the PDF is stamped "Draft, please do not circulate" and no published version resolved in Crossref.
- Gino & Flynn (2011), the cleanest "recipients prefer what they asked for" result — dropped on reputational grounds given the 2023 data-fabrication findings against the first author. Ward & Broniarczyk covers the same ground.
- Galak, Givi & Williams (2016), *Current Directions* — SAGE returns 403 and no open copy resolved.
- *Behavioral Sciences* 13(5):396 (n=223, experiential purchases → greater commitment → more happiness) — verified via Europe PMC full text, but nothing in it earned a sentence once the two full-text sources were in, so it was cut rather than listed as an uncited source.
