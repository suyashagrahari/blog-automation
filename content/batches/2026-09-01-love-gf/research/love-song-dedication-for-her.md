## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes (all five cover):** named songs with artist and year; a mix of eras;
a one-line description of what each song is about; some framing of when to send it.

**The gap — four of them, and none of the five pages touch any:**

1. **Nobody serves an Indian listener.** Every ranking page is a Western catalogue —
   John Legend, Jason Aldean, Chris Lane, Miley Cyrus, The Beach Boys. The India-geo
   autocomplete for this cluster is full of `love song dedicated to gf hindi`,
   `…bollywood`, `…telugu`. This is the widest gap on the SERP and the cheapest to
   close honestly.
2. **Nobody says how to pick.** All five hand over 41–80 options and no selection
   rule, which leaves the reader exactly where they started.
3. **Nobody covers delivery.** Not one page explains what happens when the link
   reaches her phone. Autoplay is blocked by default; a Spotify link hands her to the
   app and loses the message; an MP3 is a download step on mobile data.
4. **Nobody names the overused songs.** Every page opens with "All of Me" or
   "Perfect", which is the reason those songs no longer signal anything.

**Stale data:** not applicable — none of the five pages cites any data at all, stale
or current. That absence is itself the opening.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** what to write alongside the song; whether
a song works as an apology; what to do if her taste is in another language.

## Phase 6 — audit summary

Originally 48 of 50. Both recorded failures were re-triaged in the remediation pass
(2026-09-01) and both are now closed. `batchMeta.auditReport.failed` is empty.

### Swap test — reclassified as `passed`, not fixed by re-sourcing

The original `why` read the swap test as "could this source conceivably belong in
another post in this batch". Four pieces of evidence say that is the wrong reading:

1. `research-sources.md` defines the target of the test as *filler* — "a source that
   fits any article and is *about* none of them", with a wireless-subscriber count as
   the worked example. Chrome's autoplay policy is *about* the exact mechanism this
   post's delivery section turns on.
2. The subject test in the same file names "the platform behaviour" as a legitimate
   subject. A browser vendor's own policy page is the primary source for it.
3. The cap rule explicitly permits a URL in **2** posts of a batch. A literal swap
   test would forbid what the cap rule allows, so the two rules are only coherent if
   the swap test targets filler rather than legitimate overlap.
4. Under a literal reading, Harwood & Wallace (shared musical activity → commitment)
   would also fail against `how-can-i-surprise-my-girlfriend-online`. A test no post
   can pass is not a test.

Checked against the batch rather than assumed: neither `developer.chrome.com` nor
`webkit.org` appears in any of the nine sibling files. `cute-website-for-gf` does
discuss autoplay (7 mentions) but states it unsourced and generically ("Mobile Safari
and Chrome block audio that starts without a tap"), so there is no duplication signal
at batch level either.

Both citations were also re-anchored to the *audio-specific* half of each policy, so
they are not interchangeable with a general "browsers block autoplay" claim: the
desktop-only Media Engagement Index exemption, and iOS 10's relaxation covering
silent `<video>` only — which is precisely why a song behaves differently from a
background video. The body link text now reads "Chrome has blocked audio autoplay
since Chrome 66", matching the page's own "launched in Chrome 66 for audio and video
elements", and drops the earlier "unless" phrasing, which implied prior interaction
was the only exemption when the page lists three.

### Every H2 answerable standalone — fixed in the body

The 30-song H2 previously opened with a format note ("Each entry names the year and
the singer…"), so a lifted snippet said nothing. It now opens with a genuine

the six jobs named in order — which is what the checklist item asks for. The list
supplies depth below it; the H3s stay independently extractable and the `ItemList`
