## Phase 2 — Gap analysis
- **Table stakes:** lists of English alternatives, mostly puns (Quillbot, Romper, SwifDoo); one-language translation pages (Italian, Japanese).
- **Gap:** no page groups alternatives by register (none has a formal or professional set, regional English or sign-offs); no page puts several languages together with pronunciation and dictionary checks; no page says what people wrote before "Happy Halloween", or dates the switch.
- **Stale or absent data:** the competitors cite no data at all. Romper and WordStream are from 2022.
- **Fan-out sub-queries:** what else to say; professional/client wording; X language versions (Spanish, Irish, Japanese, Hindi); Hallowe'en vs Halloween; regional differences; card sign-offs; whether to wish non-celebrants.
- **Angle:** wins by being the only post that sorts original alternatives by tone alongside dictionary-checked phrasings in 11 languages and dated print evidence (a 1912 invitation guide, the 1947 Ngram crossover), framed by SubhSandesh's 48.3% phone-open rate.

## Phase 3 — Sources (all fetched)
1. **Bădescu & Dincă 2023**, *Quaestiones Romanicae* X, doi:10.35923/QR.10.01.17, PDF at ciccre.uvt.ro. Peer-reviewed and open access. Based on 150 questionnaires from Faculty of Letters students at the University of Craiova, it finds young people replacing standardised greetings with familiar, votive (wish) or borrowed formulas (Hello, Hi, Ciao, Hola, Bye), personalised by situation. Full text read in Romanian, abstract in English. This is the linguistics source (greetings and formulaic language).

## Notes on brief and prompt claims
- "Oíche Shamhna shona — verify": **partly verified.** Oíche Shamhna = Hallowe'en (Ó Dónaill, focloir.ie); the lenited "shona" follows the dictionary's "Nollaig shona" pattern, but no dictionary checked lists the full Halloween phrase. The body and FAQ say so.
- "हैप्पी हैलोवीन is just a transliteration": confirmed. The natural alternative uses Wiktionary's हैलोवीन की शुभकामना, given in its plural greeting form शुभकामनाएँ (the plural is my inflection).
- Korean: the prompt did not mention the standard spelling. 핼러윈 is the dictionary standard and 할로윈 is the common spelling, which the body notes.
- The Korean 즐거운 … 보내세요, Tagalog "Maligayang Halloween" and German "Fröhliches Halloween" are built on attested patterns or grammar (gender from Duden). They are not dictionary-listed as whole phrases, and the body's note says where only the parts are listed.
- Keyword: the literal planned string is ungrammatical, so it appears verbatim only in the slug. This is recorded in `failed` as deliberate.
