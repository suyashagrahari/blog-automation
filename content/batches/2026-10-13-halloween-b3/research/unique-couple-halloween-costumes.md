## Phase 2: gap analysis
- **Table stakes:** many pairs, mix of funny/cute/DIY, some cost or effort guidance.
- **Gap:** no page groups pairs by the *relationship mechanic* between the two costumes; no per-pair recognisability score; build hours appear only on partyandbeyond's 10-row table; nobody gives a method for turning a shared memory into a costume; no page cites any research on shared humour in couples.
- **Stale data:** inquirer 2021; partyandbeyond cites NRF without a figure.
- **Fan-out sub-queries:** what makes a couple costume unique; creative pairs by type; which are recognisable; cost and time; inside-joke costumes; partner who hates dressing up; same-sex / friends; sending the photo; when is Halloween.
- **Angle:** wins by being the only post that groups couple costumes by the mechanic linking the two halves, scores each for recognisability, cost and hours, and gives a research-backed 3-step memory-to-costume method, using SubhSandesh's 48.3% phone-open share for the thumbnail test.

Competitor pairs deliberately avoided: ceiling fan & biggest fan, Error 404 & loading, deviled/angel egg, Netflix & chill, ketchup & mustard, salt & pepper, tourist & map pin, moth & flame, butterfly & catcher, Jack & Jill, Bob Ross & painting, milk & cookies, Father Time & Mother Nature, sunflower & farmer, artist & canvas, sun & moon, PB&J, Instagram filter, dentist & tooth fairy, golfer & course, piñata, Medusa & statue, eclipse.

## Phase 3: sources (all fetched)
1. Kurtz & Algoe 2015, *Personal Relationships*, PMC4779443 (NIH author manuscript, full text). 77 couples enrolled, 71 coded; simultaneous laughter during a "how we met" conversation was associated with closeness and support; unshared laughter with less closeness. Correlational. Link: europepmc.org.
2. Bazzini, Stack, Martincin & Davis 2007, *Motivation and Emotion* 31(1):25–34. Full text read from the author PDF on figshare (API record 30804284; OpenAlex OA host libres.uncg.edu timed out). 52 couples, 4 conditions, 5-minute recall; shared-laughter reminiscence raised satisfaction vs 3 controls; not explained by mood.
3. Borelli et al. 2024, *Family Process* 63(2):667–690, PMC11245364, CC BY, full text. Relational savoring memory-selection step: recall 2–3 recent positive memories with the partner and pick the most specific, positive and connected one, screening out "spoiling" content.
4. Kurtz 2014 MA thesis, UNC Chapel Hill (cdr.lib.unc.edu). **Abstract only.** Shared vs solo laughter linked to positive emotion and perceived similarity across online and lab studies.
