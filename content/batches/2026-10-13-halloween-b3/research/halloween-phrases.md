## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched and read)
1. **Lázaro Ibarrola & Hidalgo Gordo (2015)**, Porta Linguarum 23: 25–40, Univ. de Granada repository digibug.ugr.es/handle/10481/53761 (doi 10.30827/Digibug.53761). Full PDF read: 57 Spanish secondary EFL students (33 + 24), 10 related (city) + 10 unrelated words; unrelated retained better; beginner group 10 vs 5 full scores; no delayed post-test, authors call results preliminary.
2. **Ishii (2013)**, Vocabulary Learning and Instruction 2(1), castledown.com, doi 10.7820/vli.v02.1.ishii, open access CC BY-NC-ND (cited, nothing reproduced). Full PDF read: 64 Japanese university students, nonwords paired with meanings in unrelated / semantic / physically related sets of 6; physical sets significantly worse on both tests; within-set confusions 1 / 13 / 36; reports Tinkham 1997 thematic clustering facilitative. Note: the PDF's XMP metadata names "Taylor & Francis" (a typesetting-template artefact); the journal is published by Castledown, so the T&F ban was judged not to apply. Flag if the orchestrator disagrees.
3. **Wiktionary "jack-o'-lantern"** (exempt) plus sister entries read the same session: Halloween, banshee (1771), wraith (1513), poltergeist, ghoul, goblin, zombie, hag, grimalkin, spook, gourd, scarecrow, guising, souling, haunt, eerie (Scots sense "frightened"), creepy, eldritch, macabre, will o' the wisp (figurative sense), scared stiff (1890s), graveyard shift, skeleton in the closet (US) / cupboard (UK), candy corn.
