## Phase 2: gap analysis

- **Table stakes:** short wishes, quotes, lines for friends and family, a "Happy Navratri" sign-off, a mention of Durga and good over evil.
- **The gap:** no page writes for a recipient who does not know or observe the festival, such as colleagues, non-Hindu or international friends, or the non-Indian partners and children of NRI family. None tells the sender what to avoid (assumed fasting, devotion in a work channel, a wrong festival name). None gives a one-line explainer.
- **Stale data:** every date on the fetched pages is from 2020, 2021 or 2024. The 2026 calendar quirk (Ashtami and Navami both on 19 Oct, New Delhi) appears nowhere, and 2027 dates appear nowhere.
- **Unanswered questions:** Do all Hindus fast? Navratri or Durga Puja for Bengali friends? Is Dussehra the same thing? How should a non-Hindu reply? Is a wish late after night one?
- **Fan-out sub-queries → H2/FAQ:** what is Navratri (H2), wishes by recipient (H2 + 6 H3), when to send (H2 + table), mistakes to avoid (H2), the 2027 dates (FAQ), the Ashtami/Navami overlap (FAQ), how to reply (FAQ), whether a group invite works (FAQ).

**Angle:** wins by being the only post that writes English Navratri wishes for a mixed audience, with a pasteable explainer and a named mistake per group, and with send-timing grounded in SubhSandesh's Raksha Bandhan lead-time data (49.4% on the day, n=89) and its 48.3% phone-open rate.
