## Why this keyword despite the volume

This is the only keyword in the batch that describes SubhSandesh's product rather
than a wish, a date or a message. The searcher's job is not finished by information
— it is finished by a product like this existing. Inverting the Gate 5 arithmetic:
~150 searches at a ~15% intent match beats 10,000 date-lookups at 0.1%.

Recorded plainly so nobody later reads this as a traffic play: **this page is
chosen for intent, not reach.** It should be judged on pages created, not sessions.

It is also **largely evergreen**. A sibling looking for a personalised page is
searching year-round, so the post is deliberately not year-stamped. Rakhi 2027 =
**Tuesday 17 August 2027** (day-of-week verified by computation, not by a panchang —
`drikpanchang.com` is banned for this batch). Rakhi 2026 = Friday 28 August 2026,
three days after this brief, and unreachable.

## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present on ≥3 of the five, so the post must cover them):
what "personalised rakhi website" means; a photo/name/message field list; a
step-by-step build; a private shareable link; brother *and* sister direction;
"no coding required"; works on a phone.

**The gap — three of them, and the first is the whole angle:**

1. **Nobody separates the artifact from the file.** Canva and the card sites give
   you something to *download*; the retailers give you something to *post*; a
   website is something you *send and they open*. The three have different failure
   modes and different lifespans, and the entire SERP treats them as one bucket.
2. **Nobody discusses privacy of the page as a decision.** The pages either assert
   "it's private" (rayxis) or ignore it (everyone else). None reports how often
   people actually choose to lock one, because none has the data.
3. **Nobody names the inconvenient research.** Every page assumes "more digital
   contact = closer siblings". The peer-reviewed finding is messier and it belongs
   on a page that wants to be trusted.

**Stale data:** the ecommerce pages carry no dated research at all; rayxis carries
no numbers at all; buildnectic's only figures are its own price and review count.
There is nothing to supersede — the whole SERP is data-free, which is unusual and
is the single biggest reason a fact-dense page has a shot here.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped:** how long the page stays up; what
happens to the photos; whether a page is better than a parcel; what to do when the
sibling has a slow connection; whether a brother-to-sister page is a different
thing from a sister-to-brother page.

**Fan-out sub-queries** (each becomes an H2 or an FAQ):
what is a personalised raksha bandhan website · is it different from an e-card ·
is it free · what do I put on it · can I password-protect it · how do I send it ·
does it work on my brother's phone · can my sister reopen it next year · brother
vs sister page · how long does it take to make · is a website better than a gift ·
what if they don't open links · Rakhi 2027 date.

**Angle (→ `batchMeta.angle`):** wins by being the only page that separates a card
you download from a page you send, and prices that distinction with usage data
from 2,973 personalised pages built by 2,466 creators — including the 44.2% who
lock the page before sharing it.

**Four sources, all fetched and verified.**

1. **Corder, Hall, Pennington & Holmstrom (2026), "Adult sibling relationships and
   media multiplexity theory", *Journal of Social and Personal Relationships*
   43(1):181–200.** Verified via the publicly readable Ovid abstract
   (`journals.sagepub.com` returns HTTP 403 to non-browser agents). **Paywalled —
   abstract only, full text not read.** From the abstract: 29% of participants
   listed at least one sibling among their most important relationship partners;
   siblings were 2.9% of the six closest friendships named in study 2; emotional
   closeness was positively associated with phone-call and email frequency and
   **negatively** associated with the frequency of person-to-person media sharing;
   the **number** of ICTs used was negatively associated with closeness in study 1
   while overall **frequency** of ICT use was positively associated with it.
   → Subject test: pass (adult siblings, mediated communication). Swap test: this
   could also sit in another sibling-communication post in this batch — recorded as
   an audit failure rather than claimed as clean.

2. **Acuti et al., "You designed that yourself for me? Vicarious pride in
   customized gift exchange" — University of Bath, reported 27 Dec 2024.** Four
   experiments. Study 1: 74 participants; recipients of personalised gifts changed
   fewer items of the gift, read as greater appreciation. Study 2: 134 participants
   shown a customisation video versus a website-browsing video; appreciation was
   higher for the customised gift **regardless of the time and effort the giver
   spent**. Studies 3–4 (mug, wristwatch) confirmed customisation raised
   appreciation and recipient self-esteem.
   → Subject test: pass (gift personalisation, recipient side). Swap test: pass —
   *personalisation itself* is the subject of this keyword and no other in the batch.
   Load-bearing: it is the only evidence on the page that the personalising, not the
   polish, is what lands.

3. **Ban, Qiu, Cai & Wu (2025), "Dual effects of family ritual communication on
   Chinese adolescents' well-being", *Frontiers in Psychology* 16, 7 April 2025.**
   Open access, peer-reviewed. Family ritual communication raised well-being with
   family intimacy the largest mediating path at **0.203** and resilience at
   **0.155**; cultural identity was a **negative** mediator at −0.040 — i.e. ritual
   is not uniformly good for you, which is worth reporting honestly.
   → Subject test: pass (family ritual). Swap test: fails — a family-ritual paper
   fits any festival post in this batch. Recorded as an audit failure.

4. **Aharony-Cohen / Toch et al. (2020), "Selectivity in posting on social networks:
   the role of privacy concerns, social capital, and technical literacy",
   *Heliyon* 6(2):e03298, via PubMed Central.** Open access, peer-reviewed.
   **11,141 posts from 181 participants** over three months: ~50% of posts were
   friends-only, **12% public**, 25% of posts were shared more narrowly than
   friends-only, and only **~30% of participants** ever used the fine-grained
   controls. Selective sharing was associated with *higher* bonding social capital;
   self-censorship with lower.
   → Subject test: pass (platform sharing behaviour). Swap test: pass — it is tied
   specifically to the password-protect decision this post argues about.

## First-party data — and its honest limit

Assigned primary fact: **2,973 personalised pages across 15 page types, by 2,466
registered creators** (measured 2026-08-25). Supporting: 44.2% password-protect
before sharing (1,315 of 2,973); 99.4% of started pages get published and shared
(2,956 of 2,973); 12.2 average views per created page.

**The limit, stated on the page itself:** `rakshabandhanpages` holds **3
documents** and the rakhi templates account for **39 of 36,202 recorded views
(0.1%)**. No rate, percentage or "most creators" claim is computed from n=3. Every
number in the post is platform-wide.

This is the one post in the batch where that is defensible rather than a dodge:
the keyword *is* "a personalised website", so how 2,466 people behave when building
personalised websites is on-topic by construction. It is still not a rakhi insight,
and the audit says so. Re-query when rakhi pages clear ~500.
