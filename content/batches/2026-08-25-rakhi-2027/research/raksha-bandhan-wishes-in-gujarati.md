## Phase 2 — Gap analysis

**Table stakes (all or most pages carry them).** Real Gujarati-script wishes; a
short/one-line set; a shayari set; wishes split by brother vs sister; an Instagram
or WhatsApp status set; some explanation of what રક્ષાબંધન means.

**The gap — nobody in the set does any of this.**

1. **No page pairs script with Roman transliteration.** A reader who speaks Gujarati
   but cannot read the script — the second-generation cousin, the NRI nephew, the
   Hindi-schooled Gujarati — gets nothing usable from any of the nine results. This
   is a documented population, not a hunch (Sarma 2025, n > 1,000 Indian university
   entrants, finds shifting L1 dominance and an oracy/literacy split).
2. **No page explains તું vs તમે.** Gujarati marks respect in the second person.
   Sending તું to a bhabhi or an elder cousin reads as rude. Not one of the nine
   mentions it, and every one of them mixes both pronouns inside the same list.
3. **No page addresses rendering.** Gujarati script silently fails to empty boxes on
   older Android builds and in some app webviews. The pages hand you text to copy and
   say nothing about whether it will arrive intact.
4. **No page states what happens after you send.** No open rates, no device split, no
   view counts — nothing about the actual delivery of the message.
5. **No page carries a 2027 date.** Rakhi 2027 is Tuesday 17 August 2027.
6. **No structured data of any kind.** Zero FAQ schema, zero ItemList across all nine.

**Stale data.** There is no cited statistic anywhere in the set to supersede — the
staleness is in the pages themselves (2021, 2024, "2025") rather than in their
numbers, because they have no numbers.

**Unanswered questions raised and dropped.** Which wish suits which relative;
whether "હેપી રક્ષાબંધન" is acceptable or lazy; how to type Gujarati on a phone at
all; what to do when the recipient cannot read the script.
