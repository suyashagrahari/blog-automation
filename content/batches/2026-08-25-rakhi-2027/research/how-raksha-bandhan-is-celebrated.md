## Phase 2 — Gap analysis and angle

**Assigned angle** (`batchMeta.angle`, verbatim intent): win by treating Raksha
Bandhan as a **practice** rather than a **meaning** — the six-step order of the day
and the thali item by item, then five regional observances of the same Shravana
full moon set side by side in one table, closing on the remote layer with the
44.2% password-protect rate.

**The gap the post names:** almost every ranking page describes **one** regional
practice as if it were the whole country's. North Indian observance is presented as
the festival; Maharashtrian Narali Poornima, the South Indian Upakarma / Avani
Avittam sequencing, Odisha's Gamha Purnima and Bengal's Jhulan Purnima either
disappear or appear as decorative colour at the bottom of the page.

**Table stakes** (inferred from the post's own framing, since the teardown is lost):
the Shravana Purnima date; sister ties, brother gives; the thali; a muhurat time.

**1 — Hindu American Foundation.** The page carries the sequence the post
attributes to it, near-verbatim: *"sisters customarily perform arati by lighting a
small oil lamp and offering it to the family deity. Praying for the well-being of
the brother, she then applies tilak … on his forehead. After tying the rakhi on his
wrist, she traditionally places a sweet in his mouth. The brother then completes the
custom by reciprocating in the ritual feeding and giving his sister a gift."* Byline
Syama Allard, dated July 31 2020. **Note the limit:** it says *wrist*, not *right*
wrist — the post correctly sources "right" elsewhere (see below), not here.

**2 — Anderson.** The quoted sentence is served in the page's `meta name="description"`
and in the embedded `abstracts` JSON: *"Janai Purni, on the full moon day of August,
is a time crammed with festivities that begin the preceding evening, continuing all
through the day and lasting far…"*. **Depth of verification is unchanged from what
the audit already admits — this is the publisher's ~150-character chapter
description, not the chapter.** New finding: the same page's metadata carries
`firstPublishedYear: 1971` and `© George Allen & Unwin Ltd, 1971`, with
`plannedPublicationDate: 2024-10-30`. The post had recorded it as *"Routledge, 2024"*.
A 1971 ethnography of Nepal presented as a 2024 work overstates its currency.
**Corrected in the JSON** (body Sources line, `batchMeta.sources[1].publishedDate`
and `stat`, and the `citation` block).

**3 — Berti.** Quote served in `meta name="citation_abstract"`: *"This chapter
discusses a case which illustrates how the passage from religious festival to
cultural programme is the result of a historically complex process involving
different actors and mediations at different levels of society."* Same finding:
`firstPublishedYear: 2011`, `© 2011 Christiane Brosius and Karin M. Polit`,
`citation_inbook_title: Ritual, Heritage and Identity`, pp. **126–148**, ebook
2020-11-29, `citation_publisher: Routledge India`. The post had recorded *"Routledge,
2020"*. **Corrected in the JSON** the same way. Abstract-only status unchanged.

**4 — IJRTP (Sanchita Paul).** Both quoted fragments are present verbatim on the
landing page abstract: *"the bathing tradition for ritual ablution … has not
faded"*, and mass congregation *"during a particular astrological conjunction for a
holy dip in the river or sacred water bodies"*. Author affiliation Assam
University Silchar, ORCID present, licensed CC BY-NC-ND. This is the post's
peer-reviewed open-access source and it holds. The audit's note that the **full PDF
returned HTTP 403** was not retested against the PDF endpoint in this pass; the
landing-page abstract carries the cited text, so the claim stands at abstract depth.
