## 3. Phase 2 — gap analysis

**Table stakes** (present across the festival-explainer results, so the post must not read
as ignorant of them): what Raksha Bandhan is, that it falls in Shravana, that the sister
ties the rakhi and the brother reciprocates, that 2027's date matters to anyone planning.
Handled in one paragraph, not a history section — `raksha-bandhan-history-in-hindi` and
`why-sister-ties-rakhi-to-brother` own that ground in this batch.

**The gap.** Nobody compares the forms. The explainer pages do not mention sending
anything; the tool pages each argue for their own form. There is no page anywhere in the
set that puts a typed line, a forwarded image, an e-card, a built page, a voice note and a
video call on one table and prices them.

**Stale data.** Not applicable — the ranking pages cite no data at all. That is itself the
opening: a comparison with any real numbers in it is unopposed on this SERP.

**Unanswered questions** raised and dropped by the ranking set: how people actually send
wishes now; whether a forwarded image is rude; whether an elaborate page is too much for a
sibling you are not close to.

**Fan-out sub-queries** → became H2s or FAQs: *what counts as online wishes · which is
better, an image or a link · is a forwarded rakhi message rude · how long does each take ·
what do I send to an older relative · is a voice note better than typing · do I need a
photo · what if we are not close.*

**Angle** (recorded as `batchMeta.angle`): wins by being the only post that compares the
six forms a Raksha Bandhan wish can take and tells the reader which to pick, grounded in
2,973 pages across 15 page types by 2,466 creators and 12.2 average opens per shared page.

### Sources cited — 3, each fetched and read on 2026-08-26

1. **Savelyeva, T. V. (2025). "The transformation of sociocultural celebration practices:
   the electronic greeting card as a polycode text." *Neophilology*, 11(3), 705–720.**
   `https://neophilology.elpub.ru/jour/article/view/531` · published 2025-10-16
   Verified from the journal's own English abstract: **110 e-cards** selected by continuous
   sampling from popular open-access sites, plus **49 e-cards sent to the author by
   respondents on 12 June 2023**, plus respondent interviews. Headline finding used in the
   post: **"the dominance of the visual component over the verbal"**.
   *Why it is not filler:* it is about the ready-made e-card **as a form** — precisely the
   option this post has to price against a built page. No sibling post cites it.

2. **Melnichuk, O. A. & Alekseeva, V. F. (2025). "Axiology of Motivational E-Card Genre:
   Concept of Comfort in Russian and Yakut Linguocultures." *Nauchnyi dialog*, 14(6),
   73–91.** `https://www.nauka-dialog.ru/jour/article/view/6458` · published 2025-08-19
   Verified from the English abstract: **1,788 Russian and 800 Yakut** motivational e-cards
   analysed, sampled from **WhatsApp and Pinterest between November 2023 and November
   2024**; the genre has **"gained popularity among the older demographic."**
   *Why it is not filler:* it supplies the recipient axis of the form decision — the
   forwarded card-image is a live genre with an identifiable audience, which is the
   evidence behind this post's advice to send the image to the older relative and the page
   to the sibling.

3. **Janney, H. (2021). "Life Skills in a Minute: Sending a Card." *EDIS*, UF/IFAS
   Extension, 4H416.** `https://journals.flvc.org/edis/article/view/128378` ·
   published 2021-08-05
   Verified from the abstract: a 3-page guidance publication that describes receiving a
   card as a sequence — "pulling it out of the mailbox, reading the outside of the
   envelope, breaking the seal, pulling out the card, reading the note from the sender,
   and then placing it somewhere special."
   *Why it is not filler:* it is the only source that names what the physical form does
   that no online form reproduces, which is the concession this post has to make honestly.

**Publication dates were confirmed against `api.crossref.org`**, not read off the page.
