## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched 2026-10-13)
1. Dawel, Krumhuber & Palermo, *Affective Science* (Springer), 2025-07-24, https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13087068. Full text via the Europe PMC REST API, CC BY. Table 2 gives the mean share of posed stimuli rated fake: happy 7.1%, surprise 44.5%, fear 59.8%, anger 67.7%, disgust 72.1%. The paper also says Duchenne smiles get more positive, affiliative responses, at least in White Western cultures.
2. Witkower, Tian, Tracy & Rule, *PNAS Nexus* (OUP), 2024-08-22, https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11378078. Full text, CC BY-NC-ND, so cited and not reproduced. Study 1 photographed 303 people with the camera at eye level; Study 2 used 987 observers. It states that Duchenne smiles can be posed deliberately and cites a meta-analysis finding them more attractive, authentic and trustworthy even when posed.
3. Knight & Preston, *PLOS ONE*, 2023-10-11, https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10566732. Full text, CC BY. Four experiments with N = 272. Selfies taken at arm's length from above were judged slimmer, and the ratings were linked to disordered-eating thoughts. This is a megajournal, which the BRIEF de-prefers; it is kept because it was the only open study of viewing angle found.
4. Girard, Cohn, Yin & Morency, *Affective Science*, 2021-01-18, https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8317937. **Abstract only.** It covers 751 smiles from 136 participants and finds eye constriction carries more information about perceived than experienced positive emotion, and that context matters.

Dropped: Ward et al. 2018, "Nasal distortion in short-distance photographs: the selfie effect" (JAMA Facial Plastic Surgery, PMC5876805). It has no abstract, PMC showed a reCAPTCHA and JAMA returned 403. It was not cited unread.

## Findings against the brief / prompt
- The prompt's examples (back-to-back, walking away, the neck "bite", mirror selfies) collide with b2's poses. The categories are kept and the poses changed, as listed above.
- "Chin forward" advice has no study behind it. The body labels it photographers' practice.
- The prompt suggested a Duchenne paper on perceived warmth. The open papers found measure perceived genuineness, authenticity and trustworthiness, not "warmth" by that name, so the body says "authentic and trustworthy".
- The template spec was followed: partner mode, up to six photos, fog-wiped portraits, Boo License 1080×1920, and premium with 12 photos and a 60-second voice note. No Halloween usage claims are made.
