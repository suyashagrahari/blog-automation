## Phase 2 — gap
- **Table stakes:** named characters with glasses; Harry Potter, Velma, Clark Kent and Waldo appear on every page (all are spent in b3's no-mask post, so I excluded them here); a tip to avoid masks.
- **Gap:** no page sorts characters by the frame shape the reader already owns. None gives a cost, a recognisability score, or solo/couple/group. Nobody explains *why* glasses fog or how to fix it with a source. Nobody says what is safe near the eyes. Only Hakim mentions costume contacts, and it does so to sell its own lenses.
- **Fan-out sub-queries:** costumes for round / cat-eye / aviator glasses; couple costumes with glasses; glasses fog with a mask; face paint with glasses; are costume contacts legal or safe (US, UK); keeping kids' glasses on; cheapest glasses costume.
- **Angle:** wins by being the only post that sorts 26 glasses characters by frame shape, with recognisability, est. cost and party size, then sources the fog fix and the costume-lens prescription gap to a paper, a professional body and a regulator's warning.

## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched)
1. Agarwal P, Sharma D. *Indian J Surg* 2021 (epub 15 Jan 2021), letter. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7810190. Read in full via Europe PMC. It explains the fog mechanism and gives the fixes: pinch the mask edge, tape it, a tissue at the nose bridge, sit glasses forward, a diluted-soap film wiped off after 20 s. Limitation: it's about medical masks.
2. Berenson AB et al. *Adolesc Health Med Ther* 10:1–6 (Dove, CC BY-NC, cited not reproduced), epub 7 Feb 2019. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6369860. Read in full. n=392: 14% had worn cosmetic lenses; half got them locally without a prescription; 3.9% from an eye doctor; ~72% started before age 15; 15% had borrowed lenses.
3. American Academy of Ophthalmology, "Colored Contacts & Halloween Safety", https://www.aao.org/eye-health/glasses-contacts/colored-lenses (resolves to /eye-health/halloween-cosplay-colorful-contacts-eye-safety). It says selling contact lenses without a prescription is illegal in the US, risks permanent blindness, and says to get a prescription with brand, measurements and expiry. The page shows no publication date.
4. Optometry Today (AOP), O'Hare R., 26 Oct 2015, https://www.aop.org.uk/ot/news/2015/10/26/goc-warns-of-health-risk-of-halloween-cls. It reports the GOC's position that zero-powered lenses must be supplied under the supervision of a registered optometrist, dispensing optician or doctor.
