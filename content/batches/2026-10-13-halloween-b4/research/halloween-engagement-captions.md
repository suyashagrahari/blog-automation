## Phase 3 — sources (all fetched)
1. Papp, Danielewicz & Cayemberg (2012) "Are we Facebook official?" Cyberpsychol Behav Soc Netw — https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21988733 — **abstract only** (paywalled, Liebert). n=58 couples; men's partnered status and women's partner-in-profile-picture linked with higher satisfaction; status disagreements → lower satisfaction for women.
2. Toma & Choi (2015) Cyberpsychol Behav Soc Netw — https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26167834 — **abstract only**. Relationship status, dyadic photos, wall posts → commitment ↑ → still together after 6 months.
3. Lee, Gillath & Miller (2019) PLOS ONE, CC BY — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6398828 — full text via Europe PMC REST. Five studies (Study 1 n=189 students); deeper online disclosure → lower intimacy/satisfaction when audience inclusive; Study 5 partner/relationship-focused content dissipated the effect. (Megajournal — noted; no non-megajournal OA paper on relationship posting was found.)
4. Anna Post, "Inside Weddings: Celebrating Your Engagement" (Fall 2008) — https://emilypost.com/advice/inside-weddings-celebrating-your-engagement — announcement order; parties 1–3 months after engagement, invites 2–4 weeks ahead.
5. Emily Post Institute, "Congratulations—You're Engaged!" (undated) — https://emilypost.com/advice/congratulationsyoure-engaged — October among six most popular wedding months; never announce until a former union is dissolved.
6. NCSC, "Social Media: how to use it safely" (published 24 Jan 2019) — https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/social-media-how-to-use-it-safely — think what you post, who can see it, what detail is unnecessary but useful to criminals.

Rejected: Orosz et al. 2015 (Frontiers — banned publisher); NTU thesis "Include me in your Facebook" (not needed); Mäder 2023 ORE wedding-media ethnography (relevant but outbound cap 6). Avoided b2 couples-caption papers (PMC11351969, PMC12445260, PMC8322522, PMC8598037) and b4 couple-goals sources.
